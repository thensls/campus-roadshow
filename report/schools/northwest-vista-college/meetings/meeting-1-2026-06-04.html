Section 1 — School Snapshot

School: Northwest Vista College (NVC)

Location: San Antonio, TX

School Type: Public 2-Year — part of the Alamo Colleges District, alongside NLC, SPC, Palo Alto College, and San Antonio College. Each college operates as its own NSLS chapter within the district system.

Chapter Status: Established

Chapter Type: Standard (in-person with potential hybrid flexibility)

Advisors Present: Elizabeth "Bets" Murray (Senior Coordinator, Office of Student Life) and Miranda Aguilar (Co-Advisor, part-time). A third co-advisor, Jade, was not present but was referenced. Note: this was a multi-school session also attended by advisors from NLC, SPC, and Palo Alto College.

Notable Context: NVC's NSLS chapter currently sits within Student Life and is overseen by two part-time advisors (Bets and Miranda). The chapter is in active transition — institutional leadership has turned over significantly (new president ~3 years ago, new dean ~1 year, new director ~several months), and there is an ongoing, slow-moving effort to shift the NSLS program to the academic side. Bets and Miranda are proactively documenting processes and organizing materials to prepare for a potential handoff to new leadership or a restructured advising model. The campus culture is strongly oriented toward student-centered, student-led programming, which has prompted internal scrutiny of NSLS's induction structure and GPA eligibility requirements. The platform used for co-curricular tracking is called Alamo Experience, formerly Campus Labs (now Engagedly/Engkura Engage per Bets's clarification).

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • SNT peer-to-peer engagement: Miranda specifically called out Success Networking Teams as irreplaceable▶ 13:24 — noting that peer-to-peer accountability is more effective than advisor-led engagement, and that the vulnerable, supportive environment of SNTs is a core differentiator of the NSLS experience.
  • Student sense of belonging and accomplishment: Reflected more broadly in the session (from SPC's Dr. Roberts), but resonant across the Alamo group — the feeling that students have achieved something meaningful and that this is a "lifelong opportunity," not just a checkbox.
  • Advisor-recognized recent improvements: Miranda explicitly praised NSLS's recent structural changes — eliminating the pillars and reversing the induction fee model▶ 21:51 — crediting them with better incentivizing advisor engagement and student completion. She wants these gains protected and built upon.

What Isn't Working

  • SNT drop-off: Miranda directly confirmed the ~50% fall-off figure Chris cited, describing it as the primary engagement barrier▶ 41:16 — getting students to meet again after the initial gathering is a persistent, concrete problem at NVC.
  • Induction structure perceived as non-student-led: Bets flagged that NVC's new institutional leadership is questioning the NSLS induction process because students don't have a voice▶ 17:45 in how orientation or induction is designed or run. The current campus culture strongly prioritizes student-led programming, and NSLS's top-down structure is drawing scrutiny.
  • GPA and credit eligibility restrictions: Bets noted that campus leadership questions why NSLS shouldn't be open to all students, citing the GPA requirement▶ 17:45 as a barrier inconsistent with NVC's accessibility-first direction. Miranda echoed this, mentioning credit limits alongside GPA as access friction points▶ 21:51.
  • Part-time advisor bandwidth: Both advisors are part-time, managing documentation, transitions, and program delivery simultaneously. Time scarcity is a real constraint on their ability to support students deeply or take on new initiatives without significant platform support.
  • Co-curricular data not captured in Alamo Experience: Raised in the broader session but directly applicable to NVC — student participation in NSLS events is not automatically reflected in the Alamo Experience platform (the district's co-curricular transcript tool), creating a gap in how student engagement is documented institutionally.

School Priorities

  • Student-centered, student-led programming: Bets described this as the dominant cultural mandate from NVC's new president, dean, and director▶ 17:38. Programs need to demonstrate that students have agency and voice, not just participate in advisor-designed structures.
  • Accessibility and inclusivity: The push to remove or soften GPA and credit-hour requirements aligns with a broader institutional equity agenda — making high-impact practices available to a wider student population.
  • Institutional continuity during transition: With multiple leadership changes at the dean, president, and director levels, Bets and Miranda are actively working to document processes and prepare the program for a structural handoff▶ 11:03. Stability and clear documentation are near-term operational priorities.
  • Career readiness and workforce outcomes: Bets's immediate connection of Society's career features to the career experience team and career navigators signals that demonstrating career outcomes is an institutional priority — aligning with the broader Alamo Colleges district emphasis on student success metrics.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. SNT Drop-Off Is a Confirmed, Felt Problem

Miranda explicitly validated the ~50% SNT fall-off rate Chris cited, describing it as a real and recurring obstacle at NVC. Her reaction to the automated SNT matching feature was the most visceral positive response in the session▶ 39:29 — "that's exactly what happens." This is not a hypothetical pain point; it is a known failure mode that Society's matching and scheduling tools directly address. This should be a headline feature in NVC's onboarding narrative.

2. Student-Led Culture Creates a Structural Tension with NSLS

Bets raised a meaningful institutional friction point: NVC's new leadership culture explicitly values student agency in program design, and the current NSLS induction model does not easily accommodate that. This is not a product problem per se, but it is a positioning and flexibility problem. Society's modularity and student-driven features (AI coach, self-paced tracks, SNT matching) could be framed as a direct response to this critique — but that framing needs to be made explicit in follow-up conversations with Bets and potentially with NVC administrators.

3. Career Readiness Is an Immediate Cross-Departmental Opportunity

Bets's unprompted identification of the career experience team and career navigators as natural collaborators for Society is a strong signal. She didn't wait to be asked — she saw it immediately. This suggests that at NVC, NSLS has the relationship infrastructure to bridge into career services if Society can provide the right data and tools. The career readiness score, resume review, and dream job features are directly relevant to this opportunity.

4. Institutional Transition Creates Both Risk and Opportunity for Piloting

NVC is in active leadership transition at multiple levels (president, dean, director), and Bets and Miranda are simultaneously managing program documentation and potential structural handoffs. This creates real uncertainty around the pilot commitment — Miranda acknowledged this directly. However, it also means that Society, if positioned correctly, could be a stabilizing and modernizing force that helps NVC's NSLS program survive and strengthen through the transition rather than lose momentum.

5. Data Portability to Alamo Experience Is a District-Level Gap

Bets clarified that Campus Labs has been rebranded to Engkura Engage and is the platform used district-wide as "Alamo Experience" for co-curricular transcripts. The inability to push NSLS participation data into that system means student engagement goes undocumented in the institutional record. This is both a product signal (integration priority) and an advocacy gap — without this integration, NSLS's value is invisible in the official student success infrastructure at Alamo Colleges.

Section 5 — Society Reception

8.5/10

Miranda scored 9–10; Bets scored 8–9. Average for NVC advisors: approximately 8.5/10.

What Landed Well

  • Automated SNT matching: Miranda's response was immediate and enthusiastic — "I'm amazed. Really awesome." She confirmed the drop-off problem and directly validated the feature as solving it. This was the highest-energy moment of the session for NVC advisors.
  • Modern, interactive format: Miranda said Society made her feel like students are "more inclined to finish it since it's more interactive and modernized" — a direct contrast to how she experiences current NSLS delivery.
  • Career readiness features and advisor dashboard: Bets immediately connected the career readiness score and dream job tools to collaboration with NVC's career experience team and career navigators, without prompting. This was a strong organic signal.
  • AI coach as advisor support: Miranda described the coach as giving her "a little bit more hope" — framing it as reducing the burden on part-time advisors who can't be present for students at all times.
  • Modularity and flexibility: Chris's explanation that Society doesn't replace in-person events but enhances them — including the idea of students matching into SNTs live at Leadership Training Day — was received positively and addressed a concern before it was fully voiced.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Induction gate on Clarity Track access: Miranda asked directly whether students must be inducted before unlocking Society. While she accepted the explanation, the question signals that the gate could be a barrier at a campus where induction completion is already a struggle — and where the carrot logic may not resonate for all students.
  • Internal uncertainty around pilot commitment: Miranda was candid that NVC's organizational instability creates hesitation. She used the word "uncertainty" and stopped short of a full commitment, saying "I'm not sure how certain we can" commit.
  • Bets's visual/hands-on learning preference: Bets explicitly said she needs to be "in it" to fully evaluate — a demo alone isn't enough for her to assess or advocate internally. This is a flag for the follow-up plan: she needs a hands-on walkthrough, not just a survey.
  • Alamo Experience integration gap: Bets's clarification that the platform has rebranded (to Engkura Engage) suggests she is familiar with and invested in that system. The absence of an NSLS-to-Alamo Experience data bridge is a real friction point for institutional buy-in at the district level.

Surprises

  • Bets immediately named the career experience team as a natural collaborator — an unprompted, cross-departmental connection that suggests NVC may have stronger internal infrastructure for a Society expansion than their part-time advisor setup implies.
  • Miranda's explicit praise for NSLS's recent structural changes (pillar elimination, fee restructuring) as already improving advisor incentive alignment was an unanticipated positive — it signals that NVC advisors are paying close attention to NSLS's evolution and are responsive to it.
  • Bets's clarification that Campus Labs is now Engkura Engage at Alamo Colleges was a useful and specific piece of intelligence that the broader group didn't have, underscoring her institutional knowledge and attention to district-level infrastructure.
