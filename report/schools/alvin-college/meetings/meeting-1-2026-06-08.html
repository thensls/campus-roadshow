Section 1 — School Snapshot

School: Alvin College (formerly Alvin Community College)

Location: Alvin, TX

School Type: Public 4-Year — recently transitioned from a two-year community college. Rebranded from Alvin Community College to Alvin College as part of this expansion. Additional four-year degree programs continuing to roll out, including one launching the upcoming fall semester.

Chapter Status: Established — an NSLS chapter has been running on campus for approximately two years, primarily in an online format under a previous advisor (John Muttala). Tonya and Sheila are newly transitioning into the advisor role, initially scoped to the business bachelor's program but now expected to oversee the entire campus chapter.

Chapter Type: Online (transitioning toward hybrid) — the chapter has operated fully online. Tonya and Sheila are actively working with CSR Dejeahn Brown to build an in-person presence and increase campus visibility.▶ 7:47

Advisors Present: Tonya Jefferson and Sheila Woods, both faculty members in the business program. Dejeahn Brown (CSR) also participated and provided key context throughout.

Notable Context: The chapter's online-only operation under the previous advisor meant low campus visibility — Tonya noted she was unaware the chapter even existed when she arrived four years ago▶ 7:48 and had been searching for a separate leadership organization for the business bachelor's program▶ 7:48. The school is navigating a significant institutional shift to four-year status, an AI adoption push across faculty and staff, a mandate for closer collaboration between faculty and student services, and outcome-based funding requirements tied to the state of Texas. The institution also currently pays for a competing career platform called Career Coach, which Sheila flagged as a potential friction point for any broader Society rollout.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • CSR relationship quality: Tonya specifically called out Dejeahn Brown's responsiveness and hands-on guidance as instrumental to their onboarding▶ 5:24. She said this human support is something NSLS should never lose.▶ 5:29
  • Asynchronous, on-demand access: Tonya emphasized that the ability for students to access NSLS content at any hour▶ 6:49 (e.g., 2–3 a.m.) is critical for the non-traditional student population▶ 6:35 at Alvin College, many of whom are working adults balancing jobs and coursework.
  • Flexibility of format: Students frequently ask whether they can join on a lunch break or in the late afternoon.▶ 6:49 Any solution must preserve schedule flexibility while also opening the door to in-person options.

What Isn't Working

  • Low campus visibility: The chapter operated so quietly in an online-only mode that Tonya — a four-year faculty member and NSLS member herself — had no idea it existed on campus.▶ 7:48 Students similarly have little awareness that peers are also in the program.
  • Lack of local connection: Students receive national-level communications but lack a sense that someone nearby is personally invested in them. Sheila noted this absence of local, personal touch as a primary barrier to engagement.
  • No sense of community among members: Tonya observed that students receiving invitations may not know who else is enrolled in the program with them, reducing motivation to engage.
  • Advisor transition friction: Transitioning from John Muttala (who was overextended) to Tonya and Sheila — and expanding their scope from the business program to the entire chapter — is creating internal coordination challenges▶ 9:41 unrelated to NSLS itself.
  • No student outcome tracking: Sheila flagged that the institution is severely lacking data on where students go after graduation▶ 15:17 — a gap with direct implications for Texas's outcome-based funding model and grant reporting.

School Priorities

  • Four-year degree expansion: Alvin College is actively growing its bachelor's program offerings, with another degree launching in the fall. Supporting and serving this new student population is a top institutional priority.
  • AI adoption across campus: The institution is pushing faculty and staff to incorporate AI into classes and operations. Both Tonya and Sheila are already AI users (Claude), but many colleagues remain hesitant or resistant.▶ 42:42
  • Faculty-student services collaboration: There is a formal, grant-linked initiative requiring faculty to work more closely with student services to enhance the overall student experience.
  • Career readiness and workforce preparation: A longstanding institutional focus, now formalized through Texas's outcome-based funding model. The school needs to demonstrate that students are entering the workforce prepared, and to document where they go after graduation.
  • Entrepreneurship integration: Sheila flagged entrepreneurship as a specific, active priority within the business program.
  • Student engagement and belonging: Both advisors emphasized that students — particularly non-traditional ones — need to feel a sense of belonging and community, not just access to content.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Non-Traditional Students Require Flexible, Anytime Access

Alvin College serves a large population of working adult learners who access NSLS content at unconventional hours. Tonya repeatedly emphasized that flexibility is non-negotiable — students can't always come to campus events and need the ability to engage on their own schedule. Any in-person expansion must be paired with robust asynchronous options. Society's self-paced AI coach model was seen as directly aligned with this need.

2. Local, Personal Connection Is the Missing Engagement Driver

Both advisors identified the absence of a visible, local human presence as the central reason for low student engagement. Students receive national NSLS communications but don't know that someone at Alvin College is personally connected to the program. Sheila and Tonya see their transition into the advisor role — combined with Society's local personalization capabilities — as the opportunity to close this gap.

3. Outcome Data Is Urgently Needed for Funding and Institutional Credibility

Texas's outcome-based funding model means Alvin College must track and demonstrate what happens to students after they leave. Sheila explicitly said the school is "severely lacking" this data. Society's Career Readiness Score and planned integrations with Handshake, LinkedIn, and the Department of Education were received as highly relevant and timely responses to this institutional pain point.

4. AI Adoption Is an Institutional Mandate With Internal Resistance

The college has formally directed faculty and staff to incorporate AI, but many remain reluctant. Both Tonya and Sheila are already active Claude users and personal advocates. Society's use of Claude as its underlying LLM created an immediate credibility moment. These advisors are well-positioned to serve as internal AI champions — and Society's AI coach could be framed as a concrete, student-facing example of responsible AI adoption.

5. An Existing Paid Platform (Career Coach) Creates Institutional Inertia

Sheila proactively disclosed that the institution currently pays for Career Coach and noted that Alvin College is slow to transi▶ 1:02:02tion away from tools it has already funded. This is the most significant adoption risk identified in the meeting. Dejeahn's reframe — positioning Society as an NSLS member benefit layered on top of existing tools, not a replacement — was well-received and should be the standard messaging approach for this account.

Section 5 — Society Reception

10/10

Tonya rated Society 10/10 and immediately asked to test it herself. Sheila rated it 9–10/10. Sheila also volunteered, unprompted, that she believes Society is better than the institution's existing Career Coach platform.

What Landed Well

  • Career Clarity track: The Renee story resonated strongly — both advisors saw it as directly applicable to their non-traditional students. The idea of a second pathway beyond Foundations of Leadership was described as meeting students where they are rather than forcing a one-size-fits-all model.
  • AI coach personalization: The concept of an AI coach that learns a student's profile and tailors guidance to them was enthusiastically received. Tonya called the combination of SNT matching, meeting transcripts, and personal profile alignment "absolutely phenomenal."
  • Financial literacy component: Tonya specifically noted and wrote down the financial planning element within the Clarity track, calling it "absolutely amazing."
  • Career Readiness Score / advisor dashboard: The idea of a dashboard giving advisors a 72/100-style readiness score for each student, enabling more targeted advising conversations, was seen as immediately useful. Both advisors connected it to institutional reporting and grant accountability needs.
  • SNT matching and in-platform meeting functionality: The ability to surface cohort connections, facilitate meetings within the platform, and have the AI coach follow up on goals set during those meetings was described as a meaningful solution to the engagement and belonging gap.
  • Claude as the underlying LLM: Both Tonya and Sheila are existing Claude users. Sheila's endorsement of Claude led to a moment of genuine enthusiasm and alignment. Dejeahn noted the team is already using Claude internally across departments.
  • School-specific AI agent: The concept of an agent trained on Alvin College's course catalog, faculty directory, and departments — capable of recommending specific courses and helping students connect with professors — generated strong forward-looking interest.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Career Coach competitive overlap: Sheila flagged that the institution already pays for Career Coach and that budget-committed tools are difficult to transition away from. She asked explicitly for patience on any broader institutional rollout.
  • Proactive coaching on confidence gaps: Tonya asked whether the system would automatically prompt students to address low career clarity scores, or whether students would have to seek that help themselves. Chris confirmed proactive nudging is planned but not yet fully built out in the current beta.
  • Institutional email blocking: Tonya requested that all follow-up materials be sent to personal email addresses in case Alvin College's email system blocks external senders — a practical friction point for communication and onboarding.
  • Summer timing for student cohort testing: Both advisors and Dejeahn acknowledged that assembling a student test group during summer requires reaching students who are off-campus, requiring trusted existing relationships to pull it off.

Surprises

  • Tonya's revelation that she — an NSLS member and four-year faculty member — had been completely unaware the chapter existed on campus until recently was a striking illustration of how invisible the online-only model had made the chapter.
  • Sheila's unsolicited declaration that she believes Society is better than Career Coach was unexpected given that she had just raised the competing platform as a potential barrier. Her candor and conviction could make her a powerful internal champion if positioned carefully.
  • Both advisors are already daily Claude users, creating an unusually strong baseline alignment with Society's AI infrastructure before the demo even began.
