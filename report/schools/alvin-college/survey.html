Additional Responses

What’s Working That’s Essential to Keep
One aspect of the current FOL program that is working particularly well and should absolutely carry forward into Society is its flexibility. Many of our students are balancing multiple responsibilities, including full-time employment, parenting, caregiving, and other personal commitments. Providing opportunities for students to engage with the program on their own schedule has been instrumental in supporting participation and reducing barriers. The availability of online options has also been a significant strength. Students appreciate being able to access resources, complete activities, and engage with support services regardless of time or location. This accessibility is especially important for online learners, nontraditional students, and those who may not be able to visit campus regularly. As Society evolves, maintaining these flexible and online components will be critical. Students are more likely to engage consistently when support is convenient and meets them where they are. Preserving the ability to access content through multiple modalities while allowing students to participate at times that fit their schedules will help ensure that the program remains inclusive, student-centered, and effective.
Top Concerns
One area of the current FOL program that could be strengthened is student engagement and module completion. While the content itself is valuable, it can sometimes be difficult to sustain student participation, particularly when students are juggling work, family responsibilities, and coursework. As a result, some students may begin the program with good intentions but not complete all of the available modules. Finding ways to increase engagement and encourage ongoing participation would be beneficial. This could include incorporating more interactive elements, providing timely reminders, recognizing milestones or completion achievements, and clearly communicating how the content connects to students' academic, personal, and career goals. Ultimately, students are more likely to engage when they understand the relevance of the material and when the experience feels meaningful rather than as another item on their to-do list. Building in opportunities that motivate students to return and complete the modules would strengthen the program's overall impact and ensure that more students benefit from the resources being provided.
Missing Capability
Based on the version of Society that was presented, I did not identify any major deficits or missing capabilities that would prevent our institution from benefiting from the platform. The features demonstrated appeared to align well with the needs of our students and institution. The one area that stood out involved students who may show deficits within the strengths category. As Chris mentioned during the presentation, this concern could be addressed by encouraging students to connect with their coach for additional support and guidance. To make this process even more seamless, it may be beneficial to incorporate an automated feature, such as an email notification or a pop-up message, that directs students to their coach whenever an area of concern is identified. This gentle prompt could help bridge the gap between identifying a challenge and seeking assistance, particularly for students who may be hesitant to ask for help on their own. Overall, I was impressed with the platform as presented. Enhancing the visibility of available support resources through proactive notifications would further strengthen the student experience and ensure that those who need additional guidance are connected with the appropriate resources in a timely manner.
What Would Make Students Use It Weekly?
In my opinion, students are far more likely to actively engage with Society if it is convenient, relevant, and easily accessible through the modalities they already use in their daily lives. Today's students are balancing work, family responsibilities, and coursework, so adding another platform that requires multiple logins or intentional effort to remember may limit ongoing participation. Easy access is key. Integration with systems students already use, such as Blackboard, email, or a mobile app with push notifications, would make engagement more seamless. The ability to access Society quickly from a smartphone is especially important, as many students rely heavily on their mobile devices rather than desktop computers. Students also need a reason to return regularly. Personalized reminders, check-ins, progress updates, goal tracking, and recommendations tailored to their strengths and areas for growth could encourage continued use beyond the initial completion. Features that connect students with timely resources, coaches, campus events, or career opportunities tailored to their individual needs would add ongoing value. Ultimately, students will continue using a platform when they perceive it as helpful rather than another task to complete. If Society becomes embedded into the tools they already use and consistently provides meaningful support that helps them navigate their academic and personal goals, I believe it could become an indispensable part of the student experience rather than a one-time activity.
Data / Reporting Needed to Prove ROI
To demonstrate the success of Society to our leadership team, I would want to see the data points identified within the longitudinal report, particularly those that move beyond simple participation metrics and illustrate meaningful student outcomes over time. For example, evidence of improved retention and persistence rates among students actively engaging with Society would be valuable. I would also be interested in seeing correlations between Society participation and academic performance indicators such as GPA, course completion rates, progression toward credential attainment, and graduation rates. Data demonstrating increased student engagement, utilization of support resources, and stronger connections with coaches or campus services would further support its effectiveness. Because institutions are often asked to justify both financial investments and student success initiatives, disaggregated data would also be important. Understanding how Society impacts various student populations, including first-generation students, adult learners, part-time students, and other traditionally underserved groups, would help determine whether the platform is contributing to equity and closing achievement gaps. Ultimately, leadership wants to know whether a tool changes outcomes rather than simply generating activity. The longitudinal data presented appeared to address many of these areas. If Society can consistently demonstrate that students who actively engage with the platform persist, complete, and succeed at higher rates than those who do not, that would provide compelling evidence of its value and support continued institutional investment.
