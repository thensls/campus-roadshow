Section 1 — School Snapshot

School: La Roche University

Location: Pittsburgh, PA (suburban, ~10–20 minutes from downtown)

School Type: Private 4-Year, Catholic

Chapter Status: Established — small presence, actively working to grow post-COVID

Chapter Type: Standard (in-person orientation, leadership training, SNTs, induction ceremony)

Advisors Present: Paige Grogan (Financial Aid; NSLS alum and current chapter advisor)

Notable Context: La Roche is a small private Catholic school in the Pittsburgh suburbs. The NSLS chapter has a modest footprint on campus and is not formally integrated under the student government umbrella, though it now receives a small budget through student development. The chapter was handed to Paige Grogan — an NSLS alum herself — when the previous advisor left for another institution. This is a volunteer role on top of her full-time financial aid position. Post-COVID re-engagement is the overarching institutional priority. A new VP of Financial Aid (who was himself an NSLS member) and a new head of student development (who is actively championing NSLS inclusion in campus events) represent meaningful new internal allies. Enrollment, retention, and benchmarking against peer Catholic private schools are key institutional lenses.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The personal transformation arc for students — Paige Grogan values seeing shy students grow into confident leaders, mirroring her own experience.
  • In-person touchpoints: orientation, leadership training day, the first three SNTs, community service projects, and induction ceremony are all held in person▶ 8:19 and are central to the chapter's identity.
  • Peer-group pairing: students are grouped based on attendance at the same orientation session, with consideration for existing friendships and roommate relationships▶ 8:17.
  • Advisor-led check-ins and email reminders as a light-touch support layer on top of student-driven SNT activity.
  • The SNT framework itself — Paige Grogan personally still uses it in her own professional life and actively teaches students to apply it to career goal-setting.

What Isn't Working

  • SNT submission timing rigidity: The one-per-week submission window is causing real drop-off▶ 6:58. Students who attend meetings but delay logging (due to sports practice, homework, forgetting a laptop) find themselves locked out. A student who attended two meetings in one week could not log both because of the weekly cap▶ 7:00 — even when the advisor knew the participation was genuine.
  • Mobile/app friction: Students who don't have the app on their phone and don't have their laptop with them▶ 6:13 at the time of a meeting frequently fail to log SNTs and then forget entirely.
  • Chapter visibility and institutional integration: NSLS has historically been siloed — not under the student government umbrella, underfunded (previously self-funded from advisor pockets and fundraising), and not included in campus-wide events. This is actively improving but has been a persistent structural issue.
  • Speaker broadcast scheduling: The chapter tried to hold in-person speaker broadcast watch sessions but could not execute them this year due to scheduling conflicts▶ 9:20.
  • Advising blind spots: Paige Grogan noted that without data on member major, grade level, and initial engagement, it is hard to tailor her outreach▶ 36:03 — especially for members she doesn't already know personally from financial aid interactions.

School Priorities

  • Post-COVID re-engagement: the institution is actively pushing to restore and expand campus events and community involvement across all clubs and organizations.
  • Enrollment and retention: financial aid and admissions are collaborating on targeted outreach strategies segmented by student grade level and profile. Paige Grogan is applying these same tactics to NSLS recruitment.
  • Peer benchmarking: La Roche uses comparable small private Catholic schools to set goals and justify resource allocation. Paige Grogan explicitly wants to apply this framework to NSLS data.
  • Increased NSLS campus visibility: supported by the new head of student development and the new VP of Financial Aid (himself an NSLS alum), there is fresh institutional momentum behind elevating the chapter's profile.
  • Career readiness integration: the career office is rebranding, and job/club fairs are being coordinated. Paige Grogan sees NSLS as complementary infrastructure for helping students align major, interests, and career outcomes.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. SNT Logging Friction Is Causing Real Attrition

Paige Grogan described multiple concrete scenarios where students who genuinely participated in SNT meetings were unable to log them due to the weekly submission window, device unavailability, or simple forgetting after a busy schedule. This is not a perception problem — it is a structural compliance barrier. The one-per-week lock is penalizing students for real engagement and frustrating the advisor who has to adjudicate these situations.

2. Peer Benchmarking Is a Novel and High-Value Data Request

Paige Grogan surfaced an insight not previously heard in the roadshow: she wants to filter Society's reporting dashboard by schools that match La Roche's profile (small, private, Catholic, suburban). This mirrors how La Roche uses peer institution data for enrollment and retention decisions. This is a differentiated product signal with potential applicability across many Tier 3 and small-school segments.

3. The Advisor Is the Institution — Volunteer Capacity Is a Real Constraint

Paige Grogan runs the chapter entirely on a voluntary basis, on top of a full-time financial aid role. She is personally motivated and clearly capable, but the chapter's operational bandwidth is essentially one person's discretionary time. Any tool that reduces administrative overhead — automated reminders, dashboard visibility, pre-built reporting — directly addresses her real constraints.

4. Internal Champions Are Newly Aligned and Ready to Be Leveraged

Two new stakeholders — a VP of Financial Aid who was himself an NSLS member, and a new head of student development who proactively committed to including NSLS in campus events — represent fresh institutional support that did not exist before. Society's reporting and data tools could help Paige Grogan make the case upward to these champions more effectively.

5. Career Clarity Resonates as a Student-Facing Value Proposition

Paige Grogan's personal story — entering college thinking she wanted to be an environmental scientist or physical therapist and arriving in business/financial aid — is the exact narrative the Clarity Track is designed to address. She already uses the SNT framework in her own professional life and tells students to do the same. The Clarity Track's career direction features map directly onto both her personal experience and the chapter's pitch to prospective members who ask "what do I get out of this?"

Section 5 — Society Reception

8/10

Paige Grogan self-reported "seven and a half, eight-ish" — reflected here as 8/10.

What Landed Well

  • Clarity Track and career direction features: Paige Grogan immediately connected these to the "what do I get out of this?" question she regularly faces from prospective members. She saw it as a compelling post-induction carrot.
  • Advisor dashboard with member-level data: The idea of seeing a student's major, grade level, and engagement status at a glance resonated strongly, especially given that Paige Grogan currently relies on her financial aid brain or personal familiarity to fill those gaps.
  • SNT matching automation: Directly addresses one of her most concrete pain points around group formation.
  • Career Readiness Score: The concept of a roll-up metric tracking multiple dimensions of student readiness was well-received by someone who is explicitly numbers-driven.
  • Peer benchmarking concept: When Chris reflected this idea back to her, Paige Grogan confirmed and expanded on it — it is clearly a priority lens for her and the institution.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Enthusiasm was qualified: Paige Grogan's 7.5–8 rating was explicitly tied to wanting to see more before fully committing. She is interested but calibrating.
  • No specific technical or workflow objections were raised, but the small school context means any platform complexity or onboarding burden could be a barrier given her solo-advisor capacity.

Surprises

  • The peer benchmarking request was flagged by Chris as something he had not heard from any of the ~29 prior schools — it emerged organically from Paige Grogan's institutional context and represented a genuinely novel product signal.
  • Paige Grogan's new VP of Financial Aid is an NSLS alum — an unexpected internal champion who is already thinking about how to integrate NSLS into admissions marketing, including placing an NSLS t-shirt in the sample dorm room.
  • Paige Grogan's own career trajectory (environmental science → physical therapy → marketing → business/financial aid) is a natural testimonial for the Clarity Track and emerged without prompting during the discovery conversation.
