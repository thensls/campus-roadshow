Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The existing NSLS online platform — described as user-friendly, clean, and self-guiding for students even without staff support.▶ 7:14
  • The step-by-step structure that allows students to navigate independently while still having advisor backup.
  • The in-person community and connection experience — students are actively asking for group chats and ways to connect with each other▶ 8:00, which advisors already encourage informally.
  • The hybrid flexibility — students value being able to attend in-person SNTs or complete requirements online depending on their schedule▶ 19:26 and class conflicts.

What Isn't Working

  • Student skepticism that NSLS is a scam — a persistent barrier, especially at first contact via email invitations. Even students who attended orientation still questioned its legitimacy. Advisors trace this to a broader campus culture of distrust toward paid leadership programs received by email.
  • Difficulty switching between in-person and online modalities seamlessly — some features require students to contact advisors to enable, creating friction.
  • Enrollment timing may be tied to financial aid disbursement cycles — advisors observed (anecdotally) that sign-ups are higher when deadlines align with when students receive financial aid.
  • Communication tools feel limited — chapter leaders and student advisors need more robust, flexible communication channels with segmentation and engagement metrics.
  • Leadership development as a standalone value proposition is becoming harder to defend in budget conversations — there is a growing need to connect programming to workforce readiness and demonstrate outcomes with data.

School Priorities

  • Data collection and assessment — demonstrating year-over-year improvements in student outcomes (retention, GPA, campus involvement) to institutional leadership.
  • Rebuilding and redefining the university's leadership development program — actively evaluating what NSLS offers and how it fits within a redesigned programming structure.
  • Increasing campus engagement post-COVID — the school has seen a bounce-back in student willingness to get involved and wants to capitalize on that momentum.
  • Student awareness and marketing — an internal awareness campaign is being planned; Brandon's proactive tabling and social media work this year drove stronger NSLS results than in prior years.
  • Career readiness and workforce outcomes — students want to know the concrete benefit of any program (resume value, credentials, tangible skills), and the institution needs to demonstrate those outcomes externally.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Connection Is the Core Student Need

Both advisors independently identified student desire for peer connection as a central and recurring theme. Students have asked advisors to set up group chats for them. Ashley noted this as a pattern across all leadership programming at UTRGV, not just NSLS. For a predominantly commuter student body, the platform's ability to facilitate peer matching, SNT scheduling, and in-app chat directly addresses this unmet need.

2. Career Readiness Outperforms Leadership as a Student Hook

Brandon explicitly shared that students want to know what they get out of any program — whether it's a resume line, a credential, or a tangible skill. The platform's Clarity track and career readiness framing resonated strongly with both advisors as a more compelling entry point for students than leadership development alone. Brandon noted that as a recent grad, he personally would have valued this kind of tool as a student.

3. Legitimacy and Trust Are Active Barriers to Enrollment

Student skepticism about NSLS being a scam is a real and recurring obstacle at UTRGV. This distrust is shaped by a broader campus culture where students are conditioned to dismiss paid programs delivered via email. Brandon described students questioning legitimacy even during orientation. Tabling and in-person presence help build credibility over time, with email invitations converting better once students have seen the program on campus first.

4. Advisor Dashboard and Communication Tools Are High-Priority Operational Needs

Ashley raised detailed, specific questions about the advisor-facing side of Society — including visibility into SNT scheduling patterns, bulk messaging with segmentation, engagement metrics (opens, clicks, views), and the ability to use availability data to reserve campus rooms proactively. These are not aspirational wants; they reflect real operational pain points in running the chapter across two campuses with student staff.

5. Society Could Serve as a Programmatic Bridge Across Campus Initiatives

Ashley identified potential for Society to function as a feeder or connector between UTRGV's Freshman Leadership Academy, student mentor program, and NSLS. She specifically asked whether Society's mentoring capability could mirror the structured cohort model used in their freshman program. This signals an opportunity to position Society not just as an NSLS tool but as infrastructure for broader campus leadership and career development programming.

Section 5 — Society Reception

8.5/10

Brandon: 9–10 out of 10. Ashley: 8–9 out of 10. Score averaged across both advisors.

What Landed Well

  • The visual design — Brandon called it "very pretty" and "inviting," noting it was functional and aesthetically engaging for students.
  • The SNT scheduling and availability-matching feature — both Brandon and Ashley reacted positively and immediately saw operational value. Ashley began brainstorming room reservations based on group availability data.
  • The Clarity track and career readiness framing — resonated as a more accessible student hook than leadership development alone. Brandon reflected that he would have personally benefited from this as a student.
  • The AI coach experience — the Renee story and the ability to draft outreach emails, find courses, and set reminders were seen as genuinely useful student supports.
  • Personality assessments (Myers-Briggs, Big Five, etc.) — Ashley noted UTRGV is currently exploring StrengthsFinder, making this feature timely and relevant.
  • The program outcomes report concept — Ashley saw immediate utility in being able to report student outcomes upward to leadership and for grant applications.
  • Brandon's overall reaction was one of positive surprise: "I wasn't expecting all of this."

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Ashley asked specifically whether advisors would have a back-end view of aggregated student availability for SNTs — she wanted to use this data to reserve campus rooms proactively.
  • Ashley asked whether communication tools would include engagement metrics (link clicks, views, message opens) — Chris confirmed intent but noted full advisor access is still being scoped.
  • Both advisors expressed a need to discuss internally and align Society's rollout with existing programming timelines before committing to pilot or Product Council participation.
  • Ashley noted FERPA and privacy considerations as a natural constraint when discussing the advisor-facing AI coach that could surface student data.

Surprises

  • Ashley immediately began ideating on Society as a potential feeder program connecting the Freshman Leadership Academy and student mentor program to NSLS — an integration use case not prompted by the presentation.
  • Brandon's framing that students are sometimes more comfortable interacting with technology than with a human advisor — and that Society could serve as a bridge to human connection — was an unprompted and insightful observation.
  • Brandon's observation that Society could help "legitimize" NSLS and tie it more credibly to the university's institutional goals was offered organically, suggesting strong alignment between Society's value proposition and UTRGV's strategic positioning needs.
