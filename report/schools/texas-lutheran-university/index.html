Meeting Highlights Full Meeting Report ›

✅ What's Working / What Landed
  • 🏆 ▶ 6:18 — Cody cited the intentional, ongoing nature of leadership growth across all three roles — student, officer, advisor — as something core to NSLS that must never be lost.
  • 📦 ▶ 17:13 — Cody noted that NSLS-provided materials — books, flyers, tablecloths, ribbons — are a valued resource that removes the burden of creating promotional content from scratch.
  • 🌐 ▶ 22:11 — After a failed attempt to include grad students in in-person sessions, splitting them into their own online chapter has been successful — including a doctoral student who has never been on campus but is an active officer.
❌ What Isn't Working
  • 💸 ▶ 12:14 — Both advisors independently flagged the $95 fee as a major obstacle. Students have asked for payment plans. Scholarships are being used to offset costs but are not a scalable fix.
  • 📉 ▶ 11:01 — A large share of students pay and never begin the program. Cody estimated 30–50 sign up per semester but only about 40% reach induction. The biggest loss point is students who pay and never start.
  • 👥 ▶ 14:44 — Recurring officer changes have reduced the chapter from seven active officers to three or four at times, making it difficult to execute programming, outreach, or follow-through on engagement emails.
  • ❓ ▶ 16:23 — Cody reported that the majority of recruiting emails he receives ask whether NSLS is a scam. Lack of campus visibility has made this worse and reduced conversion from inquiry to signup.
  • 📱 ▶ 49:04 — Cody noted the existing app is sometimes confusing even for him, with inconsistent navigation between app and web. He and his team have discussed how to meet students where they are — on their phones.
⚠️ Key Signal▶ 20:48 — Career Clarity as a Re-engagement Hook for Lapsed and Non-Traditional Students: Cody's immediate reaction to the Clarity track was that it would resonate strongly with graduate students — particularly those in career transition — and that it offers a different lens than leadership for undergrads who don't yet see themselves as future leaders. He specifically cited English majors who don't know what to do with their degree as a prime use case. Melissa, coming from a workforce development background, responded with personal resonance, saying the career tools were something she wished she had access to during her own recent graduation.
