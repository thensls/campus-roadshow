1 — School Snapshot

Institution: Texas Lutheran University (TLU) — a small, private university located in Texas (Seguin area, near the Alamo Colleges).

Chapter Status: Established — Cody has been involved with the TLU chapter for six years (student → officer → advisor), now in his third year as advisor.

Chapter Type: Hybrid — two distinct chapters as of January: (1) an in-person undergraduate chapter (currently running online this semester due to officer scheduling constraints), and (2) a new online-only graduate chapter launched after a failed attempt to integrate grad students into the in-person model.

Advisors Present:

  • Cody Mazzurana — Lead advisor; TLU alum; former chapter officer and e-board member; now a professional staff member in his second year in higher education. Has advised the chapter for three years.
  • Melissa De La Cruz — Co-advisor; TLU alum; HR Coordinator in TLU's HR department; approximately one year in the advisor role. Recently inducted into NSLS herself.

Notable Institutional Context: TLU launched a new strategic plan in January centered on two institutional priorities: campus vibrancy and student engagement. The chapter is actively trying to align its work with this plan. The chapter has an existing, if underutilized, partnership with TLU's Career Development Office. The chapter has faced persistent officer turnover and a shrinking e-board, which has constrained programming capacity.

2 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Intentional leadership growth across all roles: Cody valued that NSLS offered a continuous leadership arc — from new member to officer to advisor — not just a one-time induction experience. "Even once you completed the first, like getting inducted, you were still able to grow within that leadership ability.▶ 6:03"
  • Cross-chapter connection and perspective: Cody highlighted the value of connecting with students beyond TLU through advanced certifications — "getting to hear different perspectives on something that you're all working on.▶ 6:18"
  • Confidence and leadership skill-building: Melissa emphasized the value of NSLS as a vehicle for mentoring members and building confidence in leadership, particularly for students Cody actively guides.▶ 7:38
  • Branded marketing and physical chapter materials: Cody cited NSLS-supplied tablecloths, ribbons, books, and flyers as genuinely useful tools — "that's not just their invitation they get" — and valued not having to create materials from scratch.

What Isn't Working

  • Chronic engagement decline: Since Cody joined in 2021, there has been a steady drop in students willing to join and to return as officers. "We have seen a steady decline in people wanting to get involved as students."
  • High drop-off after payment: A significant portion of students who pay the membership fee never engage with the program at all. "We probably have a bigger number of students that don't even start after they pay." Semester enrollment runs roughly 30–50 students; induction rates are around 40% of that.
  • The $95 upfront fee is a real barrier: Both advisors independently flagged cost as a meaningful obstacle. Students have asked about payment plans.▶ 9:14 Melissa described it plainly: "The $95 fee is a huge obstacle for a lot of applicants."
  • NSLS legitimacy/brand recognition problem on campus: Cody noted that recruiting emails frequently generate skepticism: "Nine times out of ten, most emails I get when we're recruiting are, 'Are you a scam? Is this a real thing?'" Greater campus visibility is seen as the antidote.
  • Officer follow-through on outreach: Despite having CSR-crafted recruitment email templates available, the officer team has not followed through on sending them. "They get the initial email. We talk about it. We want to do it. And then there's no follow through."
  • SNT scheduling is a persistent friction point: The chapter has adapted by conducting SNTs during speaker broadcasts to remove the burden of students self-organizing — but this workaround reflects how hard the standard SNT process is to sustain.
  • Current app/platform UX is confusing: Cody noted inconsistency between the app and web experience: "It's sometimes confusing for me sometimes to try to find something on the app versus how it's displayed on the web page."
  • Officer capacity limits programming: The e-board has shrunk from seven to three or four members at times, making it difficult to run events or sustain engagement initiatives beyond the minimum.
  • Content feels stale after six years: Cody gently noted that as someone who has been through the process himself, "it might be fun for something new to come out... just a shake-up even for me as an advisor.▶ 9:30"

School Priorities

  • Campus vibrancy and student engagement are the two headline priorities in TLU's new strategic plan, launched January 2025, coming from the president's office.
  • The chapter wants to grow its on-campus footprint — moving beyond the annual org fair to more consistent tabling and presence — both to address brand skepticism and to align with institutional goals.
  • There is an existing but underleveraged partnership with TLU's Career Development Office that advisors want to expand.
  • Advisors are personally interested in career development tools — both Cody and Melissa recently completed master's degrees and see the Clarity track as immediately relevant to their own situations.

3 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Upfront Cost Is a Real Access Barrier — Not Just a Talking Point

Both advisors, independently, named the $95 fee as the single most concrete obstacle to membership growth. Students are asking about payment plans. The chapter has responded with internal scholarships. This is not a perception issue — it is a structural barrier that directly suppresses conversion from nomination to enrollment. Chris acknowledged NSLS is exploring a subscription/installment model, which was received as relevant and welcome.

2. The "Are You a Scam?" Problem Is a Brand and Visibility Problem

Cody described this as a recurring pattern: prospective members who receive recruitment emails respond with skepticism about NSLS's legitimacy. The chapter's proposed solution — more visible tabling, more campus presence — is essentially a local brand-building strategy. Society's career clarity framing (every student needs a job, not just leadership development) could be a more immediately credible value proposition on campus and lower the perception barrier to joining.

3. The Graduate Chapter Is a Distinct and Promising Segment

TLU's online graduate chapter — launched formally in January after a failed hybrid attempt — is already thriving. Cody highlighted a doctoral student in interdisciplinary leadership who has never set foot on campus and is actively serving as an officer. Graduate students appear to be more self-directed, more motivated by leadership and career development, and more naturally suited to an online, AI-supported model. Society's Clarity track was immediately associated with this group: "I'm thinking grad student-wise, probably a good idea. I think they're going to love this."

4. Advisors Want Peer Benchmarking Data, Not Just Internal Reporting

Cody raised an idea that Chris noted he had not heard elsewhere on the roadshow: the ability to compare TLU's chapter metrics against comparable schools or nearby chapters (he specifically mentioned the Alamo Colleges). This is a distinct product signal — advisors want longitudinal data not just to track their own students, but to understand where they stand relative to their peers, and to use that data in institutional conversations about strategic plan alignment.

5. Society's Career Track Has Immediate Personal Resonance with Advisors

Both Cody and Melissa are recent master's graduates and flagged personal interest in the Clarity track for themselves. Melissa said she could imagine using it "on my lunch break." This is a meaningful signal: advisors who are personally excited about a tool are more likely to champion it with students and sustain pilot participation. The advisor-first rollout strategy appears well-matched to this school.

4 — Society Reception

9/10 (composite: Cody ~10, Melissa 7–8)

What Landed Well:

  • The Clarity track's career framing resonated immediately — Cody connected it to students like English majors who don't know what to do with their degree: "I can see it being a good tool for students to use on both levels." Melissa noted her workforce development background made it personally resonant.
  • The SNT scheduling tool was directly acknowledged as addressing a real problem. Cody described the workarounds TLU has already built (conducting SNTs during speaker broadcasts) and recognized the new tool as a meaningful improvement.
  • Mobile-first design was called out explicitly and positively. Cody noted the campus consensus that "you meet students where they're at, and it's on a phone, it's not on a desktop."
  • The administrator dashboard with cohort-level and longitudinal data was welcomed. Cody appreciated the "completion dashboard" as far more user-friendly than the current manual process of checking progress student by student.
  • Gamification and progress tracking — Cody saw potential not just for student engagement but for officer recruitment: "I think it could even help with Melissa and I intentionally recruiting some officers."
  • Micro-learning format replacing the long orientation video was positively received by Melissa, who noted the importance of something "interactive" that would "pique their interest and keep their interest."

What Raised Questions or Friction:

  • Cody expressed measured skepticism about whether undergrad students specifically would engage with the Clarity track without additional encouragement: "I think undergrad, may have to plug in a little more before they're willing to use some of it." He sees grad students as the more natural early adopters.
  • No hard objections were raised, but Melissa's lower score (7–8) was attributed to her relative newness to the chapter and a desire to be realistic about time capacity.

Surprises:

  • Melissa's immediate personal identification with the Clarity track — as a recent grad and NSLS inductee herself — was a positive surprise that suggests advisor-first access could generate genuine internal champions.
  • Cody's benchmarking idea (comparing TLU's data to peer schools) was noted by Chris as novel — not surfaced in prior roadshow conversations.
