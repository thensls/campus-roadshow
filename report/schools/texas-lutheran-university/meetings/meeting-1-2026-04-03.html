Section 1 — School Snapshot

Institution: Texas Lutheran University (TLU) — Private 4-Year University located in Seguin, TX.

Chapter Status: Established. Cody has been involved with NSLS since 2021 — first as a student member, then as a chapter officer, and now entering his third year as an advisor▶ 2:12. The chapter has been operating continuously across that period.

Chapter Type: As of January, TLU officially operates two chapters: an in-person chapter for undergraduate and concurrent students, and a newly formed online-only chapter for graduate students. This semester, the undergraduate chapter is temporarily running online due to officer scheduling constraints.

The chapter regularly fields skepticism from students who ask whether NSLS is legitimate.▶ 14:00 Scholarship support has been used to offset the $95 membership fee barrier.▶ 9:14 The graduate chapter — added fall of the prior year — includes at least one doctoral student in an entirely online interdisciplinary leadership program who has become an active officer without ever setting foot on campus.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The intentionality of leadership growth across all stages — from student member to officer to advisor▶ 6:00. Cody described this continuity as a consistent thread throughout his six years with NSLS.
  • The ability to build connections with other students withi▶ 6:18n the chapter and, through advanced certifications, with students across chapters and institutions.
  • Exposure to diverse perspectives through the program structure — hearing different viewpoints on shared challenges.
  • Melissa emphasized confidence-building and the mentoring of members in leadership positions as core values she wants to see maintained.
  • Physical marketing materials (tablecloths, books, ribbons, flyers) provided by NSLS — described as useful, ready-made tools for campus presence that advisors don't have to create themselves.

What Isn't Working

  • Declining student engagement: Cody identified a steady drop in students willing to join NSLS since 2021, and a parallel decline in students willing to return as officers.
  • Post-payment drop-off: A significant number of students pay the $95 fee and never begin the program. Cody noted this group may be larger than those who start and don't finish. Students who pay and don't engage rarely return or reach out.
  • Induction rate: Roughly 30–50 students pay per semester; induction rate is approximately 40% of that group.
  • The $95 fee as a barrier: Both advisors identified this as a primary obstacle. Students have asked about payment plans. The chapter has used internal scholarships to help some students. Melissa called it "a huge obstacle" and "a hurdle a lot of times."
  • SNT scheduling and coordination: Success Networking Teams have historically been a major drop-off point. The chapter has adapted by folding SNT time into speaker broadcast sessions to reduce the burden of scheduling separate meetings — an improvised workaround, not a systemic solution.
  • Officer capacity and turnover: The chapter has cycled through significant officer changes. With fewer officers, follow-through on outreach and programming has suffered. Cody noted the officer team has not been able to execute on targeted email campaigns despite having templates from Dejeahn.
  • Campus visibility and credibility: The chapter's public presence is limited largely to the annual student org fair in August. Students frequently email asking if NSLS is a scam. Cody described this as a reputational challenge tied to low visibility.
  • App usability: Cody described the current NSLS app as sometimes confusing — noting inconsistency between the app experience and the web experience.
  • Program freshness: Cody noted the program has remained largely the same since he joined six years ago. He expressed a readiness for something new, both for students and for himself as an advisor who has completed the process.

School Priorities

  • Campus vibrancy: A named priority in TLU's new strategic plan launched in January. The chapter sees itself as a potential contributor to this goal.
  • Student engagement: A second explicit strategic plan priority. The chapter's leadership development and now career readiness work are positioned as tools to advance this goal.
  • Campus connection: Related to but distinct from engagement — the idea of students feeling more connected to the institution and to each other.
  • Cody expressed a desire to be a visible, recognized organization on campus year-round — not just at the fall org fair. This is tied to both chapter growth goals and institutional alignment.
  • An existing partnership with TLU's career development office was mentioned — the advisors see Society's Clarity track as an opportunity to deepen and operationalize that partnership.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Engagement Drop-Off Is the Central Problem

Both advisors identified student engagement as the chapter's defining challenge. The problem has multiple layers: students not joining at all, students paying and never starting, students starting and not finishing, and officers not following through on outreach. Cody described this as a pattern that has worsened steadily since 2021. Any Society features that address activation, accountability, or re-engagement will be highly relevant to this chapter.

2. The $95 Fee Is a Real and Recurring Barrier

Both advisors raised the membership fee unprompted and independently. Students have asked about payment plans. The chapter has self-funded scholarships to address the issue. Chris noted that NSLS is exploring subscription-based pricing — this landed as directly relevant and the advisors were receptive. This is not a one-off complaint; it appears to be a consistent friction point in the recruitment funnel.

3. Graduate Students Are an Emerging, High-Potential Segment

TLU launched a separate online chapter for graduate students in the most recent semester after attempts to integrate them into the in-person chapter failed. Cody described graduate students — including one doctoral student who has never been on campus — as more intrinsically motivated and already engaging actively as officers. Cody's immediate reaction to the Clarity track was that graduate students "are going to love this." This segment may be an easier early win for Society adoption than the traditional undergraduate population.

4. Career Readiness Adds a Compelling New Entry Point

Cody responded positively to the framing that not every student wants to be a leader, but every student needs a job. He specifically cited English majors who don't know what to do with their degree as a group the Clarity track could serve. Both advisors expressed personal interest in the career clarity features as recent master's graduates. Melissa's workforce development background made this theme particularly resonant for her. The advisors also saw an opportunity to use Clarity as a bridge to TLU's existing career development office — a partnership they want to strengthen.

5. Peer Benchmarking Would Strengthen Internal Advocacy

Cody raised an idea not previously surfaced in the roadshow: the ability to compare TLU's chapter data against nearby or similar NSLS chapters. He specifically mentioned Alamo Colleges as a reference point. His motivation was twofold — curiosity about best practices and the need to tell a credible story to campus stakeholders about NSLS's impact relative to peer institutions. Chris affirmed this as a novel idea worth exploring. This represents a meaningful product signal from an advisor trying to advocate for NSLS internally.

Section 5 — Society Reception

9/10

Composite average: Cody ~10/10; Melissa 7–8/10.

What Landed Well

  • Clarity track / career readiness: Immediately resonated with both advisors. Cody said it gives students "a different lens to consider" and cited English majors as a clear use case. Melissa connected it directly to her workforce development background.
  • The Renee story: The example of a student who paid, did nothing, and then re-engaged through Clarity landed well as a recognizable scenario. Cody noted the same pattern plays out at TLU regularly.
  • Mobile-first design: Cody explicitly said students are "on a phone, not a desktop" — the mobile-first UX framing was validated without prompting.
  • SNT scheduling and coordination tools: Cody called this a past struggle and said the auto-matching and in-platform meeting tools "could help." He described the chapter's current workaround (folding SNTs into speaker broadcast time) as an improvised fix rather than a real solution.
  • Admin dashboard and career readiness score: Cody specifically called out the completion dashboard as more user-friendly than the current manual process of tracking student progress step by step.
  • AI coach and personalization: The concept of a school-specific agent that could surface TLU faculty contacts, courses, and directed study options was enthusiastically received by Cody, who immediately connected it to TLU's career development office.
  • Gamification and progress tracking: Cody said this could help with recruiting officers by showing students what growth through NSLS looks like in concrete terms.
  • Micro-learning format: Melissa flagged interactivity as something that would "pique their interest and keep their interest." The shift away from a long-form orientation video was implicitly welcomed.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Undergraduate adoption skepticism: Cody expressed that while he believes in the platform, undergraduates "may have to plug in a little more before they're willing to use some of it." He did not elaborate on a specific barrier, but the comment reflects realistic caution about adoption effort.
  • Time commitment of the pilot: Melissa grounded her 7–8/10 enthusiasm score in part on wanting to be "realistic" about time. She said an hour per week felt workable, but the framing suggested capacity is a real consideration for co-advisors in full-time administrative roles.
  • Product Council vs. Pilot Partner: Neither advisor committed to the Product Council in the session. Chris deferred the decision appropriately, suggesting they reassess after the pilot onboarding. No resistance was expressed — just uncertainty about the additional commitment.

Surprises

  • Peer benchmarking idea: Cody independently surfaced the idea of comparing TLU's chapter metrics against nearby NSLS chapters (e.g., Alamo Colleges). Chris noted he had not heard this idea in prior roadshow conversations. It emerged from a question about what data would help advisors tell their story internally.
  • Graduate students as early adopters: The existence of a newly formed, fully online graduate chapter — including an active officer who has never been on campus — was not anticipated in the conversation setup. Cody flagged this segment as likely more receptive to Society than undergraduates, which may represent a useful rollout sequencing insight.
  • Melissa's personal NSLS induction: Melissa disclosed that she was inducted into NSLS herself last year as an advisor — and reflected that the mobile, self-paced features of Society would have been useful to her at that time. This personal connection to the student experience added authenticity to her feedback.
