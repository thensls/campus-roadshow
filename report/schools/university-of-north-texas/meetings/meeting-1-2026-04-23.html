Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Online accessibility — the ability for commuter and remote students to complete the full induction process synchronously or asynchronously is the chapter's single most valued feature of NSLS today.
  • Sydney noted that NSLS has more student interactions than any other program in her office▶ 12:44, largely because of this online flexibility.
  • The existing relationship and support structure with the NSLS team — described as a "pretty good process and relationship."

What Isn't Working

  • The mobile app has persistent reliability issues: wrong event times displayed, content inaccessible, slow load times, and submissions not registering correctly. The chapter has effectively stopped promoting the app to students as a result▶ 4:35.
  • Students are directed to use the NSLS website via browser (desktop or mobile) rather than the app for all report submissions — a workaround that adds friction.
  • Approximately half of students who join NSLS never start the induction process▶ 21:16 — a recognized drop-off problem the chapter has not been able to solve with current tools.
  • SNTs are the primary point of student attrition. The chapter runs a manual, ad hoc process to form and track SNT groups (sign-up sheets, breakout rooms, coordinator tracking) that becomes chaotic when students from one session are unresponsive and must be re-paired with another group.
  • The current platform provides participation and completion counts but does not surface skill-level learning outcomes — making it difficult to tell a compelling story in budget and reporting conversations.

School Priorities

  • A new university president launched a strategic plan in August focused on student success and career readiness — explicitly shifting institutional emphasis away from community service and general extracurricular involvement.
  • UNT has formally adopted the NACE competencies as an institutional framework. Sydney and her team are actively working to align programming with these competencies for both student-facing clarity and institutional reporting.
  • The office has narrowed its scope to leadership programming specifically, with a lens on how students can articulate leadership skills in career and interview contexts.
  • The school faces reputational pressure as a liberal-arts-heavy, music-focused institution — there is internal and external scrutiny around whether students are graduating career-ready. Outcome data would directly address this narrative.
  • Serving a large first-generation college student population (approximately 40%) and a commuter-dominant student body shapes every program and cost decision the team makes.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Career Readiness Is an Institutional Mandate, Not Just a Program Goal

UNT's new president has explicitly repositioned the student affairs office around career readiness and leadership articulation. Sydney's team is redesigning programs to align with NACE competencies and building reporting infrastructure to demonstrate workforce outcomes. Society's Clarity track and career readiness score land directly in this institutional priority — this is not a peripheral interest but a strategic necessity for Sydney's office.

2. Skill-Level Outcome Data Is a Unique and Unmet Need

Sydney explicitly stated that no current NSLS platform data surfaces the specific leadership or career skills students develop through participation — only participation and completion counts▶ 48:21. She described needing the top three skills students gain from NSLS to support budget requests, end-of-semester reports, and institutional justification. Chris noted this was the first time he had heard this request across approximately 20 roadshow calls▶ 51:00. This is a clear product signal for Society's advisor dashboard and reporting layer.

3. App Reliability Has Eroded Trust and Changed Advisor Behavior

Persistent mobile app issues — wrong event times, inaccessible content, failed submissions — have caused Sydney's team to actively stop promoting the app to students. Students are redirected to the browser-based portal instead. This is a notable behavioral workaround that signals a broken experience channel. Any new platform must demonstrate reliability before it will be promoted to students at UNT.

4. Accessibility and Data Compliance Are Institutional Gatekeepers

Sydney raised two compliance concerns unprompted: (1) Texas accessibility law requiring platforms to support screen readers and assistive technologies▶ 37:39, and (2) the need for IT department approval and data privacy compliance (FERPA, SOC 2). She noted she would need to check with IT before any rollout. She also raised the topic of AI safety — specifically, whether the AI coach has protocols for surfacing concerning student behaviors▶ 39:56. These are not hypothetical concerns; they reflect active institutional processes at UNT that will determine adoption timelines.

5. First-Generation Students and Cost Sensitivity Shape Every Adoption Decision

With approximately 40% first-generation college students, Sydney flagged that the current $95 membership fee already generates student hesitation. Any increase in price — or perception of added cost for new tracks — would amplify this barrier. She proactively proposed a department-level purchasing model for the Clarity track so that students would only need to pay for the leadership component▶ 1:00:43. This pricing sensitivity will need to be addressed clearly in pilot onboarding conversations.

Section 5 — Society Reception

9/10

What Landed Well

  • The Clarity track and AI coach concept resonated immediately — Sydney had independently conceived of a nearly identical tool in a campus workforce readiness ideation session▶ 1:36, validating the concept before seeing the demo.
  • The career direction and dream job generation features were described as genuinely exciting. Sydney engaged directly with the AI coach live, asking it a personal career question about education roles beyond K–12 teaching▶ 21:34.
  • The SNT matching and scheduling tool generated real interest as a solution to a known pain point — Sydney asked specific follow-up questions about how the matching logic would interact with her current coordinator-managed process.
  • The advisor dashboard with longitudinal data and individual student progress tracking was well received as a significant improvement over current reporting capabilities.
  • The career readiness score concept was noted as potentially useful for institutional reporting, especially given the NACE alignment.
  • The framing of Society as an add-on to — not a replacement for — Foundations of Leadership helped reduce concern about disruption to existing workflows.
  • The positioning of AI as a critical thinking support tool rather than a replacement for human judgment was explicitly appreciated.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Accessibility compliance: Sydney asked whether the platform had been tested against Texas accessibility law requirements for screen readers and assistive technology. Chris acknowledged testing is incomplete and that the beta process would help address this.
  • AI safety and concerning behavior protocols: Sydney asked whether the AI coach has mechanisms to flag or escalate if a student discloses distressing or concerning information. Chris acknowledged this had not been deeply addressed yet.
  • IT and data compliance approval: Sydney indicated she would need to route any adoption through IT for FERPA and data security review before committing.
  • Pricing structure uncertainty: Sydney asked directly whether new tracks would cost students more. The current answer (included in existing membership, with modular pricing coming later) was accepted, but she immediately proposed a department purchasing model to protect first-gen students from cost increases.
  • SNT coordinator override: Sydney asked whether her S&T coordinator could manually adjust AI-generated team matches — a workflow the current system does not support. Chris acknowledged this as a gap to explore.
  • Implementation pathway: Sydney wanted to understand how other institutions are integrating Society alongside the existing leadership track before making a final decision.

Surprises

  • Sydney had independently conceptualized an AI career exploration tool in a campus ideation session — she described seeing Society as essentially the tool she had already imagined, which created an unusually high baseline of enthusiasm and conceptual alignment.
  • The skill-level outcome data request was flagged by Chris as unique — no other school in approximately 20 roadshow calls had raised this specific need, making UNT a potentially valuable input source for Society's reporting roadmap.
  • UNT already runs SNTs at orientation and Leadership Training Day while students are physically present — a proactive workaround that meaningfully reduces their drop-off exposure compared to schools that rely on students to self-organize post-session.
