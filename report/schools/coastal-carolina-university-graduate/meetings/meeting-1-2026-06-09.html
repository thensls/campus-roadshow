Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • In-person programming as the primary engagement mode — Daphne was unequivocal that moving to fully online would cause membership numbers to "plummet" and is not aligned with campus culture.
  • The hybrid model they have built: in-person first, with virtual availability the following day for those who cannot attend.
  • The strong in-person e-board culture — Maryanna noted the incoming e-board is already trained and described them as "rock stars."
  • The articulation agreement covering advanced and executive NSLS courses, which remains on the legacy system through fall.
  • The momentum and community built from years of in-person relationship investment.

What Isn't Working

  • Society login via magic link (email verification) was broken for multiple users during the live session — both Daphne Holland and Megan Grandey received "verification link expired or already used" errors repeatedly.
  • Megan Grandey was also unable to log into the existing NSLS legacy dashboard with her credentials (invalid credentials error), a separate issue resolved temporarily by a CSR password reset.
  • Society has not yet been trained on school-specific data (course catalog, faculty directory, departments) — the AI coach cannot make campus-specific recommendations yet.
  • The in-person facilitation support features (SNT matching in-platform, orientation flow, LTD support) are not yet available in Society, creating a gap for chapters running hybrid or in-person models.
  • No leadership track exists in Society yet (confirmed as under development).

School Priorities

  • Successfully launching the graduate chapter with Tyrone McKenney before or at the start of fall semester.
  • Completing the Society pilot testing cycle (advisors → faculty/staff → students) before orientation begins in a matter of weeks.
  • Maintaining strong in-person induction and programming for the undergraduate chapter in fall.
  • Coordinating fall orientation dates and event scheduling with the e-board (meeting with Lily referenced as upcoming).
  • Using chapter credits for Summit registrations — logistics actively in progress.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Society as a Post-Induction Benefit, Not a Replacement

Both Daphne and Maryanna aligned strongly around the framing of Society as a "carrot" unlocked after induction — preserving the in-person programming pipeline while adding a digital career readiness layer. Daphne used the exact framing: "once you get through all of this, this is an added benefit that you guys get all of this access." This is a replicable positioning model for hybrid-culture chapters.

2. Graduate Chapter as a Natural Parallel Pilot

Maryanna proactively proposed using the graduate chapter (led by Tyrone McKenney) as a second pilot track running Society as the primary platform in an asynchronous model — in contrast to the undergraduate chapter's post-induction benefit approach. This creates a rare A/B comparison opportunity across two delivery models at the same institution. Tyrone McKenney has not yet been engaged directly and represents an open onboarding item.

3. Urgency Around Pilot Testing Window

Orientation is imminent. Daphne explicitly noted that faculty are largely off-campus and the window to complete advisor, faculty, and student testing is extremely narrow ("if they're going to do it, we got to do it now"). The product team needs to move quickly on invitations and onboarding support to hit the pre-orientation deadline.

4. Authentication Bugs Discovered Live

Multiple login failures were documented in real time: magic link expiration errors for both Daphne and Megan on Society, and a separate invalid credentials failure for Megan on the legacy NSLS dashboard. These are the first confirmed Society bugs surfaced in a roadshow setting. The product team received screenshots and screen-share visuals. Gmail login was confirmed as a functional workaround for Society access.

5. Broad Enthusiasm for Career Clarity Value Proposition

Maryanna expressed genuine personal enthusiasm for Society's career clarity features, noting their relevance beyond students — including for people already in the workforce who are unhappy or seeking validation. This "broader than students" signal suggests the tool could resonate with faculty reviewers, career services, and the graduate audience Tyrone McKenney will serve.

Section 5 — Society Reception

8/10

What Landed Well

  • The career clarity track and dream job recommendations — Maryanna described immediate personal resonance, noting the tool validates people already in careers and offers new direction to those who are unfulfilled.
  • The framing of Society as a post-induction benefit / membership perk — Daphne adopted the language immediately and repeated it back as "these are the things to focus on."
  • SMART goal methodology integration within the AI coach — presented clearly and seen as a practical accountability tool.
  • The values-sorting flow within personal insights — the progressive narrowing (top 12 → 8 → 6) was walkable and understandable in demo.
  • Maryanna's enthusiasm was explicit: "I'm really excited for this new product. I think it's going to be huge."
  • The graduate chapter fit — Maryanna immediately saw Society's asynchronous-first structure as a natural fit for the graduate chapter model being built with Tyrone McKenney.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • SNT matching in-platform is not available for the hybrid/in-person rollout model — Chris acknowledged this as a meaningful missing feature for this fall.
  • No leadership track yet built — noted as under development, but its absence means the platform is incomplete for the Foundations of Leadership workflow.
  • In-person facilitation features (orientation flow, LTD support) are not yet in Society — the legacy system still handles this better, which limits Society's near-term utility for undergraduate chapter operations.
  • School-specific AI training is not yet complete — the coach cannot reference Coastal Carolina's course catalog, departments, or faculty, limiting the relevance of its campus-specific recommendations.
  • Login authentication failed for multiple participants during the live session, undermining confidence in the readiness for wider pilot distribution.

Surprises

  • Maryanna surfaced the graduate chapter opportunity unprompted during the rollout planning discussion — this was not on the agenda and added meaningful scope to the pilot.
  • The login bug was discovered live, in front of the advisor being asked to become a pilot partner — a notable timing risk that was handled transparently by Chris.
  • Daphne's willingness to leverage her dean/associate dean network for faculty testing was not anticipated and expands the potential reach and credibility of the pilot feedback cohort.
  • Maryanna expressed interest in having a personal connection (her niece) test Society — an unsolicited signal of personal trust in the product beyond professional obligations.
