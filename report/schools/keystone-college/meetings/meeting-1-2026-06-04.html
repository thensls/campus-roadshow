Section 1 — School Snapshot

School: Keystone College

Location: La Plume, PA (Northeast Pennsylvania)

School Type: Private 4-Year

Enrollment: Approximately 600–700 students

Chapter Status: New / Establishing — advisors described themselves as "just getting our feet wet this past year" with NSLS▶ 3:18

Chapter Type: Standard (in-person leadership training day referenced; no hybrid or online-only structure mentioned)

Advisors Present: Carl Hicks (Assistant Vice President of Student Life) and Lucas R. Taylor (Director of Student Life)

Notable Context: Keystone is a small institution that serves a high proportion of first-generation and lower-income students. The school has appeared in the top 10 of U.S. News & World Report regional rankings for upward mobility. Retention is the institution's primary strategic priority. Career services has experienced recent staff turnover but currently has a new hire who is actively building programming, including a well-received spring career fair. The chapter is co-advised by Hicks and Taylor, both of whom acknowledged limited familiarity with the NSLS system early on; that friction has since been resolved through consistent support from their CSR, Dejeahn Brown▶ 6:50.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The existing NSLS mobile app — Carl specifically cited it as something students can download and use at their leisure▶ 5:27, and noted it as a positive feature worth keeping.
  • Clear student-facing checklists showing what members need to complete to reach induction — Lucas emphasized this as particularly valuable for student clarity▶ 7:54.
  • The relationship with their CSR (Dejeahn Brown) — Lucas credited consistent CSR support as the factor that resolved early friction▶ 6:50 with the platform.

What Isn't Working

  • Student engagement is persistently difficult. Carl described a recurring pattern where every new outreach method — apps, email, text messaging — initially seems promising but fails to sustain engagement▶ 11:39. Students have blocked the school's texting service and ignore college email▶ 11:39.
  • Early in their chapter, advisors lacked sufficient knowledge of the NSLS system to answer member questions, which limited their effectiveness and likely contributed to member drop-off.
  • Career services has been unstable due to staff turnover, making it difficult to build sustained career readiness programming — though this appears to be improving under new leadership.
  • Current success tracking is thin — when asked what data they use to measure NSLS success, the implied answer was limited to enrollment counts, member counts, and induction numbers.

School Priorities

  • Retention is the top institutional priority. Carl described it as the "biggest focus" — ensuring students see the value of Keystone▶ 8:19 and buy into the campus community enough to stay enrolled.
  • Upward mobility and career outcomes are central to Keystone's identity and external reputation, particularly given the first-generation and financially constrained student population they serve. The school's U.S. News & World Report top-10 upward mobility ranking was cited with pride▶ 8:25.
  • Career readiness infrastructure is being actively rebuilt. A new career services professional has been brought in with a strong focus on internship placement and career events; the first career fair was described as "extremely successful."▶ 10:37
  • Student engagement at a small residential campus with a difficult-to-reach student body is an ongoing operational challenge that leadership is actively trying to solve.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Retention Is the Lens Through Which Everything Is Evaluated

Carl framed nearly every institutional priority through the lens of retention. Keystone's small size, first-gen student population, and financial pressures make keeping enrolled students the dominant strategic concern. Society's potential to increase NSLS engagement and give students a tangible, personalized reason to stay invested in their membership and their institution maps directly onto this priority.

2. Student Engagement Is a Chronic, Unsolved Problem

Carl described a pattern of failed engagement attempts — apps, email, text blasts — where each new channel quickly stops working. This is not a failure of effort but a structural challenge with a student population that is hard to reach through broadcast methods. Society's personalized, AI-driven approach — meeting students where they are with individually relevant content — directly addresses this and distinguishes it from the tools Keystone has already tried.

3. Career Readiness Has Institutional Momentum and External Stakes

Career services is newly reinvigorated at Keystone, and the school's upward mobility ranking in U.S. News & World Report creates real external pressure to demonstrate career outcomes. Carl connected career readiness directly to the school's identity and student value proposition. The career readiness score and Clarity Track are well-timed features for this school's current trajectory.

4. Advisors Want to Be Freed From Administrative Overhead

Carl explicitly said Society would free them up to "be more effective with members"▶ 37:30 by helping answer student questions and track progress automatically. In their first year, advisors were often unable to answer members' procedural questions because they didn't know the system well enough. A platform that reduces that burden — particularly through the AI coach and advisor dashboard — is directly valuable to advisors operating without dedicated NSLS staff time.

5. Exclusivity and Student Pride Are Legitimate Motivators at This School

Carl unprompted identified the value of students being able to say Society is "unique to me"▶ 39:17 and "something I can feel proud about" — and connected that pride directly to the Keystone brand. For a small school competing for student loyalty and retention, the pilot partner framing (early access, one of very few campuses in the world with access) has real resonance beyond just product novelty.

Section 5 — Society Reception

10/10

Both Carl Hicks and Lucas Taylor gave Society a 10 out of 10 when asked at the close of the meeting. The score was confirmed by Dejeahn Brown on behalf of both advisors.

What Landed Well

  • The AI coach demonstration generated genuine positive reaction. Carl described it as "neat" and "great" — understated but consistent with his communication style▶ 17:29. The concept of a student receiving personalized, actionable career guidance clearly resonated.
  • The career readiness score was received as "a very useful tool" by Carl▶ 36:36. The connection Chris drew to U.S. News & World Report rankings was a strong contextual tie-in given Keystone's prior ranking for upward mobility.
  • The framing that Society would free up advisor time — so they can focus on human-centered support rather than logistics — was explicitly affirmed by Carl as something that would make them "more effective."
  • The idea that Society is something students can take with them after graduation and continue using resonated strongly. Carl spontaneously extended this into a narrative about student pride and institutional differentiation.
  • The Renee story appeared to land well as a framing device — a non-traditional student who did nothing in NSLS until the AI coach created personal relevance. This mirrors Keystone's own engagement challenges.
  • The SNT matching and meeting management features were received without friction — Carl said it was "all pretty clear."▶ 33:41

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • No significant friction or skepticism was surfaced during the meeting. Both advisors were receptive throughout and did not raise objections, budget concerns, or implementation worries.
  • Neither advisor proactively asked about the transition from the current NSLS system to Society, though Chris addressed this preemptively by noting it would use the same login and that pilot schools would still have access to both systems.
  • When asked what data they currently use to track NSLS success, there was a notable pause and no direct answer — suggesting limited current engagement with NSLS reporting tools. This is a gap to address in onboarding.

Surprises

  • Carl independently framed Society as a source of student pride and a differentiator for Keystone's brand — without prompting. This organic connection to institutional identity was not a talking point introduced by Chris and represents an authentic value driver specific to this school.
  • The advisors were notably agreeable and low-friction throughout — somewhat atypical for a first product demo. This may reflect their newness to NSLS (less entrenched in existing workflows) and the strong relationship already built by Dejeahn Brown.

Section 6 — Feature-Level Feedback

Feature Signal Notes
AI Career Coach (Clarity Track) Strong Carl said "it's neat, that's great" after the live coach demo. The concept of personalized, always-available career guidance maps directly to Keystone's career readiness push and engagement challenges. No questions or concerns raised.
Career Readiness Score Strong Carl called it "a very useful tool." The tie-in to U.S. News & World Report rankings and upward mobility — which Keystone already tracks as an institutional metric — gives this feature natural institutional credibility. Strong reporting-up-the-chain use case.
Student-Facing Progress Checklists (App) Strong Lucas specifically called out checklist clarity — knowing what to complete to reach induction — as a feature he wants preserved. This is a "do not break" item for this school.
Personality Assessments (Myers-Briggs, DISC, Enneagram, Holland Code, Big Five) Positive No direct verbal reaction, but the assessments were presented during the demo without pushback. Given the school's focus on student self-awareness and career direction, this feature is contextually well-suited. No friction noted.
Advisor Dashboard & Longitudinal Data Positive Carl confirmed that better tracking would help them guide members and answer questions more effectively. However, their current chapter is only one year old, so longitudinal analysis value will grow over time. Current data use is minimal — onboarding should set realistic expectations.
SNT Matching & Meeting Management Positive Carl said it was "all pretty clear" with no questions. The asynchronous matching option and in-app scheduling, recording, and chat features were presented without friction. No specific enthusiasm expressed beyond acceptance.
School-Trained AI Agent (Course Catalog, Staff Directory) Open Question Presented as a future capability. No reaction recorded from Keystone advisors specifically. Given the small campus size and active rebuilding of career services, this feature could be highly valuable — but needs follow-up to assess feasibility and advisor interest in configuring it.
Post-Graduation Access to Society Strong Carl spontaneously extended this into a student pride and institutional brand narrative — noting students would see it as unique and something that helps them beyond Keystone. This was the most organically enthusiastic moment in the meeting.
Foundations of Leadership v2.0 Integration Positive Received without friction when Chris clarified it would be available in Society by fall. No specific questions. Given the chapter's newness, advisors are not deeply invested in legacy FOL workflows, which lowers change-management risk.
Dual-System Transition (Society + Legacy Login) Needs Care Neither advisor raised concerns, but they are new enough to NSLS that they may not fully anticipate transition friction for students. The same-login assurance was noted but should be reinforced during onboarding. Worth monitoring as students begin using both environments.
