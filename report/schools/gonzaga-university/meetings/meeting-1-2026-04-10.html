Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Bulk email messaging feature in the current platform — Ayaka specifically called this out as a feature she values and actively uses to drive engagement▶ 9:45.
  • Ability to target students by stage in the induction process when sending communications▶ 9:45 — Ayaka highlighted this segmentation capability as important for reaching the right students at the right time.

What Isn't Working

  • Bulk email tool lacks versatility and reader-friendly formatting — Ayaka noted (echoing a point from Daphne Holland) that the email feature could be improved with capabilities like hyperlinks▶ 10:20 to make messages more effective.
  • No friction points unique to Gonzaga were explicitly stated beyond the email formatting limitation; Ayaka's other concerns were expressed at the institutional priority level rather than as direct platform complaints.

School Priorities

  • Growing graduate programs and professional certificate offerings — Ayaka flagged this directly in the chat▶ 19:43, noting it as an institutional priority and raising questions about student affairs' role in supporting working professionals.
  • Increasing student engagement — implied by Ayaka's emphasis on using segmented bulk messaging to move students through the induction funnel more effectively.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Targeted Communication as an Engagement Driver

Ayaka's most substantive contribution centered on the bulk email messaging feature and the value of being able to segment outreach by where students are in their induction journey▶ 8:52. This signals that Ayaka sees proactive, staged communication as a primary lever for improving completion rates — and expects the platform to support that workflow natively.

2. Graduate and Professional Learner Expansion

Ayaka raised the topic of increasing graduate programs and professional certificates for working professionals unprompted▶ 19:43, framing it as an institutional priority and asking about student affairs' role in that growth. This mirrors a theme raised by other advisors in the session (notably Hunter Hammock at UT) and suggests cross-institutional demand for Society to serve non-traditional and post-traditional student populations.

3. Platform Usability and Email Formatting Gaps

Ayaka endorsed a point raised by Daphne Holland about improving the email tool's formatting capabilities — specifically adding hyperlinks▶ 10:20. While a seemingly minor feature request, it reflects a broader signal that advisors are using the communications tools heavily and want them to be more polished and functional for student-facing outreach.

4. Measured Enthusiasm — Needs Deeper Discovery

Ayaka's 8/10 enthusiasm score was the most reserved in the session. Combined with her relatively limited verbal participation, this suggests she is interested but may have unresolved questions or institutional constraints that weren't surfaced in the group format. A 1:1 follow-up with Ayaka could unlock more specific feedback relevant to Gonzaga's context.

5. Student Affairs' Role in Career and Professional Development

Ayaka explicitly named student affairs as a stakeholder in the conversation about graduate programs and professional certificates. This suggests she is thinking about Society not just as a chapter management tool, but as something that could have broader institutional relevance — and that alignment with student affairs leadership may be important for adoption at Gonzaga.

Section 5 — Society Reception

8/10

What Landed Well

  • The overall platform concept generated positive engagement from Ayaka, reflected in her 8/10 score.
  • The career direction and professional development framing of Society appeared to resonate, given her independent comment about graduate programs and professional certificates — themes directly addressed by the Clarity track.
  • The idea of a force-multiplier AI coach supporting advisors and students aligns with Ayaka's interest in using platform tools (like bulk email) to increase reach and engagement without proportionally increasing her own workload.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Ayaka did not verbally surface any specific concerns during the demo, but her score of 8 (versus the group's predominant 10s) implies some reservations. The nature of those reservations was not captured in this session.
  • The complexity and volume of new features flagged by Dan Balski — how to introduce all these layers to students clearly — is likely relevant for Ayaka's context as well, though she did not state this directly.

Surprises

  • Ayaka contributed primarily through chat and required Zoë Wallis to surface her input for the group — suggesting she may be more comfortable in written or asynchronous formats, or that the group dynamic (dominated by more vocal advisors) limited her opportunity to speak.
  • Her institutional priority around graduate and professional certificate growth was raised independently and without prompting, suggesting it is a real and active concern at Gonzaga rather than a reaction to the presentation.
