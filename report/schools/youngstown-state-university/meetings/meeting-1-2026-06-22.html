School Type: Public 4-Year

Advisors Present: Rebecca McCallister (Career Services Advisor, Liberal Arts & Creative Arts); Alexis Scott (CSR, NSLS)

Notable Context: Rebecca is approximately 6 months into her role at YSU, having been assigned as NSLS advisor shortly after joining the institution▶ 0:25. Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The online accessibility of NSLS — Rebecca specifically cited the online aspect as something she values and wants to retain▶ 2:59, noting the chapter had previously gone fully online.
  • The opportunity to layer in-person elements on top of the online foundation — she is working with Alexis to introduce low-lift in-person touchpoints that build camaraderie▶ 3:10 without overhauling the current model.
  • The chapter's connection to NSLS National Events, in which YSU is already enrolled — Rebecca envisions on-campus speaker series attendance eventually counting as a group NSLS activity.

What Isn't Working

  • SNT completion is a known and acknowledged gap — Rebecca confirmed that not many students have completed their SNTs.
  • Roster hygiene had been neglected prior to Rebecca's arrival; she manually cross-referenced the NSLS member list against Banner/Hero ICO▶ 3:49 to archive inactive, graduated, or unenrolled students — a time-consuming process done without automated support.
  • Low student engagement with campus events, particularly evening programming — YSU's commuter population drives home after class and does not return for late-night events▶ 6:32.
  • Getting students to proactively schedule career advising appointments is a persistent challenge.
  • "Career exploration" appointments — students arriving with no direction — consume significant advisor time without structured pre-work from the student.
  • The chapter currently has no president following a recent graduation, compounding the rebuilding challenge.
  • Career Services recently lost its role in orientation advising, requiring the department to rethink how it gains student touchpoints▶ 5:34.

School Priorities

  • State-mandated push toward workforce readiness and work-based learning — including professional development for on-campus employees and expanded internship access for students whose programs don't require it.
  • Increasing intentional, repeated student outreach — classroom visits, tabling at orientations▶ 5:10, and departmental visibility efforts have replaced the lost orientation advising role.
  • Rebuilding the NSLS chapter from the ground up — establishing active student leadership, re-engaging existing members, and creating a functional organizational structure for fall.
  • Generating completion and engagement data that can be reported to the state to demonstrate that career services programming is actively working.
  • Expanding reach of career tools beyond the current NSLS membership (~300 students) to the broader campus population (~12,000 students).

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Commuter Campus = Asynchronous-First Engagement Model

Rebecca explicitly identified YSU's commuter population as the central structural challenge to student engagement. Students come to class and leave; late-night events draw poor attendance; in-person programming is aspirational but not yet realistic at scale. Society's fully online, self-paced model is a direct fit for this context. Rebecca confirmed the chapter is entirely online and noted this as a feature, not a limitation, for now.

2. Career Exploration Is the Primary Student Need

Rebecca described a high volume of "career exploration" appointments — students who arrive with no career direction and require significant advisor time to work through foundational self-discovery. She responded positively to Society's Personal Insights and Career Clarity tracks as a pre-appointment tool▶ 8:40 that could allow students to arrive at advising sessions having already done meaningful self-reflection, making her time more productive and impactful.

3. State Workforce Mandates Are Creating Institutional Urgency

Rebecca unprompted referenced a state-level push for work-based learning and expanded internships, framing completion and participation data as something the state is actively looking for. She connected Society's potential to generate completion and career readiness data directly to this reporting need — signaling that the platform could serve an institutional compliance and advocacy function, not just a student engagement function▶ 32:07.

4. Scale Limitation Requires Platform Openness Beyond NSLS Membership

When asked whether a career readiness score could be valuable for state reporting, Rebecca immediately noted that fewer than 300 of YSU's 12,000 students are NSLS members▶ 33:20. She responded affirmatively when asked whether opening the Clarity Track to all students could be beneficial. This is a clear signal that the value proposition of Society at YSU is contingent on its ability to serve the broader student population, not just NSLS members.

5. Manual Administrative Work Is a Friction Point Ripe for Automation

Rebecca described spending significant time manually auditing the NSLS roster against Banner/Hero ICO to archive inactive students and identify who is currently enrolled. She recognized this as a "huge" administrative task and responded positively when the presenter noted that Society's SIS integration could eventually help with this type of data management. This represents a clear adoption accelerator — reducing admin burden for an advisor who is new, under-resourced, and rebuilding from scratch.

Section 5 — Society Reception

7/10

What Landed Well

  • The Career Clarity and Personal Insights tracks resonated immediately given Rebecca's career services role — she recognized direct overlap with tools and workflows already used in her office.
  • The concept of students doing self-directed career exploration before arriving at advising appointments was compelling — she described it as beneficial for pre-appointment preparation.
  • The SNT matching and scheduling tool addressed a known, acknowledged gap at YSU; she confirmed SNT completion is low and welcomed a system-level solution.
  • The career readiness score concept was well-received in the context of pre-meeting advisor prep, drilling into specific student gaps before appointments.
  • The AI coach's ability to surface career services appointments and reduce the "why do I need to go talk to them?" barrier was described as exciting.
  • The word-of-mouth potential of Society resonated strongly — she independently described a scenario where students tell friends about the platform, organically growing participation.
  • The parallel availability of legacy NSLS and Society platforms reduced anxiety about disruption during the chapter rebuild.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Rebecca wants to verify the accuracy of AI-generated personality assessments (specifically MBTI) against her own known results before trusting the tool with students.
  • She raised the importance of being able to log in-person service participation (e.g., mock interviews conducted through her office) within the platform — this capability was described as in development but not yet available.
  • The career readiness score's relevance for institutional reporting was qualified by the small NSLS membership relative to total campus enrollment — she needs broader student access to make the data meaningful at scale.
  • As a self-described hands-on learner, Rebecca expressed that her 7/10 score reflected needing direct platform experience before forming a stronger opinion — enthusiasm is contingent on the quality of her personal walkthrough.

Surprises

  • Rebecca's background in early childhood education and Head Start administration — rather than career services — makes her perspective on student development and structured programming unusually grounded and practitioner-oriented despite being new to the career advising context.
  • She independently identified word-of-mouth student advocacy as a growth mechanism without prompting, suggesting a grassroots channel instinct that could be useful for pilot design.
  • The framing of the chapter rebuild as an advantage for piloting — rather than a liability — was notable; Rebecca seemed open to the idea that being in a rebuilding phase makes YSU a useful test case rather than a poor fit.

Section 6 — Feature-Level Feedback

Feature Signal Notes
Career Clarity Track Strong Directly mirrors career exploration work done in Rebecca's office. Cited as beneficial for pre-appointment preparation, enabling students to arrive with self-knowledge rather than starting from zero.
Personal Insights Track (AI Personality Assessments) Open Question Rebecca wants to personally test the MBTI output against her known type before trusting results. Accuracy validation is a prerequisite for her confidence in recommending it to students.
AI Coach Positive Recognized as useful for guiding students through career exploration independently. Appreciated that responses are concise rather than lengthy LLM-style outputs. Hands-on testing pending.
SNT Matching & Scheduling Tool Strong Rebecca confirmed SNT completion is a documented gap at YSU. The availability-based matching and asynchronous chat/video meeting capability directly addresses this barrier for an online-only chapter.
Career Readiness Score (Advisor Dashboard) Positive Valued for pre-appointment advisor prep. Raised a scale concern — meaningful for individual advising but limited for institutional reporting given small NSLS membership vs. total enrollment (~300 vs. ~12,000).
Advisor Progress Dashboard & Data Reporting Strong Completion data is exactly what the state is requesting around workforce readiness and work-based learning. Rebecca wants data that proves career services programming is working and that students are completing required steps.
In-Person Service Participation Logging Needs Care Rebecca specifically requested the ability to log participation in career services office activities (e.g., mock interviews) within the platform. Currently in development; this is a gap that matters for her use case.
School Agent (AI Campus Integration) Positive The concept of an AI agent that learns campus resources and proactively recommends career services appointments was received positively. Addresses the core challenge of students not knowing why they should visit career services.
Clarity Track Access for Non-NSLS Students Strong Rebecca responded affirmatively without hesitation when this was raised. Expanding access beyond ~300 NSLS members to the broader 12,000-student campus population is a prerequisite for the platform to have institutional-level impact at YSU.
Goal Setting, Reminders & Gamification / Badges Positive No direct objections raised. Recognized as beneficial for student accountability. Rebecca noted the value of students being able to surface completed goals and badges on resumes or in professional contexts.
SIS Integration (Banner / Hero ICO) Strong Rebecca manually performed roster-to-SIS reconciliation as a recent administrative project. Any automation of this process would be a significant time-saver. She uses Banner/Hero ICO — confirmed by name during the meeting.
Campus Events via Society (Career Fairs, Speaker Series) Positive Rebecca has a stated goal of having students attend campus speaker series events as a group NSLS activity. Ability to post and track non-NSLS campus events through Society aligns with this aspiration.
