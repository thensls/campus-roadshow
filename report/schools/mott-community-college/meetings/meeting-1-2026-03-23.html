Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Personal relationships between advisor and members — Dawn called this "the biggest thing" and her favorite part of NSLS.
  • In-person programming — she stated she is "not a fan of 100% online programs" because they prevent relationship building.
  • The SNT structure and SMART goal conversations — she has adapted these to whole-group format and finds peer-to-peer sharing (e.g., a student sharing his time-blocking calendar) especially impactful.
  • The DISC assessment — she has built extended DISC content into Leadership Training Day and wants more depth, not less.
  • The human element of advising — she wants any platform to be an assistant, not a replacement for in-person programming.

What Isn't Working

  • Video content (Karen): Dawn and her students find it scripted and inauthentic — "an actress instead of a real person talking."▶ 5:22 Her chapter uses it selectively and supplements heavily with their own curriculum.▶ 5:30
  • Loss of chapter pillars: Dawn valued the pillars as an achievement framework and an administrative reporting tool.▶ 7:16 She misses having a formal recognition system to show how hard the chapter is working — she noted PTK's "five-star chapter" designation as a competitive comparison point.▶ 7:25
  • Advisor-side app access: She can no longer access the chapter leader view on the mobile app. She must use a laptop to check messages, which delays her responsiveness to students.
  • Messaging lag: Students send messages and wait; Dawn would prefer something closer to instant messaging — but does not want to give students her personal phone number. Tried the Band app with mixed results.
  • Completion drop-off: A large portion of students who join do nothing or only complete orientation. She has manually worked to reduce this from ~50% dropout to ~16% before her leave, but it remains a core operational challenge.
  • Manual data work: She digs through SNT reports to extract goal completion data herself. There is no automated report for this.
  • Limited DISC depth: Students and Dawn want more detailed results from the personality assessment — she has had to build supplemental content herself to fill the gap.

School Priorities

  • New administration (new president, near-full board turnover) is focused on enrollment growth and workforce development programs.
  • Retention is not yet a stated institutional priority, but Dawn believes it should be and is building a case for it to present to her boss (the AVP, formerly Athletic Director).
  • Workforce development at Mott refers to a separate, non-credit division running trades training, certificate programs, pharmacy tech, and adult re-entry programs — these students are not currently in the NSLS invitation pool.
  • Dual-enrolled and non-traditional students are significant populations at Mott, each with very different needs.
  • Institutional support for the NSLS chapter is currently limited — Dawn described it as "just me doing my job and Caitlin helping me." She believes better outcomes data could change that.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Relationship-First Philosophy at Risk from Digital Drift

Dawn's identity as an advisor is built on deep, personal, longitudinal relationships with students — she is still tracking alumni from 2015 on LinkedIn. She is enthusiastic about Society but drew a clear line: it must augment in-person programming, not replace it. Any feature that reduces human touchpoints risks eroding what she considers the core value of NSLS at Mott.

2. Data Vacuum — Advisor Is Compensating Manually

Dawn has independently built her own student tracking system because NSLS does not provide the longitudinal outcome data she needs. She tracks enrollment, completion, SNT goals, and alumni career paths herself. She explicitly said having a structured outcomes report would help her make the case to administration for more institutional support and resources. The advisor dashboard and career readiness score resonated immediately and strongly.

3. Recognition and Achievement Matter for Administrative Advocacy

Dawn cited the removal of the chapter pillars as a meaningful loss — not because she is competitive, but because the pillars gave her a visible, tangible way to show administration what the chapter was achieving. She referenced PTK's "five-star chapter" designation as a benchmark she no longer has an equivalent for. Society's career readiness score and outcomes reporting were flagged as potential replacements for that advocacy function.

4. Time Management as the Central Student Struggle

When asked what students need most, Dawn pointed to help organizing their lives and managing their time — not career content or leadership theory. She described students consistently claiming they "don't have time" while actually having significant unstructured hours. She has integrated time-blocking into SNT conversations and found peer-led demonstrations (a student sharing his calendar) more effective than advisor instruction. She described a task-reminder app students used with "cutesy" cartoon animal prompts as highly effective — directly relevant to Society's gamification and notification concepts.

5. AI-Savvy Advisor Already Using Tools — High Adoption Potential

Dawn is already actively using AI tools (ChatGPT for content summarization, NotebookLM for converting materials into podcasts and infographics) and coaching students to do the same. She described a specific example of a 47-year-old nursing student who, guided by a younger peer, converted all her assignments into audio podcasts using NotebookLM. Dawn's view: if Society's AI is genuinely useful, students will engage. She is not a skeptic — she is an early practitioner.

Section 5 — Society Reception

10/10

What Landed Well

  • Career Clarity Track: Dawn said "I would have enjoyed this" and immediately connected it to her students' need to explore career paths more deeply — particularly the nursing example of students not knowing what kind of nurse they want to be.
  • Personality Assessments (DISC, Myers-Briggs, Enneagram, Holland Code, Big Five): Dawn's reaction was enthusiastic and personal — she referenced her existing DISC programming and called the deeper assessment integration something she has always wanted ("that has always been one of our questions, like why do we not get more information").
  • Advisor Dashboard and Outcomes Data: Strong and immediate reaction — she described herself as a data person who currently has to track everything manually. Seeing a structured report and career readiness score drove her to say "I'm really excited about this."
  • Career Readiness Score: Specifically called out as something she could use to show administration that the program has measurable impact — she said "100%" when asked if it could serve that function.
  • Gamification and Notifications: She had already described a cartoon-animal task reminder app that students loved before the demo — so when Society's gamification and notification concepts were introduced, the connection was immediate and validated.
  • SNT Matching and Tracking: Her response to automated SNT meeting credit and reporting was "that would be amazing right there" — directly addressing her current manual SNT data burden.
  • AI as a Partner, Not a Replacement: The framing of AI as a tool that empowers students resonated — she echoed it back organically with her NotebookLM anecdote.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Pricing: Dawn raised the pricing question directly at the end of the meeting — asking whether the added AI capabilities would change what students or the school pay. This was not resolved in the meeting; Chris acknowledged pricing models are still being evaluated (one-time fee, modular, or subscription).
  • In-person programming preservation: She was clear that Society should not replace in-person sessions — she wants it to extend reach, not substitute for the human elements of her chapter model.
  • MVP / early-stage caution: Chris proactively noted the platform is an MVP, and Dawn acknowledged it was "a lot" to take in, though her overall reaction remained positive.

Surprises

  • Dawn's grief over losing the chapter pillars was unexpected by Chris — he said it was "not really what I expected to hear." She framed it explicitly as an administrative advocacy tool, not a vanity metric.
  • The depth of her existing DIY data infrastructure (tracking students since 2015, annual LinkedIn check-ins across hundreds of alumni) was notable — she has been doing manually what Society would automate.
  • Her existing AI fluency and coaching of students around AI tools (ChatGPT, NotebookLM) positioned her as a potential Society champion rather than a skeptic to be convinced.
  • Workforce development at Mott is a fully separate, non-credit division — NSLS currently has no reach into that population, and those students do not appear in invitation lists.

Open Questions

  • Will Society's pricing change for schools receiving AI-enhanced features? Dawn asked this directly and it was not resolved. A clear answer is needed before her follow-up meeting.
  • How will the platform handle the chapter leader / advisor mobile experience? Dawn noted the chapter leader view was removed from the mobile app — is this being restored in Society or is web-only the intended model for advisors?
  • Is there a planned replacement for the chapter achievement/pillars recognition framework? Dawn used the pillars to demonstrate chapter performance to administration. The career readiness score may partially fill this gap, but a chapter-level recognition tier has not been announced.
  • What is the timeline for replacing or supplementing the current video content (Karen)? Dawn's chapter already works around it — this is a known friction point across the advisor base and was acknowledged as in progress but with no committed timeline.
  • Could Society's career tools eventually extend to Mott's workforce development population (non-credit, certificate programs)? This population does not currently appear in NSLS invitation lists, but represents a large group of adult learners who could benefit from the Clarity Track.
  • What level of student data will advisors be able to access under FERPA constraints? Chris raised this briefly — the advisor dashboard concept is compelling to Dawn, but the privacy boundary needs to be clearly defined before rollout.
