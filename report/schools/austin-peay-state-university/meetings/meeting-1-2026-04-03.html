Section 1 — School Snapshot

Institution: Austin Peay State University (APSU), Clarksville, Tennessee — approximately one hour northwest of Nashville, adjacent to Fort Campbell, Kentucky.▶ 0:54

School Type: Public 4-Year University

Student Population: Large commuter and non-traditional student base. Approximately 33% of students are military-affiliated in some form.▶ 0:59 Several active-duty students are currently deployed.▶ 5:00 The population skews toward students balancing heavy academic loads, employment, childcare, and off-campus living.

Chapter Status: Established

Chapter Type: Hybrid (shifted to fully in-person for Spring after virtual speaker broadcasts underperformed in Fall▶ 6:34; supplemental events run alongside core induction steps)

NSLS Organizational Home: Housed within the Office of Student Life and Engagement. Advisor's formal title is focused on Leadership and Student Organizations, making NSLS a natural fit within her portfolio.

Notable Context: The chapter hosts supplemental programming beyond required induction steps, including community service events, a de-stress event around midterms, a campus cleanup, and a general board meeting open to all members. APSU students have responded strongly to in-person programming this spring. The chapter participates in homecoming annually. A Gov's Lead program also sits within the advisor's portfolio.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The step-based induction model — students value it because it differentiates NSLS from other honor societies that require only a GPA threshold with no ongoing engagement.
  • The sense of challenge and progression — students like having clear milestones to work toward ("I've got to hit this step and this step").
  • In-person programming — after a Fall experiment with virtual speaker broadcasts underperformed, the chapter shifted to fully in-person for Spring and saw significantly better attendance and student satisfaction.
  • Supplemental community events that keep inducted members engaged and visible on campus (e.g., homecoming, community service, de-stress events, general board meetings open to all members).
  • The current mobile app experience — students prefer it over the web portal for ease of navigation.

What Isn't Working

  • SNT group formation and portal language is confusing for students, especially commuters, non-traditional students, and those who are deployed.▶ 4:43 Students often do not understand what to do if they lack a ready group of NSLS peers nearby.
  • Students who procrastinate frequently hit a wall when they realize SNT meetings cannot be compressed into one week — leading to semester deferrals rather than completion.
  • Induction step volume feels overwhelming to students who are already managing heavy course loads (up to 20 credit hours), jobs, and family responsibilities, even though the advisor noted the actual time commitment is approximately 10 hours.
  • Post-induction engagement gap — students complete induction and then ask "now what?" The chapter has addressed this with supplemental programming, but there is no structural platform support for ongoing involvement.
  • Attendance tracking is entirely manual. The advisor uses two monitors simultaneously — NSLS portal on one screen and attendance records on the other — and manually enters names to assign credit. This workflow is incompatible with real-time SNT matching at in-person events.
  • Pulling progress reports requires filtering by specific date ranges, which is cumbersome. The advisor wants to compare milestone completion rates (e.g., SNT completions by March 1 year-over-year) without needing to run custom exports.
  • Basic professional readiness gaps among students — the advisor noted that teaching students how to read and reply to emails is a real and ongoing challenge, attributed in part to COVID-era disruptions to professional skill development.
  • Student org involvement data is siloed — the advisor has no way to see within NSLS tools whether a student is involved in other campus organizations, which she believes correlates with induction completion rates.

School Priorities

  • Student engagement is the overarching institutional priority — keeping students connected, active, and progressing both inside and outside of formal programs.
  • Career and workforce readiness is addressed in fragmented ways across departments (NACE competency programming through Career Success Center, leadership through Student Life, etc.) but is not integrated into a single experience.
  • The Career Success Center recently signed with Handshake through 2030, which limits near-term integration opportunities on the career tools side.
  • No formal SLO-based reporting requirements are currently tied to NSLS — the advisor operates on self-directed metrics and a competitive drive to improve year over year.
  • CliftonStrengths is institutionally embedded — the campus is a CliftonStrengths division, and the advisor noted this as a meaningful part of student development programming.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Military and Non-Traditional Students Demand Flexible Engagement Infrastructure

Approximately 33% of APSU students are military-affiliated. Several are currently deployed. Commuter and working-student populations dominate. The current SNT model — which requires scheduled group meetings with peers — creates structural barriers for these students. The advisor has developed informal workarounds (e.g., allowing deployed students to meet with peers in the field) but described the portal language as actively confusing. Society's virtual SNT support and matching algorithm were flagged as directly relevant to this problem.

2. Manual Attendance Workflows Are a Functional Blocker for Real-Time Features

The advisor's current attendance process — manually cross-referencing Ready Education exports with the NSLS portal — takes approximately 30 minutes per event and cannot be completed in real time during a leadership training day. This directly conflicts with Society's proposed SNT matching feature, which would require students to be marked as present before being matched. The advisor specifically flagged this as a workflow that would break down at scale during in-person events. Chris Higbee identified this as a broadly shared problem and flagged it for engineering follow-up.

3. Data Access Is a Personal and Operational Driver — Not Just a Reporting Requirement

The advisor is not under institutional pressure to produce outcome reports for NSLS, but she is intrinsically motivated to track progress data. She wants year-over-year comparisons, milestone completion rates by date, and student-level context (living situation, work hours, co-curricular involvement) to inform how she guides students and pushes her executive board. The Society advisor dashboard and career readiness score were well-received as potential answers to this need.

4. Students Are Already AI-Native — Society Enters a Receptive Environment

When Chris Higbee noted that students tend to be skeptical of AI, Janesa immediately pushed back: "Not here. They love it and they use it." Student workers in her office actively use ChatGPT and Gemini throughout the day, copying and pasting between tools. She framed Society's AI coach as a natural fit — students would likely treat it as another brainstorming tool, but with the added benefit of persistent record-keeping in one place. This is a meaningful positive signal for adoption.

5. Cross-Campus Involvement Data Could Unlock Deeper Student Success Insights

The advisor hypothesized — without having data to confirm — that students who complete NSLS induction tend to be involved in multiple student organizations, while students who fall off are often less embedded in campus life overall. She asked whether Society's onboarding questionnaire could capture student org membership (e.g., Alpha Lambda Delta, ODK) alongside NSLS participation. She sees this as a way to identify at-risk students earlier and personalize support. This surfaced as a novel product idea not previously raised in the presentation.

Section 5 — Society Reception

8/10

Self-reported. Janesa noted her 8 was not negative — she is genuinely excited, particularly about data capabilities — but tempered by realistic concern about integration complexity and implementation growing pains, given that APSU has recently completed two full platform implementations.

What Landed Well

  • The Renee story resonated — the idea that a student who never engaged with leadership content spent 70+ minutes on Career Clarity was compelling evidence of meet-them-where-they-are design.
  • The Career Clarity track and AI coach concept were well-received. The advisor immediately connected them to her students' existing AI habits and saw Society as a natural extension of tools they already use.
  • The advisor dashboard and ability to filter and export student progress data over time directly addressed a pain point she described unprompted — wanting easier access to milestone completion data for year-over-year comparison.
  • The SNT matching algorithm was exciting to her — particularly the idea of running it at the end of leadership training day to assign groups in real time.
  • CliftonStrengths integration potential was noted with enthusiasm. The advisor confirmed competition is in her top five and responded positively when Chris mentioned active talks with Gallup.
  • The career readiness score concept aligned with her self-described competitive drive to benchmark and improve student outcomes over time.
  • The framing of Society as an upgrade to NSLS — not a replacement — was reassuring.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • App experience: The advisor asked directly whether Society would be a separate app or integrated into the existing NSLS app. She expressed concern that a second downloadable app could create friction for students who already prefer and use the current app. Chris confirmed they will initially be separate, with partial accessibility from the existing app, and full migration planned years out.
  • Attendance integration: The SNT matching feature as demoed would require students to be credited for attendance before matching can occur. The advisor's current manual process makes this infeasible in real time. She raised this as a concrete blocker during the demo — not a hypothetical concern.
  • Career Success Center overlap: The center recently signed with Handshake through 2030. The advisor was uncertain what a Society career tools layer would mean for that relationship and how the two would coexist.
  • Implementation complexity: The advisor referenced APSU having recently gone through two full platform implementations and acknowledged that growing pains are inevitable with any new tool — she was realistic, not dismissive, but it was a noted hesitation.

Surprises

  • Students at APSU are enthusiastic AI adopters — not skeptical. This is a meaningful divergence from the assumption built into the demo framing and should be noted for future roadshow positioning.
  • The advisor proposed an entirely unprompted feature: the ability to collect student org membership data during onboarding to help identify whether co-curricular involvement correlates with induction completion. This is a novel signal worth tracking across the roadshow.
  • The advisor's hypothesis — that students who fall off the induction path tend to be less involved in campus life overall — offers a potential early-warning model that Society's data layer could help validate at scale.
  • Despite the lack of formal institutional pressure to report outcomes, the advisor is one of the most data-oriented advisors encountered — entirely self-motivated by competitive benchmarking.
