Meeting Highlights Full Meeting Report ›

✅ What's Working / What Landed
  • 🤝SNTs and Accountability Groups ▶ 24:01 — Cole explicitly said this must never be removed. He has observed measurable peer connection gains from success networking teams, especially following Leadership Training Day, and credits SNTs as a major differentiator for students in a compressed two-to-three semester timeline.
  • 📱SNT Automation in Society ▶ 26:22 — Cole responded positively to the automated SNT matching and in-app chat/video meeting functionality, noting it would replace his current manual process of connecting students by email and give them a one-stop resource.
  • 🧭Career Clarity Track for Trades Students ▶ 21:51 — Cole saw immediate relevance of the Clarity Track for trades students who struggle to connect NSLS to certification pathways like ag mechanics or diesel tech, calling it a feature he could use as a selling point with that population.
❌ What Isn't Working
  • 📺Leadership Broadcasts — No Traction ▶ 5:39 — Cole identified this as the number one ongoing hurdle. Despite reserving spaces and offering rebroadcast options, he rarely gets student attendance. The only time a student showed up was because they had an unrelated question for him. He cannot measure the impact of broadcasts relative to other program steps.
  • ⏱️Short Enrollment Window Limits Engagement ▶ 4:45 — With most students completing programs in roughly three semesters or less, sustained engagement is structurally difficult. Cole noted that more than 70% of students are below full-time status, and many treat involvement as optional given their compressed timelines.
  • 🏫Institutional Narrative Crowds Out Involvement ▶ 34:41 — Cole expressed concern that the school's new focus on retention, degree completion, and workforce outcomes is making it harder to defend involvement and leadership programs internally. He is actively trying to reframe NSLS as essential rather than supplemental.
⚠️ Key Signal▶ 21:51 — Trades Student Gap Is the Unlocked Use Case: Cole repeatedly returned to the challenge of making NSLS relevant for trades students in auto tech, diesel tech, and ag mechanics — populations that do not naturally see leadership development as applicable to their career paths. He saw the Clarity Track as the first feature that could credibly bridge that gap, particularly around financial planning and certification pathways. This is a distinctive product-market signal from a technical college context that is unlikely to surface in four-year university meetings and represents a high-value positioning opportunity for Society at community and technical colleges.
