Section 3 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Two-Year and Trades Students Require Purpose-Built Positioning

Madison College's student body is meaningfully different from a four-year university chapter. A growing share of students are in trades programs (diesel, auto tech, agricultural mechanics) with certification-based outcomes rather than degree-transfer pathways. Cole expressed genuine enthusiasm for Society's potential to address this gap — particularly the AI career coach's ability to handle non-traditional career paths — but made clear this is an open question that needs to be worked through. Product rollout materials and the Clarity Track framing may need vocational-specific examples to land effectively here.

2. Leadership Broadcasts Are Effectively Non-Functional at This School

Cole was direct that leadership broadcasts — live or rebroadcast — produce almost no student attendance and that he cannot measure their impact. This is a structural issue tied to school hours and student schedules, not a content quality problem. Society's move toward on-demand, asynchronous, interactive micro-content is well-positioned to address this, but the broadcast format as a program requirement appears to be a systemic friction point worth tracking across other two-year schools on the roadshow.

3. Career Readiness Data Is the Missing Proof Point

Cole explicitly named career readiness data as the thing he wishes he had. He described fielding frequent student questions about how NSLS applies to their post-Madison path, and noted that having quantifiable readiness data would "cut out a lot of that thinking work and more be able to put into action." This aligns directly with Society's career readiness score feature and positions it as potentially high-value for advisor-level storytelling and institutional reporting.

4. Involvement Is Under Institutional Pressure — And Needs a New Justification

The college's current strategic direction prioritizes speed-to-degree, which creates structural pressure on co-curricular programs. Cole described involvement as "kind of left out" of the dominant institutional narrative. He is actively working to reframe involvement as integral to student success — not supplemental — and sees Society's career readiness angle as a potential tool to make that case. This is a meaningful strategic signal: NSLS needs to position itself as a retention and workforce readiness asset, not just an engagement program.

5. Cross-Departmental Integration Is Cole's Instinctive Next Step

After seeing the Society demo, Cole's immediate instinct was to think about how to integrate the AI career coach "homogeneously" between student life and career services. He also referenced plans to build a credit-bearing workforce skills class and saw Society as something that could complement or feed into that work. This suggests that at Madison, the highest-value use case for Society may be as a bridge between student affairs and academic/career services — not just a standalone NSLS member tool.
