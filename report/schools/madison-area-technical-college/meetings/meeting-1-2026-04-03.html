Section 1 — School Snapshot

School: Madison Area Technical College (Madison College), Madison, WI

School Type: Technical College · Public 2-Year

Chapter Status: Established — NSLS has been present at Madison College since approximately 2016–2017.▶ 6:41

Chapter Type: The chapter operates in a hybrid-adjacent environment. The advisor describes offering both reserved in-person spaces and rebroadcast options for leadership broadcasts, with most engagement happening in-person around events like Leadership Training Day.

Advisors Present: One advisor (Cole) participated. CSR Gregg Gudelinis was also on the call.

Notable Context: Madison College is a two-year technical college with a student body that skews non-traditional — over 70% of students are below full-time status. The school serves a diverse mix of university-transfer students and trades program students (auto tech, diesel tech, ag mechanics). The institution is in active transition: a new president has introduced a focus on retention, degree completion, and workforce readiness.▶ 6:59 A 300-employer survey has shaped a set of eight or nine core workforce skills now central to institutional priorities.▶ 6:59 The NSLS chapter competes for student attention alongside Phi Theta Kappa, the dominant honor society on campus.▶ 6:41 Cole notes that roughly 120 students hold paid student involvement positions and approximately a quarter have gone through the NSLS program. Cole's primary institutional role is with Student Government, and he is also working to develop a credit-bearing workforce skills course.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Success Networking Teams (SNTs) and accountability group structures — Cole stated these "100% have to stay" and described them as producing the greatest peer connection outcomes▶ 4:26, especially in a compressed two-year timeline.▶ 4:36
  • Team-building activities during orientation and Leadership Training Day — Cole noted visible improvement in peer connection following these experiences.▶ 5:02
  • The ability for students to seek out the advisor directly — Cole highlighted that NSLS alumni among his 120 paid involvement students regularly come to him with questions, which he attributes to the program.

What Isn't Working

  • Leadership broadcasts have very low attendance. Cole offered reserved spaces and rebroadcast options but gets almost no response.▶ 5:42 He said the only student who showed up did so because they had an unrelated question for him.▶ 5:44
  • Student drop-off after sign-up is a persistent problem. Students enroll, pay the membership fee, and then disengage — similar to the Renee example Chris described.
  • The current program does not translate clearly for trades students (auto tech, diesel tech, ag mechanics). Cole regularly struggles to explain how FOL applies to a student who wants to be an agricultural mechanic or earn a specific certification.
  • SNT coordination is currently handled manually via email, which Cole described as inefficient and dependent on students figuring out logistics themselves.▶ 5:02
  • The "what's in it for me" question is increasingly the dominant mindset among students, and the current program framing does not always answer it quickly enough for students with limited time at the college.

School Priorities

  • Workforce readiness is now the central institutional focus, shaped by a 300-employer survey identifying eight or nine core workforce skills for graduates.
  • Retention, degree completion, and success after completion are the new president's stated priorities.
  • A credit-bearing course around core workforce skills is in active development — Cole is leading this effort.
  • More than 70% of students are below full-time status; Pell Grant full-time requirements are creating new pressure around enrollment and programming relevance.
  • Student involvement is at risk of being de-prioritized as the institutional message shifts toward an efficient, structured degree-completion path — Cole is trying to ensure involvement and leadership are framed as integral, not supplemental, to that path.
  • The school enjoys rare bipartisan political support, somewhat insulating it from broader federal funding pressure, though Cole acknowledges workforce alignment is still a growing expectation.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. The Two-Year Clock Creates Urgency and Constraints

Cole repeatedly returned to the compressed timeline at Madison College — roughly three semesters of real engagement opportunity. This shapes everything: how he sells the program, which students stay engaged, and what outcomes are realistic. He contrasted this explicitly with his prior experience at UW-Whitewater where a four-year runway allowed for deeper programming. Any platform or feature that doesn't respect this compressed window will struggle to gain traction here.

2. Trades Students Are an Underserved and Growing Constituency

Cole noted that more auto tech, diesel tech, and trades students are completing the NSLS program, yet the current framing and content doesn't map cleanly onto their goals. He struggles to explain how FOL connects to earning a certification or becoming an ag mechanic. He expressed genuine excitement about the Clarity Track's potential to bridge this gap — particularly around certifications and financial planning tied to specific career paths — and described this as a meaningful selling point he currently lacks.

3. Career Readiness Data Is the Missing Justification Layer

When asked what data he wishes he had, Cole immediately named career readiness metrics. He described regularly fielding the question "how does this benefit me when I'm done here?" and having to answer it manually and individually. He also referenced students asking whether their FOL progress would transfer to a four-year school. Career readiness data would give him a concrete, institutional-level answer to these questions and help him advocate for NSLS in conversations with his VP of Student Affairs and the President's Office.

4. SNT Infrastructure Needs to Modernize

Cole described the current SNT setup process as manual and email-dependent, with students left to sort out logistics on their own after being connected. He responded strongly to the automated matching, in-app chat, and video meeting features shown in the demo — calling it "huge" and noting it would consolidate what is currently a fragmented, labor-intensive process into a single touchpoint at the end of Leadership Training Day.

5. Involvement Is Fighting for Legitimacy Within a Degree-Completion Culture

Cole articulated a structural tension: the institution's new messaging is focused on efficient degree completion, which risks marginalizing involvement programs. He is actively trying to reframe NSLS — and involvement more broadly — as integral to that success pathway rather than optional enrichment. He sees Society's career-readiness framing as a potential tool for making that argument more compellingly to institutional leadership, career services, and students themselves.

Section 5 — Society Reception

7/10

What Landed Well

  • The Clarity Track concept resonated strongly, particularly the ability for students to explore career direction on their own terms and timeline without requiring in-person attendance at an event.
  • The AI career coach's supplemental, guided role — Cole specifically noted it "works in tandem" with existing programming rather than replacing it, which he said is important given his unique school context.
  • The potential for the Clarity Track to serve trades students and help them map certifications and financial planning to specific career goals — Cole described this as a gap the current program does not fill.
  • Automated SNT matching and the integrated chat/video meeting functionality — Cole called this "huge" and said it directly addresses a manual coordination burden he currently carries.
  • The advisor engagement dashboard and career readiness score — Cole immediately connected this to the institutional story he needs to tell to his VP and President's Office.
  • The platform's flexibility across online, hybrid, and in-person environments was noted positively given Madison College's hybrid student population.
  • The idea that induction could potentially be tied to completing the Clarity Track was mentioned by Chris and received without objection — Cole appeared to see the logic in broadening the entry point.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Cole was candid that it will take two to three semesters to determine whether Society can improve follow-through at Madison College, given current drop-off patterns.
  • He expressed uncertainty about how well the platform will serve trades-track students specifically, and said he needs to drill down into this before he can fully commit or promote it.
  • He wants to think through how to position the AI career coach as a bridge between student life and career services — he flagged this as a conversation he wants to have with Gregg before moving forward.
  • He noted that because NSLS is not the dominant honor society on campus (Phi Theta Kappa holds that role), the platform will need to demonstrate clear, differentiated value to gain broader institutional buy-in.

Surprises

  • Cole stated the platform was "more than what I was expecting" — indicating that prior expectations were modest and Society exceeded them in scope.
  • Cole's framing of trades students as an active and growing segment of his NSLS membership was notable — this is a student population not typically centered in NSLS conversations, and it surfaced organically.
  • Cole is already developing a credit-bearing workforce skills course, which represents an organic institutional alignment opportunity that NSLS was not previously aware of or engaged with.
