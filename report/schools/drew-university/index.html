Meeting Highlights Full Meeting Report ›

✅ What's Working / What Landed
  • 💌Physical Invitation Letter ▶ 7:05 — Kim cited the mailed invitation as a key driver of higher-than-expected induction numbers — students were surprised and excited to receive actual mail, which felt special and differentiated.
  • 🎙️Speaker Broadcasts ▶ 6:51 — Both advisors independently called out speaker broadcasts as a highlight. They appreciated the inspirational content and used clips selectively in their in-person facilitation.
  • 🏆Leadership Development Focus ▶ 5:24 — Both advisors cited their shared passion for leadership development as a core reason they value NSLS. Kim emphasized that no leadership infrastructure existed at Drew before her arrival.
❌ What Isn't Working
  • 📧Multi-Email Identity Problem ▶ 10:44 — Students can register with any email address — NSLS, personal, or school — making it extremely difficult to match students to Drew's Handshake system and maintain clean cohort records. Advisors with 130 students had to manually track down missing school emails and maintain a separate spreadsheet.
  • 📊No Major or Demographic Reporting ▶ 11:00 — The current NSLS platform cannot tell advisors how many students in a cohort are marketing majors, what year they are, their ethnicity, or other breakdowns faculty request. This makes ROI reporting and grant accountability difficult.
  • 👥SNT Tracking and Group Visibility ▶ 13:05 — Advisors cannot see which students are in which SNT groups or whether groups have actually met. Kim and Cassandra built their own color-coded paper system and maintain a separate numbered spreadsheet to manage groups. Reports exist but do not confirm actual meetings occurred.
  • 🎬Video-Only Facilitation Format ▶ 15:29 — The existing LTD content was designed for virtual/asynchronous use and is not suitable for live in-person sessions. Advisors created their own facilitation guide and used only short clips. They recommended NSLS develop a formal in-person facilitation deck.
⚠️ Key Signal▶ 53:56 — Anti-AI Student Segment Is a Real Adoption Risk — and a Potential Test Case: Cassandra flagged that a vocal group of Drew students actively opposes AI on ethical and environmental grounds — they wrote a formal letter to admissions objecting to AI-generated Instagram captions. She noted this was on her mind throughout the Society demo and raised it unprompted as a barrier to student adoption. Chris acknowledged this is a known pattern across campuses, particularly among creative and independent-minded students, and suggested Drew's student body could serve as a valuable test case for messaging and framing AI as a personal tool rather than a replacement for human effort. Cassandra indicated she could reach out to skeptical students directly to gather perspective as part of the pilot.
