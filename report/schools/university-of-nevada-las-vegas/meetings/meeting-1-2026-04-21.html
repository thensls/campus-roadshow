Section 1 — School Snapshot

School: University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV)

Location: Las Vegas, NV

School Type: Research University · Public 4-Year

Chapter Status: Established

Chapter Type: Standard (in-person with online participation)

Segment / Tier: Tier 3

Advisors Present: Dr. Elana Paladino (Faculty Advisor) and LaDonnia Petersen (Staff/Board Advisor, Lee Business School)

Notable Context: UNLV is a commuter-heavy campus, which advisors identified as a persistent challenge for student engagement and SNT participation. The university is actively pursuing Carnegie Community Engagement Classification, placing increased institutional emphasis on service learning and community partnerships. The Lee Business School is piloting workforce readiness tools including an AI resume tool (VMOC). The school is broadly prioritizing micro-credentials, badging, and AI-integrated skill-based training. Advisors noted that SNT attendance has been disappointing and that a restructure of the format is already under consideration. Most NSLS members skew first- and second-year, many of whom have limited work experience and do not yet self-identify as leaders.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The SMART goal-setting framework — both advisors identified this as the core of what makes NSLS valuable and said it should never be lost.▶ 4:05
  • National team support in reviewing student-submitted goals and work — Dr. Paladino said this is something she could not replicate on her own and deeply appreciates.▶ 7:37
  • The inspirational quality of NSLS video content — LaDonnia noted the motivational segments "really get the teams fired up" and are "stitched together very well."▶ 10:11
  • High-profile speaker credibility (e.g., Barack Obama with the NSLS crest) — cited as adding legitimacy to the program.
  • Leadership Training Day and national Summit events — Dr. Paladino sees these as valuable networking experiences and wants more in-person attendance from members.

What Isn't Working

  • SNT attendance is low and disappointing — advisors are already considering restructuring the format.▶ 6:51
  • The commuter campus model makes sustained on-campus engagement difficult — this is a known, persistent structural challenge, not a new one.
  • Video content feels dated and inflexible — LaDonnia noted it "doesn't seem to have been updated to work for us instead of us working for it."▶ 10:05 Dr. Paladino noted that long, unbroken videos don't hold engagement well during events.▶ 9:00
  • The "why" of NSLS is unclear to students — particularly first- and second-year students who don't identify as leaders and have no workforce context to anchor the value proposition.
  • Leadership as a standalone value proposition is hard to defend — students are skeptical of the value of college broadly and want demonstrable, practical outcomes, not just a stole and cord.▶ 26:49
  • Existing career tools at the school are poorly designed — LaDonnia called out VMOC (the Lee Business School's AI resume tool) as ineffective for students with real work history, using templates geared only toward traditional undergraduates.▶ 42:12
  • Workforce readiness resources are siloed by school/college — students outside the Business School may not have access to the same career preparation tools or awareness.

School Priorities

  • Carnegie Community Engagement Classification — UNLV is working to maintain this designation, which requires faculty-led service learning and community-partnered curriculum. Dr. Paladino is personally developing a service learning course in support of this.
  • Workforce readiness and skill-based training — the university is actively exploring micro-credentials, digital badging, and AI-integrated learning modules to prepare students for the labor market.
  • Community and employer partnerships — advisors are actively recruiting external partners for the chapter; LaDonnia has been leading those outreach efforts.
  • Student engagement on a commuter campus — keeping students connected and progressing when they are not consistently on campus is an ongoing institutional challenge.
  • AI integration across campus — UNLV is leaning into AI as a tool for student support, though existing implementations (like VMOC) have not fully delivered.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Students Need a "Why" Before Leadership Can Land

Both advisors independently surfaced the same core problem: students — especially undeclared first-years with no work experience — don't see themselves as leaders▶ 17:59 and can't connect NSLS's leadership framing to anything personally meaningful. LaDonnia articulated this sharply: "If they're undeclared in their major...what do I need to know about leadership right now?" Chris's reframe — that career clarity may be a more compelling entry point than leadership identity — resonated strongly with both advisors and aligned directly with the Renee story.

2. Service Learning Is an Institutional Mandate, Not Just a Nice-to-Have

UNLV's Carnegie Classification pursuit means service learning is a top-down institutional priority, not just an advisor preference. Dr. Paladino is personally building a service learning course. This creates a direct alignment opportunity: embedding service-oriented SNTs or community partnership tracks into Society could help the chapter tie NSLS participation to formal institutional goals — a significant lever for budget justification and cross-departmental buy-in.

3. Commuter Culture Demands Flexibility and Asynchronous Options

UNLV's commuter-heavy student body was cited multiple times as a structural barrier to engagement. Students are not reliably on campus, which limits synchronous programming like SNTs. The Society platform's ability to support asynchronous self-paced tracks, mobile-first SNT setup, and online meeting/chat tools within the platform was seen as directly responsive to this challenge — not a feature, but a necessity for this campus profile.

4. Career Readiness Data Is a Competitive Institutional Asset

Dr. Paladino flagged micro-credentials, badging, and workforce readiness as active school priorities. Chris's mention of Wall Street Journal and U.S. News rankings incorporating career readiness metrics landed as a meaningful institutional argument. The career readiness score concept — with longitudinal cohort tracking — was framed as something that could help UNLV demonstrate outcomes in budget conversations, accreditation discussions, and enrollment marketing. This reframes Society from a student tool to an institutional reporting asset.

5. Existing Career Tools Are Failing — and Advisors Know It

LaDonnia offered an unprompted critique of VMOC, the Lee Business School's AI resume tool, citing its inability to serve students with real work experience as a concrete failure. This signals both an unmet need Society could address and a heightened standard of scrutiny: LaDonnia will evaluate Society's career tools with a discerning, experienced eye. Her question about whether the career readiness score adapts to a student's chosen track shows she is already thinking about edge cases and personalization depth.

Section 5 — Society Reception

9/10

LaDonnia: 10/10. Dr. Paladino: 8–9/10 (held back by long-term cost and institutional sustainability concerns, not product skepticism).

What Landed Well

  • The Renee story — the narrative of a non-traditional student who ignored NSLS but spent 70 minutes deeply engaged in the Clarity Track resonated immediately and was received as a credible proof point, not a sales pitch.
  • The Clarity Track concept — LaDonnia immediately connected it to the Working Genius assessment framework she was personally studying, showing strong conceptual alignment. She called it "a really good starting point" and said, "I feel like we were taking them from point A to G."
  • The AI coach's ability to tailor to a student's school — the prospect of the agent knowing UNLV's course catalog, departments, and staff and directing students internally (rather than to University of Virginia) was described as exactly what LaDonnia wanted to know was possible. She had been writing this as a question before Chris addressed it.
  • The SNT scheduling and online meeting tool — Dr. Paladino said it would be "really valuable in facilitating that and making sure people follow through," connecting it directly to their current SNT attendance problem.
  • The visual rebrand — LaDonnia offered spontaneous positive feedback: "The colors, the entire topography, it feels very fresh and modern... it looks more visually appealing to the demographic that we're targeting."
  • The advisor dashboard and career readiness score — the ability to see a student's readiness score before a meeting was received as practically useful, with Chris's framing of freeing advisors from low-value tasks (like resume cleanup) resonating well.
  • Personality assessments (Myers-Briggs, DISC, Big Five, etc.) embedded in the profile — directly validated by LaDonnia's interest in the Working Genius framework.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Long-term pricing — Dr. Paladino's primary hesitation. She is already thinking about whether the university will fund this as it scales and whether it will be sustainable. Pricing flexibility was appreciated but not fully reassuring without specifics.
  • Career readiness score logic — LaDonnia asked whether the score differs based on the student's chosen track, and offered a concrete example (a student with 10 years of workforce experience vs. a traditional undergrad) to illustrate why a one-size score would be inadequate. Chris acknowledged this was not yet resolved.
  • Induction eligibility via alternative tracks — LaDonnia asked directly whether completing the Clarity Track (instead of Foundations of Leadership) could count toward induction. Chris confirmed this is the long-term vision but is 12+ months away. The question signals advisors are already planning around this use case.
  • Integration timeline with existing NSLS dashboard — LaDonnia asked whether Society would replace the current student dashboard. Chris confirmed yes, over approximately two years. The multi-phase rollout was understood but advisors will need clarity on what runs in parallel during the transition.

Surprises

  • LaDonnia rated Society a 10/10 without hesitation — the depth and specificity of her engagement throughout (Working Genius comparison, VMOC critique, score logic question, induction eligibility question) made her enthusiasm feel fully informed rather than reflexive.
  • The visual rebrand was called out spontaneously and positively — this was not prompted and suggests the design work is connecting with the target demographic as intended.
  • Dr. Paladino had difficulty identifying what "needs to change" on their end — she acknowledged the national team's support has been genuinely helpful and that there are no major pain points screaming at her. This is a positive signal for the relationship, though it may mean product feedback will come more from LaDonnia than Dr. Paladino.
