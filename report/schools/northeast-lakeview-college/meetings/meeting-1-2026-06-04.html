Location: Universal City, TX

School Type: Public 2-Year — part of the Alamo Colleges District, a multi-campus system in which each college operates its own independent NSLS chapter. Students may take classes across campuses but designate one home campus.

Chapter Status: Established

Chapter Type: Not explicitly stated; context suggests a hybrid/in-person model given references to in-person orientation, Leadership Training Day, and national online events.

Advisors Present: Dr. Jennifer Quintero (faculty advisor, biology instructor). Her student life counterpart was invited but did not attend; Dr. Quintero noted he would receive the recording.

Section 2 — Advisor Profiles

Dr. Jennifer Quintero

Role: Faculty Advisor, NSLS at Northeast Lakeview College; Biology Instructor; works in coordination with student life (counterpart not present).

Background: Biology faculty member at NLC who became involved with NSLS because she observed that students who engage outside the classroom show higher persistence and academic success. She is also involved in at least one other honor society with a higher GPA threshold (referenced as a point of comparison). She has remained a sustained NSLS advisor, suggesting multi-year involvement.

Motivations: Driven by student persistence and retention outcomes rather than programmatic tradition. She values the NSLS GPA/invitation threshold (NLC uses 2.75) because she sees it as an incentive for academic improvement. She views NSLS primarily as an engagement mechanism that keeps students connected to campus and to each other — particularly when they are struggling. She is explicitly interested in how AI tools can model appropriate, professional use of AI for students, framing it as a learning opportunity beyond just career tools.

Communication Style: Direct and question-forward. Asked multiple clarifying questions during the demo — about whether Society replaces in-person events, how in-person induction steps integrate with the platform, when and how pilot access would begin, and whether chapters decide independently to join. Comfortable pushing back or probing rather than offering surface-level agreement. Described her enthusiasm as an 8/10, noting there is still more to learn — a measured, evidence-based orientation.

Pilot Partner / Product Council Potential: High. Dr. Quintero asked explicitly how and when NLC could start using Society, signaled institutional alignment (admin investment in engagement/retention), and confirmed that each Alamo chapter decides independently — implying she is prepared to make that call at NLC without waiting for other colleges. Her critical but constructive engagement style makes her a strong Product Council candidate capable of providing specific, actionable feedback.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Scholarship/financial benefit as a recruiting tool: Dr. Quintero explicitly named the scholarship/money component as a key recruitment technique that should be retained. ("That's something that we use as a recruiting technique, for sure.▶ 17:07")
  • Invitation-based model with GPA expectations: Dr. Quintero defended the selective threshold (NLC uses 2.75 GPA), arguing it incentivizes academic improvement▶ 19:53 and that "having certain expectations" produces better outcomes. She contrasted this with her other, higher-threshold society involvement to ground her perspective.
  • Student momentum and sense of accomplishment: While stated by Dr. Roberts (SPC), Dr. Quintero's remarks align — she values the program's ability to keep students invested and returning to campus even when struggling, underscoring the importance of not disrupting the motivational arc of the experience.

What Isn't Working

  • Advisor bandwidth and time constraints: Dr. Quintero directly stated that NLC struggles with "not having enough time to do all the things that NSLS needs."▶ 49:14 This is a recurring operational pain point that limits program depth and consistency.
  • SNT drop-off: Dr. Quintero did not name this explicitly, but she engaged strongly with Chris's disclosure that ~50% of NSLS fall-off happens at the SNT stage▶ 39:29, suggesting it resonates with NLC's experience. She highlighted that flexibility in scheduling (including national events) is critical to keeping part-time students engaged.
  • Scheduling access for part-time and dual-credit students: NLC has a large population of part-time and credit students whose scheduling constraints make it hard to participate in fixed in-person programming. Dr. Quintero cited student emails asking about missed events and alternative engagement options as evidence of unmet demand.
  • Unclear integration path between platform and in-person events: Dr. Quintero's questions about how Society intersects with orientation and Leadership Training Day suggest that the current model's in-person/digital boundary is not well-defined for advisors. This is both a pain point and a product clarity gap.

School Priorities

  • Student engagement outside the classroom as a retention strategy: NLC administration is actively focused on getting students to engage beyond class time, with the evidence-based belief that such engagement increases persistence and academic success. This is the primary institutional reason Dr. Quintero remains involved with NSLS.
  • Accessibility and scheduling flexibility: The addition of national events has been well-received at NLC because it addresses the reality of a largely part-time student body. More asynchronous and flexible options are aligned with campus priorities.
  • Appropriate AI literacy and professional use: Dr. Quintero raised unprompted the value of modeling good AI engagement for students — framing Society's AI coach as a teaching tool for professional AI use, not just a product feature. This reflects an emerging campus-level priority around AI readiness.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Retention Through Engagement Is the Core Value Proposition at NLC

Dr. Quintero's involvement in NSLS is entirely grounded in observed student outcomes — specifically that students who connect outside the classroom show higher persistence. This is not a soft preference; it reflects an institutional priority that comes from NLC's administration. Society's engagement and career clarity features land most powerfully when framed through this retention lens rather than as standalone leadership programming.

2. Advisor Bandwidth Is a Real Constraint That Society Must Actively Relieve

Dr. Quintero stated directly that NLC doesn't have enough time to do all the things NSLS requires. Her enthusiasm for the AI coach was explicitly tied to the idea of having an "assistant" — not just for students, but to reduce the load on advisors. Any pilot onboarding plan must account for this constraint and demonstrate time savings, not just feature richness.

3. Part-Time and Dual-Credit Students Require Flexible, Asynchronous Access

Dr. Quintero highlighted the large part-time and credit student population at NLC multiple times. National events have already proven valuable because they accommodate unpredictable schedules. Society's asynchronous tracks and SNT matching with flexible scheduling windows directly address this structural challenge and should be emphasized in NLC-specific rollout messaging.

4. The Invitation/GPA Model Is an Asset at NLC — Openness Messaging Needs Care

While other Alamo advisors (notably NVC) raised concerns about the selective model limiting access, Dr. Quintero explicitly defended the 2.75 GPA threshold as motivationally valuable. She sees the standard as an incentive for students to improve. NSLS and Society messaging around expanded access or "openness" should be handled carefully at NLC to avoid undermining the program's perceived prestige and incentive structure.

5. AI as a Professional Learning Tool, Not Just a Feature

Dr. Quintero raised — without prompting — the idea that Society's AI coach could teach students how to engage with AI appropriately in a professional context.▶ 49:21 This signals that NLC (and potentially the broader Alamo system) is developing an AI literacy agenda, and that Society could be positioned as a practical on-ramp for that. This is a differentiated, campus-level use case worth exploring in product council conversations.

Section 5 — Society Reception

8/10

Score provided directly by Dr. Quintero during the group polling exercise at the close of the demo.

What Landed Well

  • AI coach as advisor support: Dr. Quintero responded positively and immediately to the idea of the AI coach as a bandwidth multiplier. She explicitly said it would be "very helpful" given NLC's time constraints▶ 49:14 with NSLS administration.
  • SNT matching automation: The disclosure that ~50% of NSLS drop-off occurs around SNTs resonated. Dr. Quintero engaged with the matching feature and added that cross-campus SNT matching would be especially valuable at Alamo Colleges▶ 39:29, where students already take classes across multiple campuses.
  • Scheduling flexibility and asynchronous access: Society's ability to serve students who can't attend in-person events directly addressed NLC's part-time student challenge. Dr. Quintero connected this to existing evidence that national events have increased engagement.
  • Campus-aware AI agent (on roadmap): The concept of an AI agent trained on the school's course catalog and staff directory — able to recommend specific courses and contacts — was received as directly useful. Dr. Quintero's comments about advisor overload make this feature especially relevant to NLC.
  • AI literacy framing: Dr. Quintero's unprompted observation that the AI coach could model appropriate professional AI use suggests Society's AI layer resonated as educationally meaningful, not just functionally convenient.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Relationship between Society and in-person induction events: Dr. Quintero asked twice — and with increasing specificity — how Society integrates with in-person orientation and Leadership Training Day. She wanted to understand whether the platform runs in parallel to those events, replaces any steps, or is layered in afterward. This suggests the current messaging around hybrid use is not yet clear enough for advisors planning their fall programming.
  • Induction gate on Clarity Track access: Dr. Quintero questioned whether requiring induction completion to unlock Society would conflict with using the platform during the induction process itself. Chris clarified that some content (Insights Track) could be used during orientation, but the Clarity Track unlocks post-induction. Dr. Quintero acknowledged this ("Okay, okay") but the back-and-forth indicates advisors need a clearer visual or written flow of the student journey.
  • Independent chapter decision-making: Dr. Quintero raised whether all Alamo chapters must decide together or individually to join the pilot. This was not a negative signal — she was clearly thinking through how NLC could move independently — but it reflects that the pilot structure and decision process had not been pre-communicated clearly to individual advisors.
  • Remaining uncertainty ("still a lot to learn"): Dr. Quintero's 8/10 score was accompanied by a note that there is still more to learn. This is not resistance but a signal that she needs hands-on access to the platform before committing fully.

Surprises

  • Dr. Quintero independently reframed Society's AI coach as a vehicle for teaching students professional AI use — a use case not introduced by the presenter. This suggests a latent campus priority around AI literacy that Society is not yet explicitly marketing to.
  • Dr. Quintero's defense of the GPA threshold stood out in a meeting where another Alamo advisor (NVC) questioned it. Her perspective — grounded in observed student behavior rather than policy preference — offers a strong counterpoint that could inform how NSLS communicates the value of the invitation model to different campus audiences.
  • Dr. Quintero asked about the cross-campus SNT matching unprompted in the context of how Alamo students already move between campuses. This is a concrete, systems-level insight about the Alamo student population that could directly shape how the SNT matching roadmap is scoped for multi-college districts.
