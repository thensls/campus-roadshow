Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Multiple participation modes: weekly in-person meetings, weekly virtual meetings, and self-paced — essential for serving CSN's wide demographic (ages 16 to 60+).
  • Community building and cohesion, especially the sense of connection students develop through SNTs and shared goal-setting.
  • Access to the full speaker broadcast library, including older archived sessions (e.g., John Maxwell, Barack Obama). Charlene noted it was harder to surface older recordings and wants them easily accessible to students for credit or review.
  • The recognition and credibility of the NSLS brand and induction ceremony as a meaningful milestone for students.
  • The chapter's strong cross-pollination with student government and the in-house student leadership academy.

What Isn't Working

  • Technology reliability: BigBlueButton failures, video loading issues, and app-based SNT reports not transmitting to advisors — causing students to fall off pace without realizing it.
  • The seven-day minimum between SNT reports creates a structural bottleneck when the induction timeline is compressed. Approximately 10–12 students nearly missed induction this spring due to this constraint combined with a delayed chapter start.
  • Online and self-paced SNT groups lack the depth of connection seen in in-person SNTs. Attempts to group online students by goal similarity became unmanageable; random grouping reduced connection quality.
  • Self-paced students frequently complete SNTs with close friends or family rather than NSLS peers, diluting the experience.
  • Students who group manually online and get removed from their SNT groups face friction re-entering, which advisors found too cumbersome to resolve consistently.
  • Engagement drop-off between the first and second NSLS meetings — students come in excited but lose momentum, and advisors are unsure at what point perception shifts.
  • Contract renewal delays caused by Charlene's new department chair responsibilities created a compressed spring timeline that nearly derailed the induction cohort.

School Priorities

  • A new president is actively reshaping institutional priorities and a new strategic plan is being developed; CSN's co-chair of that process is a close colleague of Charlene's.
  • Addressing the looming enrollment cliff and maintaining competitive edge against UNLV and Nevada State University.
  • Expanding access to career-readiness and vocational pathways, including leveraging new Pell Grant eligibility for vocational programs.
  • Pushing for in-person student engagement across classrooms and organizations — both Charlene and Jane are explicit advocates for this despite transportation and cost barriers in Las Vegas.
  • Expanding CSN's physical footprint: a potential fourth campus (Northwest Las Vegas) is in early planning stages, and Nevada State University is building a second campus near CSN's North Las Vegas location.
  • Positioning NSLS as a web of involvement that follows students from CSN through their transfer and beyond — particularly to UNLV and Nevada State.
  • Nevada's biennial legislature (next session February–June 2027) creates a budget planning cycle that the institution must prepare for now.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Technology Reliability Is a Trust Issue

Jane was direct: "The technology is holding us back." Specific failures — BigBlueButton outages, SNT reports vanishing from the app, video loading problems — have eroded advisor and student confidence in the platform. Any new technology introduced via Society must be demonstrably stable before it can serve as a carrot or engagement driver. Reliability is table stakes.

2. SNT Quality Degrades Online and at Scale

The SNT experience is the chapter's highest-value touchpoint in person but its weakest in virtual and self-paced formats. Advisors have tried manual goal-based grouping (too unwieldy), random sorting (less connection), and watched self-paced students default to friends and family. Automated, intelligent SNT matching — with the ability to hold meetings inside the platform, record sessions, and generate transcripts — was the single most enthusiastically received feature shown in the demo.

3. Campus-Aware AI Is a Buy-In Accelerator

Charlene explicitly stated: "If you get it to the place where CSN comes up as a top thing and is talking about courses at the college, you're definitely going to have a lot of buy-in from me." Both advisors see the AI agent's ability to surface CSN-specific resources — courses, advisors, career services — as the feature most likely to convert Society from a general tool into an indispensable campus resource. This is also the feature most likely to open doors with career services and faculty beyond NSLS.

4. Pricing Sensitivity Is a Real Barrier — Especially for Community College Students

Christian raised a pointed concern: students are already paying $95 to join NSLS▶ 5:22, and adding any subscription cost on top risks losing participation. Jane asked probing questions about whether pricing would be bundled into membership or offered as a school-level subscription. Charlene flagged that while free access is welcome now, she needs to understand the long-term cost structure to plan. CSN's student population faces real financial pressure (inflation, high gas prices, commuter demographics), making any incremental cost politically sensitive.

5. NSLS as a Transfer-Ready Network — Not Just a Campus Program

Jane articulated a vision for NSLS as a "web of involvement" that follows students from CSN to UNLV to Nevada State and beyond. She is actively pitching NSLS to Nevada State University and coordinates with UNLV's student government. The chapter's strong cross-pollination with student government and leadership academy already demonstrates this network effect. Society's career clarity and professional networking features were seen as tools that could extend NSLS's value proposition across the full student arc, not just the induction window.

Section 5 — Society Reception

8/10

All four attendees independently rated Society an 8/10. Charlene stated she would move to a 9–9.5 once the campus-aware agent feature (surfacing CSN courses and advisors) is live. Jose noted the SNT video meeting feature was the most compelling element and that a solid 8 was contingent on that feature being completed.

What Landed Well

  • The SNT automated matching and in-platform video meeting tool — described by Jose as "very helpful" and cited by Jane as the feature she is most excited about.
  • The Renee story (68-year-old non-traditional student who spent 70 minutes in the Career Clarity Track) resonated immediately given CSN's similarly diverse age range, including a recently graduated chapter president in her 60s.
  • The AI coach's role as a guide rather than a generator — Charlene was visibly reassured when Chris clarified the coach reviews and coaches on resumes rather than writing them, which addressed her concern about employer guidance against AI-written applications.
  • The Career Readiness Score concept — framed as a tool that frees advisors to focus on high-value human interactions by handling baseline coaching through AI.
  • The promise of the campus-aware agent (surfacing CSN-specific courses, staff, and career services) — Charlene called this a clear buy-in trigger.
  • Christian's live public speaking goal-setting exercise using the AI coach made the feature tangible and personally relevant for the student attendees.
  • Society being free for the pilot period was well-received as a low-risk entry point.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Pricing structure post-pilot: Jane asked whether cost would be bundled into membership fees or billed as school subscriptions. Christian raised concern that any add-on cost on top of the $95 membership would reduce student uptake.
  • AI resume writing: Charlene flagged that Google and other major employers have explicitly told students not to use AI to write application materials. She needed reassurance — which Chris provided — that the coach coaches rather than generates.
  • Charlene asked directly whether completing the Clarity Track could ever lead to induction credit, and whether there would be monetary rebates for chapters that drive engagement through Society. Chris confirmed these are under consideration but not yet defined.
  • Handshake integration: Jane noted CSN is a top Handshake user and career services is fully built around it. Lack of current integration is a gap, though Chris confirmed preliminary discussions with Handshake are underway.
  • Christian asked about what additional skills tracks beyond Foundations of Leadership and Clarity would look like — Chris acknowledged these are not yet fully defined.
  • Charlene asked whether Society runs parallel to or sequential with Foundations of Leadership. Chris recommended encouraging students to complete induction first, then access Clarity as a post-induction benefit.

Surprises

  • Christian, a student attendee, opened the meeting by saying he had "been waiting so long for this kind of thing to happen with NSLS" — signaling that students themselves have been sensing a gap between NSLS's potential and its current platform experience.
  • Charlene's most direct enthusiasm trigger was not leadership or career clarity broadly, but the specific campus-aware AI agent feature — more so than any other capability shown.
  • Jane volunteered that CSN is actively trying to pitch NSLS to Nevada State University, indicating advisors are already acting as informal NSLS evangelists to neighboring institutions without being asked.
