Section 1 — School Snapshot

School: Southern Utah University (SUU)

Location: Cedar City, UT

School Type: Public 4-Year

Chapter Status: Established — approximately one semester of active relationship with current advisor

Chapter Type: Standard (primarily in-person delivery with occasional online accommodations for individual students)

Advisor Present: Brandon Street, Director of Career Development

CSR: Gregg Gudelinis

Notable Context: SUU has undergone a significant institutional shift toward career services as a strategic priority, investing heavily in career coaching infrastructure over the last one to two years. Brandon arrived approximately six months ago and has quickly become an engaged chapter partner, actively supporting Gregg in other regional efforts (e.g., helping locate a Utah State advisor). The chapter experienced a student president departure but Brandon moved proactively to address it. SUU uses Canvas and Handshake. The university has a homegrown career readiness program called T-Bird Ready built around eight competencies, skills articulation, and a merit badge/medallion system. The school's stated institutional goal is to become a career flagship in Utah.

Section 2 — Advisor Profiles

Brandon Street

Role: Director of Career Development, Southern Utah University

Background: Career services professional who has been advocating for a stronger career-readiness focus at SUU for approximately five years. Joined as NSLS chapter advisor roughly six months prior to this meeting. His wife is currently enrolled in SUU's master's in education program. He is deeply embedded in career ecosystem frameworks and has been instrumental in designing and implementing the T-Bird Ready program — a structured, evidence-based competency badging system tied to the SAR articulation method. SUU has recently hired dedicated career coaches for each college on campus as part of its expanded career services investment.

Motivations: Brandon is motivated by student outcomes — specifically gainful employment and graduate school admission. He is driven by the belief that students must not only develop skills but learn to articulate them clearly to employers. He sees NSLS as a meaningful partner when it demonstrates intentional, structured progression and real-world value. He is focused on closing the skill articulation gap and building SUU's reputation as a career-focused institution in Utah.

Communication Style: Direct, thoughtful, and collaborative. Comfortable raising concerns (e.g., the upsell friction) without being confrontational. Asks clarifying, practical questions during demos (e.g., whether SNT SMART goals flow into the achievement engine). Engages analytically, mapping what he sees against frameworks he already uses (career design, planning, and campaign phases). Tends to qualify enthusiasm with a desire to explore more deeply before making broad institutional claims.

Pilot Partner / Product Council Potential: High. Brandon gave the platform a 10/10 enthusiasm score and explicitly expressed willingness to participate as a pilot partner. His background in structured career development, his T-Bird Ready program alignment with Society's career readiness score concept, and his institutional authority in career services make him a strong candidate for both early piloting and the Product Council. He noted he would need more hands-on time with the system before assessing broader institutional impact.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The intentionality of NSLS in moving students forward through structured leadership development — Brandon noted students recognize this as distinct from other honor societies.
  • The emerging career readiness dimension of NSLS, which Brandon said he appreciates as a Director of Career Development and aligns with the school's institutional direction.
  • In-person delivery of Foundations of Leadership — the chapter runs orientations, kickoffs, and Leadership Training Day in person, with rare online accommodations for individual students. Brandon values this format for connection and engagement.

What Isn't Working

  • Upselling within the NSLS experience has damaged student trust and perceived legitimacy. Brandon specifically cited the for-credit course offering — SUU does not honor those credits, so students who pay feel misled, and those relationships are difficult to repair.
  • Lack of clarity for students about what they signed up for — some students feel slighted when expectations don't match the reality of their membership experience.
  • Student drop-off at the SNT stage — Brandon confirmed this is a known problem, consistent with NSLS-wide data (40–50% drop-off). He acknowledged students struggle to find value in that component.
  • Data collection is still relatively dated — SUU does not yet have a reliable way to track career learning outcomes or demonstrate skills progression over time.

School Priorities

  • Career services has been elevated to a core institutional priority at SUU within the last one to two years, driven by national enrollment concerns and student ROI expectations around college degrees.
  • SUU has hired a dedicated career coach for each college on campus and is building a structured, three-phase career development model: career design, career planning, and career campaign.
  • The T-Bird Ready program operationalizes eight competency areas through evidence submission, SAR-based articulation, badging, and a graduation medallion — currently live and showing early results (students wore medallions at graduation).
  • Institutional goal is to become a career flagship in Utah by demonstrating measurable career learning outcomes and placement data.
  • The school is moving toward capturing outcome data — specifically whether students achieve gainful employment or graduate school admission — and needs tools to show in-progress career learning development, not just end results.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Career Articulation Is the Real Skills Gap

Brandon reframed the employer-student skills gap as substantially a skill articulation problem — students don't always know they've learned something, and when they do, they can't explain it clearly. His T-Bird Ready program and SAR method are direct responses to this. Society's AI coach and career readiness score concept map closely to this concern and were received as genuinely useful tools for addressing it.

2. Structured Progression Matters — Students Get Lost in Content-Rich Systems

Brandon raised an unprompted concern that systems with rich content can overwhelm students who don't have a clear, guided path. He framed this as a known failure mode of career platforms. His question — whether Society drives students through a formal, sequential process — was the most substantive product question he asked. Chris's explanation of prerequisite-gated tracks and AI guardrails addressed this directly and visibly satisfied Brandon.

3. Upsell Friction Is a Legitimacy Threat

The upselling perception — particularly around for-credit courses that SUU does not honor — has caused concrete trust damage with students and reflects a broader legitimacy concern Brandon has about NSLS. This is a known issue (Chris noted at least half of the ~30 schools visited have raised it), and it directly undermines advisor credibility on campus. Messaging clarity and student expectation-setting were flagged as needed improvements.

4. Institutional Alignment Is Strong — Society Fits SUU's Strategic Direction

SUU's pivot to career services as a flagship priority, its three-phase career development model, and the T-Bird Ready program all align closely with Society's structure and value proposition. Brandon noted the platform aligns with what students are looking for — visible progress across both leadership and career success — from day one of membership. The career readiness score concept was specifically called out as addressing a data gap the school is actively trying to close.

5. Broader Platform Potential Beyond NSLS Members

Brandon responded positively to Chris's suggestion that Society could eventually serve all SUU students through the career center — not just NSLS members. He connected this to the groups/networking feature as a mechanism for alumni re-engagement and long-term value. While he noted he'd need more time to assess full institutional fit, he acknowledged the potential for a campus-wide deployment through career services.

Section 5 — Society Reception

10/10

What Landed Well

  • The Clarity track and the overall career direction framework — Brandon mapped it directly onto his own three-phase career development model (design, planning, campaign) and saw all three phases represented.
  • The AI coach concept — particularly the ability to personalize guidance based on personality assessments (MBTI, DISC, etc.) and push students toward skill and career articulation.
  • The SNT matching and huddle tool — Brandon called it a beautiful expansion of students' networks beyond campus and said it addresses one of the biggest drop-off points in NSLS engagement.
  • The career readiness score and advisor dashboard — he directly confirmed this addresses a data gap SUU is actively trying to close. The ability to see where a student like Maya Rivera is struggling before a coaching appointment was described as genuinely useful.
  • The achievements engine and SMART goal integration — Brandon asked specifically whether SNT goals flow into achievements, suggesting he sees this as a valuable accountability mechanism.
  • The framing of Society as helping students see they are doing both leadership and success from day one — Brandon said this was "probably the piece that's been missing."

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Content overwhelm risk — Brandon raised a concern about students getting lost in rich content without a clear guided path before Chris explained the prerequisite-gated track structure.
  • SNT SMART goals auto-populating into the achievements engine — currently requires a manual step (student must ask the coach); Brandon noted this is an important workflow. Chris acknowledged it's on the roadmap.
  • Broader institutional impact beyond the NSLS chapter — Brandon said he would need hands-on time with the system before he could speak to how Society would fit alongside SUU's existing and planned career infrastructure.
  • Other career coaches at SUU are not part of NSLS — Brandon noted Society's direct benefit to him as an advisor is clear, but the pathway for non-NSLS career coaches to leverage the tool is less defined.

Surprises

  • Brandon's T-Bird Ready program is a notably sophisticated, independently developed system that mirrors several Society concepts (competency badging, skill articulation, evidence submission, SAR-based reflection). This represents a strong existing framework that Society could complement or integrate with — Chris flagged this explicitly as a potential feature development opportunity.
  • Brandon asked about the SNT goal-to-achievements workflow unprompted, indicating he was mentally stress-testing the system's coherence in real time — a sign of genuine engagement rather than surface-level enthusiasm.
  • Brandon's 10/10 score was given directly and without qualification — unusually strong given his otherwise measured and analytical communication style throughout the call.
