Section 1 — School Snapshot

School Type: Private 4-Year (Career College / University)

Location: Fort Lauderdale, FL (Graduate School & eCampus)

Culture: Keiser is a career-focused institution that deliberately omits general education electives in favor of career-aligned coursework. The school serves a predominantly non-traditional student population — average student age is mid-30s to 40s▶ 4:54 — consisting of working adults, parents, and career changers. Programs are offered across ground, hybrid, and online modalities. Keiser has a significant and growing international presence with campuses in Nicaragua, Shanghai, Indonesia, Spain, Taiwan, Ecuador, and Bolivia.

Chapter Status: Established

Chapter Type: Online / Hybrid

Advisors Present: Heather Cordner (Director, Student Services & Career Services)

Notable Context: Keiser operates both an undergraduate NSLS chapter and a graduate (master's/doctoral) NSLS chapter▶ 43:39. The advisor oversees NSLS across all campuses. There is active discussion about launching an alumni NSLS chapter targeting Keiser graduates from approximately the last two to five years who were not previously offered membership. The institution is actively pursuing AI integration as a strategic institutional priority. Career services and student services are combined under one department. SNT coordination is currently student-led due to time zone and scheduling complexity across a distributed online student body.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Asynchronous flexibility — students in their 30s and 40s who are working and parenting need to move through programming on their own time without required live meeting attendance.
  • The ability for students to build their resumes and credentials through NSLS membership — the "leveling up" value proposition.
  • The SNT structure, even though matching is currently student-managed; Heather sees value in the peer group model and noted students do use NSLS social pages to self-organize.
  • National events — Heather confirmed students do participate in national NSLS events, noting it surprised her given difficulty getting them to attend local live events.

What Isn't Working

  • Campus-level knowledge gaps: Heather oversees all campuses but reports that not all campuses are making themselves knowledgeable about NSLS▶ 6:48, creating a burden when managing large student lists (5,000+ students sent to Maryanna) and fielding resulting questions.
  • SNT matching is entirely student-managed due to time zone and scheduling complexity▶ 36:06 — no systematic process in place; occasional manual matching done ad hoc.
  • The existing career center platform is not AI-powered and described as feeling like "19th/early 20th century"▶ 42:26 — lacking analytics and unable to keep up with industry-specific resume trends.
  • Heather prefers the mobile app dashboard over the web dashboard, citing clarity of progress tracking▶ 6:52 — noted the app feels cleaner.
  • No data infrastructure to prove NSLS ROI to students who are skeptical about whether membership is worth it, particularly at the graduate and doctoral level.

School Priorities

  • Career readiness is the institutional core — Keiser defines itself as a career college and prioritizes getting students job-ready▶ 9:10 over broad general education requirements.
  • AI integration is an active institutional strategic priority▶ 8:22 — Keiser has been in meetings with Anthropic and Perplexity▶ 34:52 and is looking to leverage AI across the institution.
  • International and multilingual expansion — Keiser now has campuses in multiple countries and has added languages to support a growing international student body.
  • Demonstrating ROI to students — particularly for graduate and doctoral students who question what NSLS membership adds to their experience.
  • Alumni engagement — active interest in launching an alumni NSLS chapter for recent graduates (within the last two to five years) who did not have access to membership as students.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Non-Traditional Student Population Requires Asynchronous-First Design

With an average student age in the mid-30s to 40s and a student body of working adults, parents, and career changers, Keiser's population cannot participate in synchronous programming reliably. Heather emphasized that asynchronous flexibility is the single most important feature for student engagement▶ 4:11. Society's mobile-first, self-paced design directly addresses this need. The Renee story — a 68-year-old non-traditional student who had never engaged with NSLS until Society's Clarity Track — resonated immediately as a recognizable student profile at Keiser.

2. Career Readiness Data Is the Missing ROI Proof Point

Heather's biggest operational pain point is the absence of meaningful career readiness analytics. Her current career center tool lacks AI capabilities and modern analytics. She explicitly called out the Career Readiness Score as something she found valuable — "I like anything where I can get analytics."▶ 44:20 She sees this score as a tool to demonstrate NSLS ROI to skeptical graduate and doctoral students, support budget conversations, and reduce departmental burden by enabling more targeted advising interactions.

3. AI Integration Is a Strategic Institutional Priority — Not Just a Feature Request

Heather confirmed that AI integration is one of the two biggest institutional changes at Keiser in recent years. She noted that Keiser has already been in meetings with Anthropic and Perplexity. She asked pointed technical questions about Society's AI infrastructure — hosting (AWS), LLM (Claude/Anthropic), and whether the product is proprietary. The fact that Society is built on Claude and hosted on AWS, and that Keiser already has an Anthropic relationship, is a meaningful alignment signal.

4. SNT Matching Is a Known Friction Point With High Drop-Off Risk

Heather confirmed that SNT matching is entirely student-managed at Keiser due to time zone and scheduling complexity. She occasionally facilitates manual matches but has no systematic process. This aligns with the platform-wide signal that approximately 50% of student drop-off happens at the SNT matching stage. Society's automated matching tool — with built-in scheduling, online meeting capability, attendance tracking, and AI-analyzed transcripts — was received positively. Heather also asked whether cross-institutional matching would be possible, signaling interest in broader networking for students.

5. Alumni Expansion Is an Active Opportunity — Not a Future Hypothetical

Heather and Maryanna were already in conversation about launching an alumni NSLS chapter before this meeting. Heather confirmed interest in rolling Society out to both current NSLS members and to alumni who were never offered membership. She asked directly about the pilot's scope for alumni, the pricing model post-pilot, and how the subscription structure would work long-term. This represents a meaningful expansion opportunity — Clarity Track as a standalone alumni career coaching tool, potentially with a subscription model.

Section 5 — Society Reception

9/10

What Landed Well

  • AI coaching integration — Heather called it out directly: "I like the AI integration and the coaching."
  • Personality assessments (Myers-Briggs, Enneagram, Holland Code, DISC, Big Five) — described as one of the standout features: "especially the access to the five big personalities."
  • Career Readiness Score and advisor dashboard — "I like anything where I can get analytics." She noted this would be immediately useful for advising conversations and reporting up the chain.
  • Clarity Track framing for undergraduate students — "I think what it puts in the students' hands is very, very clear, especially for our undergraduate students."▶ 41:44
  • Renee's story resonated — Keiser has many students who match that profile; the asynchronous, self-paced, career-focused engagement arc is directly relevant.
  • Mobile-first design philosophy — confirmed preference for the existing NSLS app over the web dashboard; Society's mobile-first approach directly aligns.
  • Department burden reduction — "It would definitely ease the burden on our department so we can better serve our students."▶ 43:03
  • Pilot being free — confirmed as an important factor given current career center limitations and cost constraints.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • FERPA compliance — first substantive question asked after the demo; wanted to understand how student data is protected and who controls access permissions.
  • Whether Society is a proprietary product or third-party — asked directly: "Is this your own product or are you using a company to provide you the product?" Reassured by the answer that it is fully in-house.
  • Hosting and LLM infrastructure — asked specifically about AWS hosting and the AI agent powering the platform; positively received the Claude/Anthropic answer given Keiser's existing Anthropic relationship.
  • Pilot period duration — asked what the pilot period is; noted that the timeline has not yet been finalized (estimated six months to a year).
  • Post-pilot pricing — asked whether Clarity will carry a fee after the pilot; confirmed it will move to a subscription model, which Heather acknowledged and accepted.
  • National events handling in Society — Maryanna raised this and Chris acknowledged it is still being worked out; no final answer provided, noted as a follow-up item.
  • How Society access is communicated to students — asked whether a notification goes out like other NSLS communications; clarified that it is already enabled but outreach needs to be coordinated.

Surprises

  • Heather had already started her own Society profile during or immediately after the meeting — unprompted, she mentioned it at the close: "I already started the profile, so I'm going to play with that today."
  • Keiser already has an active relationship with Anthropic and Perplexity — this was not anticipated and represents a strong institutional AI alignment with Society's underlying LLM choice.
  • Heather confirmed pilot partnership verbally during the call without requiring a follow-up decision meeting — she said "No, I'm fine telling you to go ahead and launch it" and "Let's do it then."
  • Heather expressed interest in the Product Council proactively before being formally invited — she asked about it directly while it was still on-screen and confirmed interest in participating in the future.
