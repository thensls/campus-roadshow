Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Flexibility of programming — Dan emphasized that flexible participation options (both in-person and asynchronous) are essential for Arapahoe CC's student population▶ 6:37, many of whom cannot attend every program element in person.
  • NSLS as a gateway to broader campus involvement — Dan values that NSLS reaches students who would not otherwise engage on campus and connects them to additional opportunities.
  • Centralized platform management — Dan appreciates having a single platform through which students and advisors can manage the member experience▶ 7:18, even while acknowledging its current limitations.

What Isn't Working

  • Cross-semester data visibility — Dan cannot easily compare induction rates or engagement metrics across semesters or years within the platform▶ 8:52. Data resets before semesters even conclude, forcing him to maintain parallel spreadsheets▶ 9:00.
  • Low engagement and completion rates — Dan expressed long-standing concern that more than half of paying members do not complete the program, and that this has not felt like a national-level priority. He framed it as a values issue: students are paying and not receiving proportional value.
  • Platform does not actively drive engagement — Dan noted that he does significant work to encourage students to engage with Foundations of Leadership content and reach induction, but the platform itself does not reinforce that engagement in tandem with his efforts▶ 15:58.
  • Student-facing complexity — Dan raised concern that adding new tracks (Insights, Clarity, Connections) on top of existing Foundations of Leadership content could overwhelm students▶ 20:13 if not introduced clearly and digestibly.

School Priorities

  • Serving a non-traditional, flexible-needs student population — Arapahoe CC students require program structures that accommodate varied schedules and participation modes.
  • Improving member completion and induction rates — This is Dan's clearest stated priority; he wants the platform itself to share responsibility for driving students through the program▶ 7:00.
  • Reducing manual administrative work — Dan wants multi-semester analytics and data comparisons handled within the platform rather than through external spreadsheets▶ 9:00.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Platform Must Share Accountability for Engagement

Dan stated directly that he does a great deal to push students toward Foundations of Leadership content and induction, but the platform does not work in tandem with those efforts. He wants the technology to actively encourage engagement — not passively display it. His comment in the chat ("If the tech platform is also encouraging that engagement in tandem with me, that would elevate our engagement even more") signals a clear product need for proactive, AI-driven nudges or prompts built into the student experience.

2. Multi-Semester Analytics Are a Critical Gap

Dan described a specific and frustrating workflow: induction rate data disappears before the semester even ends, and there is no way to compare metrics across terms or years within the platform. He compensates with personal spreadsheets. This is a named, recurring pain point that Dan has also submitted through NSLS survey feedback, indicating it is not a passing complaint but an entrenched operational issue.

3. Non-Completion Rates Are a Values and Trust Issue

Dan framed the ~50% non-completion rate not just as a program challenge but as an ethical concern — students are paying for membership and not receiving value. He noted that Society's additional benefit layers (Clarity, Insights, Connections) address this concern by giving non-completing students something meaningful to engage with. He expressed that this shift makes the membership feel more defensible and worthwhile.

4. Flexibility Is Non-Negotiable for a 2-Year Population

Dan's opening contribution emphasized that Arapahoe CC students benefit from NSLS precisely because they are not required to attend every element in person. Any evolution of the platform must preserve and deepen flexible access — both for students who engage in-person and those who need fully asynchronous options. This aligns with Society's stated multi-modality goals but requires deliberate execution.

5. Student-Facing Onboarding Clarity Is a Launch Risk

Dan raised a specific concern about how Society's expanded track structure will be introduced to students. He acknowledged that all of the new content is valuable, but noted that explaining multiple tracks on top of existing Foundations of Leadership requirements could overwhelm students if not handled carefully. He signaled confidence that NSLS will solve this but identified it as the primary open question affecting his enthusiasm score (9 rather than 10).

Section 5 — Society Reception

9/10

Dan scored Society 9/10 overall, clarifying that content alone would be a 10. The single point withheld reflects uncertainty about student-facing onboarding and how the expanded platform complexity will be communicated to students.

What Landed Well

  • The Career Clarity track and AI coach — Dan responded positively to the idea that students who don't connect with leadership content will now have an alternative pathway that still delivers meaningful value for their membership fee.
  • Society's framing as a solution to low completion rates — Dan explicitly said that adding benefit layers "takes away" his long-standing concern about students paying without engaging. The broader value proposition resonated directly with his stated frustrations.
  • The promise of multi-semester analytics in the new admin dashboard — Dan had already raised cross-semester data comparison as a pain point, so the dashboard feature addressing this was a direct hit on a named need.
  • The AI coach as a force multiplier for advisors — The concept of a platform-level engagement driver that works alongside (not instead of) the advisor aligned with Dan's stated desire for the tech to support his efforts in real time.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Student-facing complexity — Dan's primary concern was how the full suite of tracks (Insights, Clarity, Leadership, Connections) will be introduced to students in a way that is clear and digestible, especially given that Foundations of Leadership is already a multi-step commitment.
  • Rollout pacing and translation — Dan was curious about how Society's features would be sequenced for students and how advisors would be supported in communicating the new structure to their chapters.

Surprises

  • Dan's candor about feeling that national-level urgency around engagement had historically been lacking was a notable moment. He framed Society's direction as a positive shift but was clear that this concern has been present for years — suggesting that trust will need to be built through demonstrated follow-through, not just product vision.
  • Dan confirmed he had already submitted the cross-semester analytics concern via a prior NSLS survey, indicating he is an active feedback contributor whose documented input should be cross-referenced in the product backlog.

Open Questions

  • How will Society's multiple tracks (Insights, Clarity, Leadership, Connections) be sequenced and introduced to students in a way that does not feel overwhelming, particularly for community college populations managing competing priorities?
  • What is the specific rollout plan for pilot partners — will advisors like Dan begin with a small student cohort, and what support will NSLS provide during that phase?

  • Is Career Clarity being considered as an accredited curriculum pathway to induction? Dan's concern about non-completing members paying without value would be substantially addressed if Clarity could serve as an alternate induction route.
