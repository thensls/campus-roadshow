School & Chapter Snapshot

About Central Wyoming College
  • 🏔️Central Wyoming College is a rural public 2-year institution in Riverton, WY, situated near the Wind River Range with the Wind River Reservation in its backyard, shaping a student population that is heavily commuter-based, part-time, and connected to tribal communities. ▶ 17:23
  • 🔧CWC operates a robust Career Technical and Entrepreneurship division covering welding, cosmetology, culinary, automotive, medical assistant, and more, alongside two Bachelor of Applied Science degrees with specialty tracks including Tribal Leadership, Agricultural Management, and Outdoor Education Management. ▶ 21:46
  • 💼A Recompete Grant supports on-the-job training and certifications for low- or underemployed adults aged 25–55, while a collaborative grant with Wind River Development Fund focuses on CNA, culinary, and trail-building certifications for tribal members and local workforce needs. ▶ 16:39
Chapter Status
  • 🌐Established chapter that transitioned to a fully online model this academic year; 30 new members joined this semester — the strongest recruitment since COVID — but induction completion rates have not improved despite the shift online.

Meeting Highlights Full Meeting Report ›

✅ What's Working / What Landed
  • 🤝Cross-program peer connections ▶ 7:58 — Lisa identified cross-degree peer interactions as one of the most valued aspects of NSLS — students meeting others they wouldn't normally encounter. This was cited as something the online transition has weakened and must be preserved.
  • 🎯Career clarity demo resonated strongly ▶ 41:43 — Lisa responded enthusiastically to the Society career clarity track demo, immediately connecting it to students who need realistic expectations about salaries, cost of living, and career pathways — especially those who may not want to engage in traditional extracurriculars.
  • 📊Data for grants and accreditation storytelling ▶ 45:48 — Lisa validated the value of a career readiness score and longitudinal reporting, noting it could support grant writing, state-mandated outcomes, and marketing — especially as career services grant funding is ending.
❌ What Isn't Working
  • 📉Induction completion rate unchanged after going online ▶ 10:00 — Lisa expected the online chapter transition to make it easier for students to complete induction steps on their own schedule, but completion rates have remained flat. Scheduling mismatches and commuter lifestyles continue to be barriers.
  • 💸Members paying fees but not participating ▶ 54:45 — Lisa repeatedly flagged the frustration of students paying $95 in membership fees and then not engaging at all — despite outreach from her and other members. She wants data to understand why this happens.
  • 🔗Online SNTs losing cross-program connection value ▶ 7:58 — Because online SNTs now allow students to use their existing friend groups, the stretch of connecting with members from different programs is lost. New members are not getting the peer discovery experience that prior members valued most.
  • 📹Existing video content length and quality ▶ 48:25 — Though Lisa did not raise this herself, Chris confirmed it is a consistent theme across school visits — long-form video content leaves something to be desired and students need shorter, more modular formats.
⚠️ Key Signal▶ 17:55 — Rural Community College with CTE + Tribal Pipeline Is an Untapped Society Use Case: CWC's combination of a CTE-heavy curriculum, tribal community ties, Recompete Grant workforce programming, and a commuter student population that resists traditional extracurricular engagement makes it a distinctive and high-value pilot context. Lisa proactively connected with the CTE Dean to explore introducing NSLS through advisory council meetings in the fall, signaling institutional appetite beyond student services. Society's career clarity track was immediately seen as a tool that could reach students who join NSLS but never engage with leadership programming — and as a complement to grant-funded workforce initiatives that are losing funding.
