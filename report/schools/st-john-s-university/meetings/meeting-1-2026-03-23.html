Section 1 — School Snapshot

School: St. John's University — Queens, NY

School Type: Private 4-Year University

Chapter Status: Established — Natalie Maio has been the sole advisor for the NSLS chapter at St. John's for approximately 13–14 years.

Chapter Type: Live Online (hybrid model offered; virtual attendance dominates in practice)

Advisors Present: Natalie Maio (Director-level, Career Services & Leadership Development); Maryanna Laferriere (CSR, NSLS)

Notable Context: St. John's is currently navigating a university-wide strategic reorganization under a new presidential plan. This includes a 5–10% workforce reduction across faculty, staff, and administrators planned for May. Career Services — Natalie's department — is explicitly named in the strategic plan as a priority area tied to student retention, graduation outcomes, and career readiness. The university's data analyst has recently requested 6–7 years of NSLS induction data to analyze the relationship between leadership program participation and student retention. Natalie manually maintains her own tracking data because the existing NSLS dashboard does not reflect full academic-year views. The chapter consistently inducts 300–500 students per year.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The earned-membership model — students must complete steps to be inducted, not just meet a GPA threshold. Natalie described this as something she "genuinely loved about it in the beginning."▶ 5:58
  • All existing steps toward induction: orientation, Leadership Training Day, SNTs, and speaker broadcast. No issues with the program structure itself.
  • The change that moved the first SNT meeting to the end of Leadership Training Day — Natalie praised this as "a really great change."▶ 6:56
  • The live online chapter format — described as "fantastic" because it accommodates students who are commuting, working, or at internships▶ 7:02 and cannot reliably attend in person.
  • The accredited FOL curriculum — emphasized that the curriculum should not change, only be modernized in delivery.

What Isn't Working

  • Dashboard shows semester-only data: The current dashboard only reflects students who registered in the current semester. Natalie has 322 students who completed induction steps this academic year, but the dashboard shows only 30–40.▶ 9:05 She cannot view full academic-year statistics without manual workarounds.
  • Attendance data mismatch: Dashboard attendance figures only count students who registered in the current semester. Students who joined in prior semesters but still attend events are not counted, causing significant discrepancies between dashboard numbers and Natalie's own records.
  • No year-over-year member progress view: There is no easy way to see how students from prior cohorts are progressing through the program over time. Pulling member progress requires many manual exports.
  • Email system behaves like Gmail threading: Deleting a reply in a threaded conversation also deletes the original sent email. Natalie now saves every sent email as a Word document on her computer as a workaround. Notification emails arrive in her St. John's inbox but the original message is often already in trash when she goes to look for it in the dashboard.
  • Leadership Training Day video — end sequence: The video's transition to SNTs doesn't work for live online chapters. The presenter instructs students to discuss questions, return to breakout rooms, and re-enter — which is not possible with randomly assigned Zoom breakout rooms. Natalie recommended the end of the video simply display discussion questions and transition cleanly into SNT time.
  • No program evaluation data shared with advisor: Natalie has been the only advisor since the chapter's founding and has never received program evaluation data from NSLS, even if surveys were sent to students. She has no visibility into what students report learning or gaining from the program.
  • Manual data tracking: Because the platform doesn't support academic-year views, Natalie maintains all her own data externally. She described it as doing "everything manually."

School Priorities

  • Student retention is the primary institutional focus under the new strategic plan — leadership and co-curricular program data is being evaluated for its contribution to keeping students enrolled.
  • Career outcomes and graduation rates are central to the university's strategic direction. Career Services is explicitly named in the strategic plan as a priority area.
  • The university's data analyst has requested 6–7 years of NSLS induction records to analyze retention correlations with leadership program participation.
  • The university is undergoing a 5–10% workforce reduction in May; departments must demonstrate their contribution to retention and outcomes to remain funded and staffed.
  • Natalie needs reportable, shareable outcome data she can surface to university leadership — data currently requires significant manual effort to compile.
  • Students predominantly prefer virtual or hybrid engagement. In-person events are offered but attendance is low in practice. Students are often attending programs from their cars, during lunch breaks, or between work commitments.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Outcomes Data Is Now an Institutional Survival Issue

St. John's is in active restructuring. Natalie's department is protected in part because it's tied to outcomes in the strategic plan — but she needs data to prove impact. She is already being asked for years of NSLS induction records to demonstrate retention correlations. The Society admin dashboard and career readiness score landed directly on this need. Natalie explicitly said that if NSLS can provide this data, "I don't have to do anything additional on my end."

2. The Dashboard Doesn't Reflect How Advisors Actually Work

Natalie's single most persistent pain point — raised multiple times — is that the dashboard operates on a semester view while she operates on an academic-year view. She has 322 students who completed induction steps this year, but the dashboard shows a fraction of that. She has compensated by building and maintaining her own external data systems. This is a significant time cost and a trust issue with the platform's data accuracy.

3. Student Confusion Is a Predictable, Pre-emptable Problem

Natalie raised a detailed, experience-based concern about the "tracks" language in Society — predicting that students will interpret numbered tracks as a sequential requirement, even when told otherwise. She drew on real examples: students who won't register for Leadership Training Day because they haven't done orientation yet, students who email asking if it's a fraud, parents who enroll their kids but never engage. She recommended considering "paths" over "tracks" and noted this concern was not unique to Society — it's a recurring issue with any NSLS communication. This is a high-signal UX and onboarding risk that she said has already come up in other roadshow conversations.

4. Virtual-First Is the Reality, Not a Compromise

Natalie was clear that while students say they want in-person options, actual attendance data tells a different story. Students attend programs from their cars, on lunch breaks at internships, and between classes. The live online chapter format has been essential to maintaining participation. Any platform changes need to reinforce — not undercut — that flexibility. She also noted that this generation of students ("COVID students") has normalized remote engagement in ways that are unlikely to reverse.

5. Advisors Need Feedback Loops That Currently Don't Exist

Natalie has never received student program evaluation data from NSLS despite being the sole advisor for 13+ years. She runs her own evaluations for institutional programs and explicitly described how that data helps her connect students to additional resources. She requested a structured, timely end-of-program evaluation — sent in April, not June — and suggested a cohesive report be shared with advisors. She framed this both as a program improvement tool and a way to generate outcome narratives she can use internally at St. John's.

Section 5 — Society Reception

8/10

What Landed Well

  • The career readiness score concept resonated immediately — Natalie connected it directly to the outcome reporting she is already being asked to provide to university administration.
  • The new administrator dashboard with year-over-year filtering addressed her most chronic pain point. She said having access to that data would mean "I don't have to do anything additional on my end."
  • The AI-powered career clarity track was understood and appreciated, particularly the framing of AI as a thought partner that helps students rather than replaces their own thinking.
  • SNT matching within the platform — the concept of students entering availability during Leadership Training Day and being matched in real time — landed well. Natalie immediately understood how it would eliminate the breakout room drop-off problem.
  • The comparison to Handshake was made by Natalie herself, suggesting she sees Society's advisor dashboard as a natural fit with tools she already uses and values.
  • The confirmation that FOL curriculum would not change — only the delivery and platform — was important reassurance for her.
  • Hearing that membership pricing would not increase (and might even be reduced with new models) was a meaningful detail she probed for.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • "Tracks" language: Natalie raised this concern twice and at length. She predicted students would read numbered tracks as a sequential requirement and generate significant advisor support burden. She suggested "paths" as a stronger alternative and offered a reframe: Insights and Foundations should arguably be positioned as step one and two given that FOL is the actual requirement, with Clarity and Connections framed as membership benefits rather than program steps.
  • What is required vs. optional: Natalie asked multiple times — in different framings — whether the Society tracks are required for induction or additional resources. The answer (only FOL is required; everything else is supplemental) was eventually clear, but the repetition of the question signals the distinction needs to be more immediately visible in the platform and onboarding communications.
  • Incentive structure for optional content: Natalie raised the question of what would motivate students to engage with Clarity and Connections if they aren't required. She suggested certificates, credentials, or explicit framing as "NSLS membership benefits" could help drive engagement. She noted that NSLS already offers job banks and career coaching that many students don't use.
  • Whether Insights should be required: Natalie proposed that requiring the Insights profile at registration (since it gathers personal information similar to what registration already collects) would guarantee a baseline data set and remove the ambiguity of whether students need to "complete" it before starting FOL.
  • Workload concern: Natalie was direct that she does not want to take on a platform that creates more work for her. She said she would not give a hard yes "because I also don't want to create more work for myself."

Surprises

  • Natalie's institutional context — a live restructuring with a 5–10% staff reduction and an active request for NSLS outcome data — was more urgent and specific than typical advisor feedback. Society's outcome reporting features are not just nice-to-have for her; they are directly relevant to her department's position at the university right now.
  • Natalie has never received any NSLS student evaluation data despite being the sole advisor for over 13 years. The absence of this feedback loop — and the fact that it has apparently not been surfaced before — is a significant gap with a long-tenured, high-performing partner school.
  • Natalie spontaneously reframed Society's track structure as an extension of existing NSLS membership benefits (job banks, career coaching, letters of recommendation) rather than a new program layer — a positioning angle the product team may not have articulated in that way yet.
  • Chris Higbee noted this was not the first time the concern about "tracks" implying sequential order had come up — validating Natalie's feedback as a cross-school signal.

Directly addresses her existing complaint about stop-and-start video instructions that don't work with random Zoom breakout rooms.

Natalie followed up to confirm students would retain access through graduation. Chris confirmed that is the expectation. No formal pricing structure shared.
