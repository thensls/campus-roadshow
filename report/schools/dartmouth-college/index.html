School & Chapter Snapshot

About Dartmouth College
  • 🏛️Dartmouth College is a private Ivy League research university in Hanover, NH, where the NSLS chapter is housed under the Nelson A. Rockefeller Center for Public Policy and the Social Sciences.
  • 🎯President Bylock has established institutional pillars including 'Dartmouth Dialogues' and a renewed emphasis on leadership training and career readiness, with a revitalized career services office now called the Dartmouth Center for Career Design.
  • 📚Students at Dartmouth are highly engaged and typically already involved in multiple organizations, making recruitment for newer initiatives like NSLS particularly challenging among upperclassmen.
Chapter Status
  • 🆕Chapter is early-stage and self-described as 'relatively still new,' with peer-led programming still aspirational rather than fully operationalized.
  • ⚠️Recent invitation cycle saw limited student interest in the traditional Foundations of Leadership program, particularly among upperclassmen already embedded in campus organizations.

Meeting Highlights Full Meeting Report ›

✅ What's Working / What Landed
  • 🤖AI-Powered Career Clarity Track — Both Bob and Travis reacted positively to the Clarity track. Travis called it 'fascinating' and said it works for students at all stages of professional development. Bob described it as 'a very innovative and logical reinvention' using modern tools.
  • 📊Advisor Dashboard and Longitudinal Data — Bob confirmed that a dashboard with quantitative roll-up data is exactly what the Rockefeller Center needs to tell its story to donors and the executive director. He explicitly said 'I do' when asked if the dashboard could be a good starting place for collecting that data.
  • 📱Mobile-First Design — Bob noted approvingly that having the platform right in students' hands is 'almost a prerequisite these days,' and Travis echoed that students want things easily accessible without scheduling meetings.
❌ What Isn't Working
  • 🔄Traditional Foundations of Leadership Appeal — Linda flagged and Bob confirmed that the traditional Foundations of Leadership program has had limited appeal in recent invitation cycles at Dartmouth, and it does not sell well among the student population.
  • 🏢Institutional Overlap with DCCD — Bob repeatedly raised concern that Society's career readiness features overlap significantly with what the newly revitalized Dartmouth Center for Career Design already offers, and indicated any broader adoption would require DCCD awareness and likely sign-off.
  • 👥Upperclassman Recruitment and Retention — Travis explained that juniors and seniors are already embedded in two or more organizations on average, making it difficult to recruit them to NSLS. Academic pressure is also the primary reason students disengage after joining.
⚠️ Key Signal — Institutional Caution Paired with Genuine Openness to Augmentation: Bob Coates expressed real enthusiasm for Society as a technology platform, rating it around an eight or nine, but consistently framed any adoption decision as above his pay grade and contingent on executive director approval and DCCD coordination. His most actionable signal was his explicit request for a non-proprietary shareable document he could bring to his boss, and his openness to scheduling a follow-up call to explore a pilot scoped as an augmentation to Foundations of Leadership. The clearest path forward is a tightly scoped pilot that stays within the chapter's existing leadership curriculum lane, deferring the career services integration conversation to a later stage once institutional stakeholders are aligned.
