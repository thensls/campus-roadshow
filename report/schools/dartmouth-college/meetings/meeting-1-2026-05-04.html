Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The peer-led model — this was the original and primary draw for the Rockefeller Center; it fills a gap that the Center's staff-led programs cannot
  • Career coaching and professional development programming — Travis identified this as the most distinctly valuable piece of NSLS for Dartmouth students
  • Foundations of Leadership as a curriculum base, particularly for incoming freshmen who are still forming their leadership identity and professional direction
  • Goal-setting and self-reflective components — Travis specifically cited the experience of clarifying where he is and where he wants to go as personally meaningful

What Isn't Working

  • Recruitment and retention of upperclassmen — juniors and seniors are already embedded in two or more organizations and are not looking for another club; NSLS must offer something that elevates, not merely adds
  • The traditional Foundations of Leadership invitation cycle has seen declining interest at Dartmouth — Linda confirmed this directly as the catalyst for this meeting
  • NSLS's value proposition is difficult to communicate without visible, local proof of impact — Travis noted he cannot point to evidence on his own campus yet
  • SNT formation and execution remain a friction point — the chapter has not yet reached the point of running peer-led training sessions at scale
  • The leadership curriculum feels redundant to students who have already built leadership experience through Dartmouth's existing co-curricular ecosystem
  • School workload is the dominant competing priority — academic demands cause students to disengage from organizations they have joined

School Priorities

  • President Bylock's institutional pillars include "Dartmouth Dialogues" (civil discourse, pluralism of viewpoints) and a renewed emphasis on leadership training across campus
  • Career readiness has received "revitalized emphasis" — a new hire from Trinity College leads the newly renamed Dartmouth Center for Career Design, with expanded community-based offerings organized by career interest area
  • The Rockefeller Center is actively investing in measurement and evaluation frameworks to demonstrate program impact — attended a Rice University / Doerr Institute course specifically for this purpose
  • Donor stewardship requires the Center to tell a quantitative story about the outcomes of its endowed and current-use funds
  • Pre-professional and public policy internship programming is a major Center function — a competitive first-year fellowship places students with federal agencies and policy organizations in D.C.
  • Bob and his executive director have a strategic interest in knowing how Society would interface with or be distinguishable from the DCCD's existing offerings before committing

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. "Elevate Me, Don't Add to My Plate"

Travis articulated the clearest signal of the meeting: Dartmouth students — especially upperclassmen — are not looking for another organization to join. They want something that demonstrably advances them toward their professional goals. The value proposition must shift from community membership to tangible, personalized career progression. Bob reinforced this by noting that what attracted the Rockefeller Center was the peer-led model, not a generic leadership curriculum.

2. Proof of Impact Is the Missing Link

Travis acknowledged he personally believes NSLS can help but cannot point to evidence of that impact on campus yet, making peer-to-peer advocacy difficult. The Rockefeller Center has invested directly in learning to measure and evaluate leadership interventions. Society's career readiness score, longitudinal dashboards, and outcome data were received as directly addressing this gap — Bob confirmed he sees the dashboard as a viable starting point for quantitative reporting▶ 51:58.

3. Institutional Navigation Is Required Before Any Expansion

Bob was consistently clear that even an augmentation-to-FOL approach would require informing his executive director and potentially coordinating with the DCCD. The revitalized Dartmouth Center for Career Design already performs many of the functions Society is designed to provide. Any deeper integration of Society beyond the chapter context will require a multi-stakeholder conversation that Bob does not currently have authority to initiate alone.

4. Mobile-First, Frictionless Access Is a Near-Prerequisite

Travis highlighted that students want tools that are immediately accessible — no scheduled meetings, no setup, available in the palm of their hand. Bob echoed this, noting Society being "right at their fingertips" powered by AI was a key part of what made it feel innovative. This reinforces that any Society rollout at Dartmouth should lead with the mobile experience and low-barrier onboarding.

5. Peer-Led Infrastructure Has Not Yet Been Realized — SNT Friction Remains

Both Bob and Travis acknowledged the chapter has not yet successfully stood up peer-led training experiences at scale. The original vision — that NSLS would bring something the Rockefeller Center's staff-led programs could not — remains aspirational rather than operational. Society's SNT matching, scheduling, attendance tracking, and virtual meeting tools were noted as potentially addressing this directly, though neither advisor had seen these features before and reaction was measured rather than enthusiastic.

Section 5 — Society Reception

8/10

Chris estimated both Bob and Travis at 7–8; Travis self-reported 8–9 on the product itself. Bob cited institutional cautions as the reason he would not go higher. Score above reflects the composite, weighted toward product enthusiasm rather than commitment readiness.

What Landed Well

  • Travis responded immediately and positively to the Career Clarity track — called it "fascinating" and highlighted the flexibility for both beginning and advanced users
  • The mobile-first design was noted by both as essentially a prerequisite for student adoption; Bob specifically appreciated that it puts capability "right at their fingertips"
  • Bob called Society "cutting edge" and a "very innovative and logical reinvention" of career readiness using modern tools, specifically AI
  • The career readiness score concept resonated strongly — Travis confirmed "100%" when asked if the dashboard and outcome data were directionally right
  • The Renee story (68-year-old nontraditional student achieving a 10/10 experience through the Clarity track) was well-received as a proof-of-concept for AI meeting students where they are
  • Bob immediately connected the longitudinal dashboard to his and his executive director's measurement and evaluation work — confirmed it "could be a good starting place"
  • Travis appreciated the flexibility of AI for students at different stages of professional development and suggested expanding dream job tiering as a feature enhancement

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Bob's most immediate reaction to the Career Clarity features was that they fall outside his sphere of authority and into the domain of the DCCD — he said he would almost need to "refer you over to them"
  • Bob raised the question of whether Society was an additional product layered on top of the existing NSLS chapter experience, or something that could replace or reframe parts of it — this was not fully resolved in the meeting
  • Bob noted that even adopting Society purely as an augmentation to Foundations of Leadership would likely require sign-off from the DCCD and his executive director, given the DCCD already offers similar services
  • The curriculum add-on fee model was mentioned briefly — no reaction was captured, but this could be a friction point given donor-funded budget constraints at the Rockefeller Center

Surprises

  • Bob's enthusiasm escalated meaningfully by the end of the meeting — he moved from "I'd almost have to refer you over to them" to "I would love to explore that a little bit further" and agreeing to take a non-proprietary overview to his executive director
  • Travis's suggestion to add tiered dream job ranking (analogous to college choice tiers) was an unsolicited product idea that showed deeper engagement with the feature set than expected
  • Bob's unprompted disclosure that the Rockefeller Center manages endowed funds and has stewardship obligations to donors surfaced a concrete internal use case for the dashboard that Chris had not anticipated

Section 6 — Feature-Level Feedback

Feature Signal Notes
Career Clarity Track Strong Travis called it "fascinating" and confirmed it works for both early-stage and advanced students. Bob called it "cutting edge." Directly addresses Dartmouth's most pressing engagement gap with upperclassmen.
AI Career Coach (conversational) Strong Both advisors responded positively to the idea of AI as a thought partner. Bob highlighted "at their fingertips, powered by AI" as the core appeal. Emphasis on AI not doing the work for students was well received given academic integrity sensitivities at an Ivy League institution.
Dream Job Generator Positive Travis suggested expanding this into a tiered ranking model (dream job → realistic options → fallback) — analogous to how students approach college selection. Strong product signal worth logging for the roadmap.
Career Readiness Score Strong Travis confirmed "100%" alignment with this direction. Bob connected it immediately to donor stewardship reporting and the need to tell a quantitative story about leadership outcomes. High relevance to both internal reporting and potential external visibility (U.S. News, Wall Street Journal framing resonated).
Advisor / Institutional Dashboard Strong Bob confirmed the dashboard could be a "good starting place" for the quantitative measurement work he and his executive director are actively pursuing. Longitudinal cohort data was specifically noted as valuable. This is a direct hook for the Rockefeller Center's current strategic initiative.
SNT Matching & Meeting Tools Positive Bob acknowledged that getting students quickly into organized peer groups would help build excitement and conversation. Travis did not comment directly. Seen as potentially addressing a real friction point, but both advisors framed SNT challenges more as a recruitment/retention issue than a logistics issue — the tool may not fully resolve the root cause.
Accountability Engine (AI reminders / check-ins) Positive Not discussed in depth but consistent with what Travis described as the student need: something that nudges progress without requiring meeting scheduling. No objections raised.
Campus-Specific Curriculum Integration (e.g., Dartmouth course listings surfaced by AI) Strong Chris introduced this as a near-future feature — the AI surfacing Dartmouth-specific courses, professors, and advisors rather than generic platforms. Bob's immediate reaction was that this is where it starts to overlap with DCCD territory. High interest but requires careful institutional positioning to avoid stepping on an existing department.
Custom Curriculum Add-On (fee-based) Open Question Bob acknowledged this could be interesting if the Rockefeller Center or other departments wanted to bring their own curriculum into Society. No reaction to the fee model was captured. Given that the Center operates on endowed and current-use funds with donor stewardship obligations, pricing sensitivity should be anticipated.
Mobile-First Design Strong Both advisors independently flagged mobile access as a near-prerequisite for student adoption. Travis framed it as students wanting things "right in the palm of their hands" without having to set up meetings or appointments.
Personality Assessments (Myers-Briggs, DISC, Holland Code, etc.) Needs Care No explicit reaction from Bob or Travis. At a research university with a sophisticated student body, these tools may be seen as familiar or even elementary. Framing and positioning within the broader Insights track will matter. Worth testing reception with Dartmouth students specifically before leading with this feature.
