Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Speaker broadcasts — Daphne explicitly named these as a valued, must-keep feature.▶ 4:24
  • The Foundations of Leadership curriculum and class opportunities — Daphne noted these are appreciated, while also flagging interest in revisiting the curriculum.▶ 4:28
  • NSLS's responsiveness and accessibility — Daphne praised NSLS's culture of listening to feedback and acting on it, calling it a distinguishing characteristic she would never want lost.▶ 4:47
  • NSLS's innovation orientation — Daphne emphasized that NSLS's commitment to staying current with research and trends is core to its value. She said she never wants NSLS to "get stale."▶ 4:50
  • Community collaboration opportunities — Daphne values that NSLS engages not just with schools but with broader community initiatives.▶ 4:42

What Isn't Working

  • Email configuration confusion: Daphne raised a recurring issue (previously surfaced in another meeting) where students do not set their school email as their primary address.▶ 13:37 This creates communication failures, Gmail bounce-backs, and student confusion about which email to use. She proposed enforcing school email as primary with an optional secondary field.
  • No student ID field in the platform: Daphne requested the ability to capture student IDs (not SSNs) in the platform to streamline cross-referencing with institutional systems (Banner/Colleague). Current process requires running IR reports manually, which is time-consuming and error-prone when students share names.
  • Sense of belonging gap: Daphne flagged this as a campus-wide institutional challenge — students feel less connected post-COVID, and Coastal Carolina is actively working to address it. This is where NSLS programming can play a direct role.
  • Engagement drop-off and membership value perception: Daphne referenced the broader concern (echoed across advisors) that too many students pay dues and disengage. She is motivated by tools that help surface NSLS value more explicitly to students.
  • Limited regional event infrastructure: Daphne raised the need for more regional leadership summits. She noted the national summit in Raleigh was accessible due to geography, but most schools don't have that advantage. She observed a direct behavioral shift in her executive board following summit attendance and believes regional events would replicate that impact more broadly.

School Priorities

  • Student sense of belonging: Daphne explicitly named this as the top institutional priority right now. Retention is strong, enrollment is not a concern — belonging is the gap being actively addressed.
  • Real-time student feedback mechanisms: Daphne referenced wanting quick-pulse surveys (e.g., 2-question mobile check-ins) to understand whether programming is meeting student needs and to surface available NSLS resources students may not know to ask for.
  • Career readiness and workforce outcomes: Implicitly aligned with Society's direction — Daphne engaged actively with the career clarity demo and asked the AI coach a student-facing question about major alignment, indicating she sees this as directly relevant to her students' needs.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Belonging as the Core Institutional Driver

Daphne was unambiguous: Coastal Carolina's top priority is students' sense of belonging, not enrollment or retention (both of which are currently healthy). This positions Society's community, SNT matching, and connections features as directly mission-aligned tools — not add-ons. Any pitch or pilot framing at Coastal Carolina should lead with belonging and community-building outcomes, not just career readiness metrics.

2. Advisors Were Pre-Sold Before the Demo

Daphne noted that she and Megan had already discussed Society's potential while completing the pre-meeting survey. Their 10/10 ratings were not reactive to the presentation — they reflected existing alignment between their institutional needs and the direction Society is heading. This signals that the pre-meeting touchpoints (surveys, prior AIN discussions) are doing real priming work and that Coastal Carolina is an exceptionally warm prospect for early partnership.

3. Data Infrastructure Friction Is Slowing Advisor Effectiveness

Daphne raised two specific, actionable data pain points: the inability to set a student's school email as primary (causing communication breakdowns), and the absence of a student ID field (forcing manual IR report pulls and cross-referencing). Both are administrative burdens that erode the advisor experience and slow down student support workflows. These are not aspirational requests — they are recurring, documented frustrations with clear technical solutions.

4. Regional Events as a High-ROI Engagement Lever

Daphne provided direct observational evidence that summit attendance drives executive board engagement and chapter momentum. She noted that most schools cannot access the national summit affordably or logistically, making regional summits a scalable way to replicate that impact. This is not a product feature request but a program design signal that NSLS should consider as it thinks about engagement architecture beyond the platform itself.

5. Real-Time Student Pulse as a Missing Feedback Loop

Daphne described a gap between programming delivery and student signal — advisors currently lack lightweight, in-the-moment tools to know whether students are getting what they need or are aware of available NSLS benefits. She envisioned short mobile surveys (2 questions) as a mechanism to close that loop. This is a distinct feature signal: not deep analytics, but quick-pulse feedback to inform advisor decision-making in near real time.

Section 5 — Society Reception

10/10

Daphne Holland: 10/10 (verbal). Megan Grandey: 10/10 (chat, before departing early).

What Landed Well

  • Career Clarity track and AI coach: Daphne engaged immediately with the live demo, asking the AI coach a real student-facing question about which majors align with a dream job. The responsiveness of the tool and its ability to generate relevant, personalized output was visibly compelling to her.
  • The Renee story: The non-traditional student narrative resonated with advisors who work with diverse student populations. Daphne's school serves students with varied life circumstances, and the idea that Society can re-engage a student that leadership alone didn't capture was clearly meaningful.
  • Society as an upgrade, not a replacement: Chris's framing that Society builds on NSLS's existing foundation without uprooting current work was well-received. Daphne's concerns had previously been about NSLS staying innovative, and Society directly answers that.
  • Pilot partner invitation: Both advisors expressed readiness. The framing of a co-creative, phased rollout matched the collaborative relationship Daphne described valuing in NSLS.
  • Campus-wide potential (career services, academic advising integration): The idea of Society becoming a hub that pulls in other departments aligned with Daphne's instinct to use NSLS as a community collaboration vehicle.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Major alignment in the AI coach: Daphne's live question — "what majors make the most sense to guide me toward my dream job?" — revealed an expectation that the platform should bridge career direction and academic planning. The coach answered generically in the demo; Daphne's question implied she wants school-specific course and major mapping. This is a near-term capability gap to acknowledge honestly.
  • Volume of content layers: While not raised directly by Daphne or Megan, the broader group concern (raised by Dan) about how all the new tracks and features get communicated digestibly to students is worth tracking for Coastal Carolina's pilot planning. Daphne's engagement with the belonging and quick-pulse themes suggests she will want a streamlined student onboarding experience.

Surprises

  • Daphne and Megan had already reached a 10/10 disposition before seeing the demo, based on the pre-session survey alone. This is an unusually strong early signal for a school in Tier 2.
  • Daphne's student ID request was novel — Chris noted he had not heard this specific suggestion before, despite having heard the email issue previously. It reflects a deeper systems-integration mindset than typical advisor feedback.
  • Daphne asked a live, unscripted question to the AI coach during the demo — treating it as a real tool in the moment rather than a passive observer. This behavioral signal indicates high personal investment in the product's utility.
