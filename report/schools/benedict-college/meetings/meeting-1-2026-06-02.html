Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The inclusive scope of NSLS — leadership development that applies to all student cohorts, not just those already in leadership roles. Nyeem specifically said leadership development should be "cohesive with all cohorts, all populations."
  • The individual accountability embedded in NSLS — the idea that each student must do their own work to cultivate leadership.
  • The credibility and national recognition of the NSLS brand, which he sees as an asset in building student and faculty trust.

What Isn't Working

  • Students perceive NSLS invitation emails as spam or scam communications — Nyeem named this as his top current challenge: "Getting people to understand what NSLS is and that it's not the average scam email."
  • Low student buy-in and difficulty getting students to understand what NSLS offers and how it benefits them.
  • Students lack focus on "the middle meat" — they know a goal but cannot sustain the effort to work through the steps required to reach it.
  • Students default to judging opportunities at face value (e.g., rejecting a job at an agency because of the agency's name) rather than exploring the full scope of opportunity inside an organization.
  • Career readiness data is fragmented across Handshake, FDS, and manual surveys — no unified view.
  • Post-graduation outcome tracking is largely absent or inconsistent.
  • Students arrive without a real plan — "there's an idea of what to do, but there's no real plan."
  • Chapter had been disengaged from NSLS for roughly a year — low advisor responsiveness noted by the CSR prior to the meeting.

School Priorities

  • Moving toward a data-driven approach to student development — Nyeem specifically cited graduation rates, internship tracking, and post-graduation workforce outcomes as key data needs.
  • Building and demonstrating student "off-campus readiness" — internships, networking, interviewing, professional brand-building.
  • Reinventing career services ahead of a new department head transition.
  • Securing faculty buy-in as a multiplier for student engagement — Nyeem described faculty as essential validators: "students will do a lot of what they should do when they know that the person telling them has skin in the game too."
  • Providing students with proof that career development works — alumni and mentor validation seen as critical motivators given current student skepticism about career outcomes.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Data as the Missing Credibility Layer

Nyeem repeatedly returned to data — not as a reporting convenience, but as the foundation of his ability to persuade students, faculty, and administration. He uses Handshake, FDS, and surveys but aggregates them manually with no unified view. He explicitly wants longitudinal data: post-graduation outcomes, internship-to-employment conversion, and career readiness scores that can be shared with faculty. The Career Readiness Score resonated immediately because it gave him a single, defensible number he could bring to those conversations.

2. Faculty as the Lever for Student Buy-In

Nyeem described a clear dynamic at Benedict: students engage more when faculty are visibly engaged. He indicated that if he can show faculty what Society does and how it supports their work, they become amplifiers for student adoption. This is a Benedict-specific pattern worth noting for rollout strategy — a faculty-first seeding approach before direct student launch may accelerate adoption.

3. Students Need the "Middle Meat" — Structured Momentum

Nyeem coined a phrase that the team responded to in real time: students know what they want but lack the structured support to work through the middle of the journey. They get lost between intention and outcome. He described Society's accountability and goal-setting features as "intentional" — a word he used specifically to name what has been missing. The AI coach, SMART goal cards, and SNT accountability loop all address this gap directly.

4. NSLS Invitation Perceived as Spam — Trust Gap at the Front Door

Nyeem identified the invitation email as his primary activation barrier — students dismiss it as a scam before they ever engage. This is a known signal across multiple schools, and Chris noted product improvements underway (sender credibility, third-party social proof, improved email targeting). However, Nyeem's framing highlights that this is not just a design problem — it is a trust and brand-awareness problem at HBCUs where students may be especially skeptical of unsolicited credentialing offers.

5. Alumni Proof Points as Student Motivators

Nyeem explicitly said students need to see that someone else "did it and it worked" before they will commit to a career path. He connected this to NSLS's 2 million alumni network as an underutilized asset. The Connections track's planned mentoring capabilities with NSLS alumni were received as a direct response to this need. This also points to the importance of surfacing peer and alumni success stories within the Society platform experience itself.

Section 5 — Society Reception

10/10

What Landed Well

  • The Clarity Track — Nyeem immediately understood its value for students who arrive without a career plan. The Renee story (68-year-old student spending over an hour engaged) resonated as proof that even disengaged members can be re-activated through career-focused content.
  • The Career Readiness Score — described as a "big box check." Nyeem connected it directly to his goal of showing measurable student progress to faculty and administration.
  • AI coach as a force multiplier — the framing of the coach as "Nyeem in everyone's pocket" that frees him to do higher-value human work landed clearly. He confirmed the 2,060:1 student-to-counselor ratio resonated.
  • SNT matching automation — Nyeem currently tags SNTs onto existing campus events. The idea of automated matching within the platform was seen as solving a real drop-off point he already experiences.
  • SMART goal cards and accountability engine — aligned with his description of students needing structure through "the middle meat" of their journey.
  • Personality assessments (Myers-Briggs, DISC, Holland Code, Enneagram, Big Five) generated from Clarity Track responses — seen as immediately useful for career advising conversations.
  • Post-graduation outcome tracking via planned Handshake and LinkedIn integrations — directly addressed his stated need for longitudinal data.
  • Alumni mentoring through Connections track — addressed his point that students need proof others have succeeded before they will commit.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • No explicit objections were raised. Nyeem asked no clarifying questions about pricing, implementation timeline, or technical requirements during the session — notable given the school's history of low engagement and cost sensitivity flagged by the CSR pre-call.
  • School-specific AI training (agent that knows Benedict's course catalog, staff, etc.) was described as "about to change" but not yet available — Nyeem was not visibly concerned, but this is a gap between what was shown and what is live.
  • The design system (Paper) was noted as pre-build — Nyeem was shown wireframes/designs, not fully functional features, for the accountability engine and SNT matching. This was disclosed but not pressed on.

Surprises

  • Nyeem gave a 10/10 and verbally committed to the pilot on the first call — unusually fast given the chapter's prior disengagement and the CSR's difficulty reaching him for months.
  • Nyeem immediately began mentally planning faculty outreach during the demo: "I'm already peering in my head, okay, what faculty are about to be annoyed by Nyeem this summer." This level of activation was spontaneous and unprompted.
  • His framing of student skepticism — "it doesn't seem like to them, based off what they're seeing around them, that this means anything" — surfaced a deeper motivational barrier rooted in current events and social context, not just career services process. This is a meaningful signal about the emotional job Society needs to do for HBCU students specifically.

SNT Matching Automation Positive Currently Nyeem piggybacks SNTs onto existing campus events. Automated matching by availability (and future: major/strengths) was seen as a real improvement. He confirmed drop-off around SNT formation is a known problem.

School-Specific AI Agent (Course Catalog, Staff Directory) Open Question Described as "about to change" but not yet built. Nyeem did not probe the timeline. Given Benedict's context — a smaller HBCU with a reinvention agenda — the relevance of a school-trained agent is high, but the gap between current state and this capability was not fully addressed.
Invitation Email / Onboarding Trust Signals Needs Care Nyeem named this as his number-one problem — students perceive NSLS invitations as scam emails. Chris noted improvements are underway (sender address, third-party credibility markers like U.S. News & World Report). This remains an unresolved activation barrier that precedes any platform feature. Particularly acute at HBCUs where student skepticism about unsolicited credentialing offers may be elevated.
Foundations of Leadership 2.0 (Modular Video) Positive Chris proactively acknowledged the current video content is dated and long. Nyeem did not push back or add criticism — suggesting this is a known issue he has already accepted. The modular, shorter-format direction was received without objection.
Advisor Dashboard (Longitudinal Cohort Data) Strong Nyeem's data-driven reinvention agenda makes this highly relevant. The ability to see cohort progress over time — not just current semester — directly addresses his need to tell a cumulative story to faculty and administration. Shown as a design/in-development screen; not yet live.
