Section 1 — School Snapshot

School: Johnston Community College

Location: Smithfield, NC (Johnston County, outside Raleigh)

School Type: Public 2-Year Community College

Chapter Status: Established — chapter founded approximately 2013–2015; has been operating for a number of years under shifting advisor leadership▶ 2:46

Chapter Type: Standard (in-person/traditional); advisor expressed strong openness to transitioning to an online or hybrid model▶ 3:01

Advisors Present: Megan Shaner (Dean of Students); Dejeahn Brown (NSLS CSR, attended throughout)

Notable Context: Johnston County was recently among the fastest-growing counties in North Carolina and the United States, driven largely by a pharmaceutical industry boom (including Novo Nordisk, primary manufacturer of Ozempic). The college is mid-transition on several institutional fronts: moving from 16-week to 8-week sessions starting fall▶ 10:31, migrating to Canvas LMS this summer, and managing rapid enrollment growth that has outpaced support infrastructure. Megan came to day-to-day chapter management after her predecessor transitioned to NC State. She oversees a large portfolio of student-facing responsibilities and is time-constrained. The college has an active career services department that is chronically understaffed and underutilized. The school is culturally inclined to pilot new initiatives. At the time of the meeting, Johnston was already piloting multiple simultaneous projects at the presidential level, which Megan flagged as a capacity consideration.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The turnkey, low-lift nature of Foundations of Leadership — structured materials, pre-built facilitation guides, and invitation workflows that don't require heavy advisor customization or daily attention
  • The CSR support relationship — Megan specifically cited Dejeahn's responsiveness as a key enabler, including helping her pull member analytics▶ 6:30 and answering ad hoc questions
  • Facilitation guides written so that students could theoretically run events themselves — Megan values that the design intent exists even if she hasn't fully realized it yet▶ 7:00
  • In-person induction — she wants to keep this as a concrete, ceremonial touchpoint regardless of how much else moves online
  • At least one in-person kickoff or welcome event to establish legitimacy and trust with new members ("I'm a real person, this is real")
  • The plug-and-play structure that allows advisors with thin capacity to sustain the chapter▶ 4:26 even through personnel transitions — she flagged this as especially important for other NC community colleges she knows

What Isn't Working

  • The advisor dashboard only displays data for a single semester, causing visibility gaps mid-cycle▶ 8:33 when the dashboard rolls over — Megan noted this was a known issue and some fixes had already been applied▶ 9:00
  • Low member completion rates — many students sign up, pay the fee, and then never follow through; Megan attributes this partly to confusion with honor societies like Phi Theta Kappa where paying is the end of the commitment
  • SNT scheduling is a persistent bottleneck — many members cannot attend in person, and finding matched availability online is difficult under the current model
  • Career services is chronically underutilized — students only seek it out just before graduation for resume help or LinkedIn, not as an ongoing developmental resource; compounded by high staff turnover (positions go vacant for 4–5 months at a time)
  • The in-person LTD/orientation format feels limiting to Megan as an educator — she described it as "basically showing videos" and noted students themselves question the value of attending in person when the same content opens online afterward
  • Insufficient member analytics — Megan wants funnel-style data (conversion rates, time-to-completion, part-time vs. full-time breakdowns)▶ 9:14 and cannot currently pull that from the dashboard

School Priorities

  • Workforce alignment and career pathway clarity — connecting students to specific local employers (especially pharmaceutical, given Novo Nordisk's presence) and ensuring programs lead to concrete job outcomes
  • Managing rapid enrollment growth without overwhelming support infrastructure — the college grew faster than its systems could handle and is now focused on quality of student experience, not just volume
  • Transitioning to 8-week sessions starting fall — this will change enrollment cycles, student pacing, and on/off-ramp timing significantly and is a major operational shift
  • Moving to Canvas LMS this summer — an institutional technology migration that is consuming bandwidth across departments
  • Reducing advisor/staff burden — Megan oversees many departments and cannot take on high-lift commitments; any new program must demonstrate a net-positive time trade-off
  • Student retention and completion — getting students not just enrolled but through to outcomes; NSLS completion gap is a microcosm of a broader institutional challenge

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Career Clarity Is the Right Hook for This School

Megan independently articulated — before seeing any product demo — that students at Johnston don't treat college as a career journey. They delay engagement with career services until graduation and lack intentionality about their path. She described wanting NSLS to shift from a "leadership development program" to a "career development program." This framing maps directly to the Society Career Clarity track and validates the core product thesis for community college contexts. Her words: "I have never had an online chapter… I wonder if an online would drive more completions." Career outcomes are not just a nice-to-have here — they are the institutional mandate given the college's pharmaceutical employer relationships and workforce focus.

2. The Online/Hybrid Transition Is Actively Under Consideration

Megan had already been in conversation with Dejeahn about moving toward an online chapter model before this meeting. The 8-week session transition, combined with the in-person LTD friction she described, makes the timing of Society's online infrastructure relevant and potentially well-timed. She outlined a preferred hybrid model: one in-person kickoff event to establish trust and legitimacy, followed by fully online completion of all program steps, with in-person induction retained at the end. She was receptive to the national events team handling LTD virtually, explicitly saying she'd be comfortable giving that up if she could keep the bookend touchpoints.

3. Advisor Capacity Is the Binding Constraint

Every feature or change was evaluated through the lens of time. Megan manages a large division and described NSLS's value partly in terms of what it doesn't require of her. She explicitly asked about pilot partner time commitment before confirming her participation, having been burned by an underestimated commitment in a personal project. The pilot must be positioned as time-neutral or time-saving to maintain her engagement. Chris framed the online model transition as a net time gain (no more room scheduling, no more in-person LTD facilitation), and this landed well. Any ask of Megan beyond logging into Society, gathering staff/student feedback, and attending a planning meeting will need to be clearly scoped upfront.

4. Data and Analytics Are a Genuine Unmet Need

Megan's background in admissions gave her a strong data orientation. She described wanting funnel-style analytics — member conversion, time-to-completion, part-time vs. full-time breakdown — and expressed frustration that the current dashboard rolls over each semester, obscuring longitudinal trends. She reacted positively to the career readiness score concept and the idea that career services staff could access a student's score before a meeting to come prepared. This is both a product validation signal and a potential differentiator for institutional buy-in beyond the chapter advisor level.

5. CliftonStrengths Integration Is a Recurring, High-Priority Request

Megan raised CliftonStrengths unprompted when viewing the personality profile section of the platform. She noted that multiple organizations on Johnston's campus already use CliftonStrengths as part of membership enrollment and that it is widely adopted across North Carolina community colleges, particularly for first-generation and non-traditional students. She described it as crossing over their leadership programming and committed to continuing to raise it. Chris confirmed this has come up repeatedly across the roadshow and noted it's on the radar for the product team. This is a strong, consistent signal warranting formal roadmap consideration.

Section 5 — Society Reception

9/10

Megan rated 9/10 for students and the college, and 7–8/10 for herself personally, citing the learning curve of adopting something new as the only friction. Her overall score is reported as 9 reflecting the institutional/student enthusiasm she led with.

What Landed Well

  • The Career Clarity track concept resonated immediately and deeply — Megan said "I think this really builds capacity" and described the demo as intuitive to how students use technology
  • The Renee story (68-year-old student who spent over an hour in the Clarity track and gave it a 10/10) landed as a compelling proof point; Megan connected it to her own non-traditional student population
  • The career readiness score concept — she immediately saw the application for career services meeting prep and for helping students understand their progress
  • The framing of Society as an augmentation, not a replacement — she was relieved it didn't mean ripping out what exists
  • The AI coach's ability to reference campus-specific resources (course catalog, departments, staff directory) — described as the type of functionality that could meaningfully connect students to career services earlier
  • The achievement engine with SMART goal-setting and reminders — seen as consistent with how students engage with technology today
  • The SNT matching and online meeting functionality — she immediately connected this to her completion bottleneck around SNT scheduling
  • The pilot being free — explicitly noted as important; "it's free for now, Megan" landed with clear relief
  • Career Clarity as a post-induction "carrot" — the idea of unlocking the track after completing Foundations of Leadership was well-received as a motivational mechanic

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Dashboard/interface confusion — Megan paused mid-demo to ask "would this be their dashboard?" indicating the relationship between the current NSLS platform and Society wasn't immediately clear from the demo flow
  • Uncertainty about the online chapter model — she said she's "still a little unclear about what specifically the differences would be" between a standard chapter using national events versus a fully online chapter, and wanted more time with Dejeahn to work through it
  • Concern about maintaining legitimacy without in-person presence — her view is that the in-person kickoff was critical to overcoming the "is this a scam?" objection early in the chapter's history; she wants to preserve at least one in-person touchpoint even if everything else moves online
  • Pilot commitment scope — she pushed back before agreeing, wanting to know exactly what was expected of her in terms of time; was satisfied by Chris's answer but the question itself signals she needs clear, written expectations
  • Timing uncertainty — she flagged that the 8-week session transition and other institutional changes create competing priorities for fall; said "if it's timed right for fall, we can do it — if not, we'll look at it for spring"
  • CliftonStrengths absence — noted as a gap, though she acknowledged it was being tracked

Surprises

  • Megan had independently arrived at nearly the same product framing as the Society pitch before seeing any of it — she used the phrase "career development program" to describe what she wanted NSLS to become, unprompted, before the demo began
  • She was already open to a full online transition, which is further along than most in-person chapter advisors at this stage of the roadshow
  • She described the in-person LTD as something she'd be willing to give up — despite having facilitated it many times — because she views it as structurally redundant when students can do the same content online
  • She raised the 8-week session transition as an argument in favor of Society's asynchronous model, framing it as making online tools more necessary, not less — a signal of strong institutional fit
