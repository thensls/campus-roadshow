Section 1 — School Snapshot

⛔ No recording — WGU institutional policy prohibits recording by Fathom or any third-party note-taking tool. This report is based solely on meeting notes.

School: Western Governors University — Salt Lake City, UT (Online)

School Type: University · Private Non-Profit · Online

Presenter: Chris Higbee, VP of Product (NSLS)

Attendees: Ann Leary, Kristin Stege, Brenna Gillman, Debbie Fowler (SVP, Student Success), Margaret Mancuso

Notable Context: WGU is a fully online, competency-based institution historically serving adult learners and working professionals. The institution is beginning to attract younger, more traditional student populations — creating new alignment opportunities with Society's career development and engagement model. Advisors manage high student-to-advisor ratios, making scalability a core consideration for any new tool.

Important — WGU's Technology & Innovation Context: WGU is widely regarded as one of higher education's most technologically sophisticated institutions. The university maintains dedicated internal software engineering, product management, data science, and machine learning teams that build proprietary tools powering their student experience. WGU also operates WGU Labs — a nonprofit R&D and incubation affiliate — and an innovation group that not only develops solutions for internal use but actively explores commercializing those solutions externally. This context is essential for interpreting the group's guarded responses during this meeting.

Section 3 — Key Themes & Signals

SNT Experience & Progression

The SNT matcher — finding peers, facilitating virtual meetings, and tracking attendance — was well received by the group. Having all of this housed within a single interface was seen as a meaningful advantage. Advisors view it as a way to reduce barriers, drive continued engagement, and help close the gap between LTD completion and active SNT participation.

Data & Reporting

The group showed clear interest in concrete data points across career readiness, networking, and skills development. Reporting dashboards showing cohort progress and engagement trends were highlighted as a standout feature. The ability to "tell the story" through data — and to share those insights up the institutional chain — was called out as a meaningful capability, particularly given WGU's scale.

Gamification

Positive reaction to goal-setting with associated tasks and usage streaks (e.g., multiple consecutive days of engagement). Seen as a practical lever for driving continued participation in an online-only environment where student momentum can stall.

Integration & Avoiding Duplication

A recurring theme was the importance of not duplicating tools WGU already has. The group expressed interest in exploring integration with current systems for data sharing rather than creating a parallel experience. Seen as a longer-term exploration — something to potentially pursue down the road rather than an immediate requirement.

⚠️ WGU Building Internally — Competitive Overlap

Debbie Fowler disclosed that WGU already has SNT-style peer matching functionality in active development within their own student systems. This is not a vague reference to existing tools — it is a direct description of work in progress that overlaps with one of Society's headline features. WGU has the engineering capacity, product teams, and institutional scale to build and deploy this independently. Critically, their WGU Labs innovation group has a track record of exploring commercialization of internally-built solutions — meaning WGU could, in theory, become a competitor rather than a customer. The tight-lipped, strategic posture of the group throughout the meeting is most easily explained by this context: they were evaluating Society against what they are already building.

Academic Integration Opportunity

One academic dean raised the idea of tying NSLS into undergraduate programs directly. The group questioned whether Society would be even more effective within an academic program context — a signal worth tracking as a potential institutional partnership model.

Section 4 — Society Reception

7–8

The group was notably guarded throughout the meeting. Scored enthusiasm reflects individual comments from Ann Leary (7–8/10) and Kristin Stege (7/10) — the other three attendees, including the most senior person in the room (Debbie Fowler, SVP Student Success), did not offer enthusiasm scores and shared limited qualitative feedback. The restrained engagement is best understood in the context of WGU's internal development activity: the group was evaluating Society against capabilities they are already building, making it difficult to read genuine interest vs. competitive reconnaissance.

What Resonated

  • SNT matcher as a low-friction peer connection tool within a single platform — though Debbie Fowler's disclosure suggests WGU is already building this capability
  • Reporting dashboards for cohort progress, engagement trends, and career readiness data
  • Gamification (goal setting, usage streaks) as a driver of continued engagement
  • Scalable, personalized support — particularly relevant given high advisor-to-student ratios
  • Alignment with WGU's growing younger student population and their expectations

Key Concerns

  • WGU is building SNT matching internally — Debbie Fowler disclosed this directly; the feature that resonated most with the group is one WGU is actively developing on its own
  • Risk of overwhelming students who already have many opportunities available through WGU
  • Potential duplication with existing WGU tools — the group was notably careful about this throughout; now understood to be more than a general concern
  • Day-to-day workflow fit for advisors — need to understand practical support impact
  • Strategic ambiguity: WGU Labs has a commercialization mandate; it is unclear whether WGU views NSLS as a potential partner or as a competitive reference point for their own product roadmap

Section 6 — Next Steps & Open Items

⚠️ Key Signal — Competitive Overlap & Strategic Ambiguity: The most important takeaway from this meeting is not enthusiasm level — it is Debbie Fowler's disclosure that WGU is already building SNT matching internally. WGU is one of higher education's most technologically capable institutions, with a dedicated product and engineering organization and a commercialization-oriented innovation group (WGU Labs). The group's tight-lipped demeanor throughout the meeting is most plausibly explained by this context: they were evaluating Society against what they are already building, not as a straightforward adoption decision. Society's clearest differentiators — the 2.2M alumni mentor network, cross-institutional peer matching, and the NSLS brand and credential — are features WGU cannot easily replicate on their own. Any continued engagement should lead with these, and NSLS leadership should assess whether this is a genuine partnership opportunity or competitive reconnaissance before investing further resources.

Open Questions

  • What is the true intent of WGU's engagement? Was this meeting primarily a competitive intelligence-gathering exercise, or is there genuine interest in adopting Society?
  • How far along is WGU's internal SNT matching development, and what is their timeline for deployment?
  • Does WGU Labs intend to commercialize their peer matching or engagement tools — and if so, could WGU become a direct competitor rather than a customer?
  • What does Society offer that WGU fundamentally cannot build internally — and how do we lead with that in any follow-up?
  • Would Society be more effective if embedded within an academic program rather than as a standalone co-curricular offering — and does this model avoid the competitive overlap entirely?
  • How can Society be positioned to avoid overwhelming students at a school already offering many opportunities?
