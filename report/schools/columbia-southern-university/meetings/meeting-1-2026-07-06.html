Section 1 — School Snapshot

School: Columbia Southern University (CSU)

Location: Orange Beach, AL

School Type: Private, For-Profit, Online-Only 4-Year Institution

Chapter Status: Established

Chapter Type: Online (fully virtual; relies entirely on NSLS National Events Team for induction facilitation)

Advisors Present: Katelyn Garrison (Director of Student Organizations & First Year Experience); Linda Condon (CSR)

Notable Context: CSU is a large-scale online institution with approximately 26,200 active students (defined as having taken a course within the past year) and roughly 20,000 currently enrolled. The NSLS chapter is approaching 19,600 members — nearly surpassing the currently enrolled student count, which Katelyn described as an exciting milestone. The average student age was approximately 37–38 in the most recent data cycle, reflecting a heavily non-traditional, working-adult student body. CSU has expanded significantly in recent years, adding new colleges (Education, Nursing, Military) and new programs (e.g., Master's in Psychology). CSU was an early pilot participant for the national virtual induction ceremony▶ 6:54 and was proactively invited into the Society roadshow. Katelyn operates a CSU-specific NSLS Facebook group to supplement national communications▶ 7:29 and is actively developing an internal mentorship program featuring NSLS inducted members.

Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • The online/virtual delivery model — Katelyn explicitly said "never get rid of that, only increase the virtual, if anything." It is foundational to CSU's ability to participate in NSLS at all.
  • Student recognition — the invitation process, physical and digital recognition artifacts (badges, membership kit), and the emotional weight of being acknowledged. Katelyn noted that even students in their 70s who don't expect career benefit still value the recognition deeply▶ 10:52.
  • Badges shared on LinkedIn — Katelyn specifically observed that during invitation cycles, she sees students sharing NSLS badges on LinkedIn▶ 11:26, signaling that social recognition is alive and meaningful.
  • The ability to communicate with members in bulk via the chapter leader dashboard — she uses it regularly to drive engagement, though the reply experience is a pain point▶ 7:44.
  • The national events team facilitation model — since CSU cannot run local in-person events, the national team is the backbone of their induction pipeline.

What Isn't Working

  • Bulk email reply threading — when Katelyn sends messages to up to ~1,000 students at a time, all responses come back on one collapsed thread rather than individual conversations. She described managing over 300 responses in a single thread after one campaign as unwieldy▶ 13:27. She wants bulk messaging to feel personal on receipt and manageable on return.
  • Students completing induction — a recurring issue where students believe they are fully members upon joining but have not completed orientation, Leadership Training Day, SNTs, or other requirements. Katelyn said she frequently receives emails from students saying they haven't received their membership kit▶ 20:38, only to discover they haven't finished induction.
  • Lack of a clear "you are here" progress experience — students don't have a simple, visual, step-by-step breakdown of where they are in the induction journey▶ 21:03 and what comes next. Katelyn wants this to be simple, not overwhelming, but complete.
  • Students not understanding time commitment upfront — "How long is it going to take me?" is one of the most frequent questions she receives▶ 21:32. The FAQ addresses it, but the context for a working adult (who may already attend weekly SNT-like meetings in their job) is different from a traditional student.

School Priorities

  • Enrollment growth — CSU is a for-profit institution and explicitly prioritizes increasing enrollments and admissions.
  • Degree program expansion — CSU has launched new colleges (Education, Nursing, Military) and new degree programs (e.g., Master's in Psychology) in recent years, with additional programs pending announcement.
  • Returning to pre-pandemic active student numbers — Katelyn noted they are close, with 26,200 active students and 20,000 currently enrolled.
  • Compliance and outcomes documentation — Katelyn mentioned a recent meeting with CSU's compliance director related to tracking whether students who complete degrees are obtaining relevant employment▶ 39:17. Career readiness and outcome data are actively relevant to this institutional obligation.
  • Career readiness and workforce preparation — though CSU already uses tools like Forage and Big Interview in Career Services, Katelyn was clearly interested in how Society's data could complement or integrate with those existing efforts.
  • Student engagement for non-traditional learners — nearly all students are working adults balancing personal, professional, and academic schedules. Any engagement tool must be frictionless, self-paced, and immediately useful.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Recognition Is the Hook — Career Clarity Could Be the Anchor

Katelyn confirmed that recognition (badges, physical kits, LinkedIn shares) is what draws students in and what she leads with. However, she also recognized — without being prompted — that for CSU's older, career-established students, traditional leadership framing may not resonate the way career clarity does. The Renee story landed because it mapped directly to CSU's student reality. Society's career clarity track is positioned as the engagement layer that could convert passive members into active participants.

2. Induction Completion Is the Core Engagement Problem

Katelyn described a persistent pattern: students join, feel recognized, then go dark — never finishing induction — and only resurface when they want something (membership kit, benefits). This is not a motivation problem; it's a visibility and scaffolding problem. She explicitly asked for a "you are here" view that shows students their full journey in a simple, non-overwhelming format. Society's welcome track and progress dashboard directly address this, and she responded positively when she saw it.

3. Data Portability and Cross-Departmental Value Are Strategic Priorities

Katelyn asked unprompted whether student-inputted career data (current job titles, dream jobs, career trajectories) would be exportable and accessible to other departments. She specifically named Career Services and Compliance as stakeholders who would want this data. She drew a direct line between Society's career readiness score concept and CSU's compliance obligations around graduate employment outcomes. This signals that Society has the potential to serve as more than an NSLS tool — it could become an institutional data asset.

4. AI Enthusiasm Is High but Scoped — Safety Guardrails Are Welcomed

Katelyn described AI as "a vast ocean that is so deep — I don't know where it begins nor ends." She asked sharp questions about AI scope: Can it help find books? Can it recommend career services staff? Can it facilitate networking practice? She was genuinely excited about the Campus Agent concept. Importantly, when Chris explained URL restrictions and malicious injection risks, Katelyn immediately validated the caution — "if it's going to happen, it's going to happen to one of my students." She is an enthusiastic AI adopter who nonetheless values protective guardrails.

5. Platform Integration Clarity Is Needed Before Rollout

Katelyn asked a direct and practical question about how Society would coexist with the existing NSLS core platform — would it require a separate registration, separate login, or additional clicks? She framed this explicitly in terms of anticipating student confusion ("just thinking of multiple clicks that a student would have to do"). Chris clarified that both platforms would be unified within a year and that students using the national events team pathway would access Society directly from their invitation. This resolved her concern, but it signals that clear communication about the transition will be essential for CSU's onboarding.

Section 5 — Society Reception

7–8/10

What Landed Well

  • The "you are here" welcome track concept — directly matched what Katelyn had already been asking for: a clear, simple, step-by-step view of where students are in their journey.
  • The career clarity track and dream job generation — she connected it immediately to her non-traditional student population and to the Renee story. She saw the career framing as more compelling than standalone leadership development for her students.
  • The AI coach and SMART goal generation tied to SNTs — she found the idea of the coach pulling goals from SNT transcripts and storing them in an achievements engine compelling and relevant.
  • The Campus Agent concept — her reaction was enthusiastic. She immediately began imagining use cases: routing students to Career Services, facilitating networking conversations, scheduling mentorship calls with NSLS alumni.
  • The advisor dashboard with career readiness score and individual student data — she immediately saw cross-departmental utility (Career Services, Compliance) and asked about data export capability.
  • SNT matching with availability-based and affinity-based parameters — she appreciated that students using the national events team could still match with students across the country on relevant criteria.
  • Custom track capability (future) — the mention of building custom tracks aligned with her existing mentorship program development.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • Platform separation — she was initially unsure whether Society was a separate registration or an add-on to existing NSLS membership. Required clarification before she felt comfortable. Resolved by Chris's explanation of the unification roadmap.
  • AI scope and limits — she asked whether the AI could recommend book purchase links, route to bookstore inventory, or facilitate networking conversations. Chris clarified current limitations (no live URLs, relatively generic responses at MVP stage). Katelyn accepted this but flagged URL safety as a genuine concern for her student population.
  • Overlap with existing career tools — Katelyn noted that CSU's Career Services already uses Forage and Big Interview, which have overlapping personality/career assessment features. She was processing where Society fits versus those tools, particularly since Society is currently scoped to post-invitation members while Forage is used pre- and post-enrollment.
  • FERPA boundaries on advisor data access — she did not raise this as a concern per se, but Chris proactively noted FERPA parameters around what advisors can view. Worth confirming her understanding of consent flows.

Surprises

  • Katelyn was already actively developing a CSU-internal mentorship program featuring NSLS inducted members — something Linda had apparently not surfaced in the pre-meeting prep. This creates a natural integration point with Society's Connections and alumni mentorship features.
  • She raised the compliance/outcomes reporting use case entirely on her own — connecting Society's career readiness data to an institutional obligation she had just discussed with CSU's compliance director. This was not a prompted response; it reflects a sophisticated understanding of how the platform could serve institutional needs beyond student engagement.
  • Her enthusiasm score was self-qualified: she rated it 7–8 (not lower, not higher) specifically because her Career Services brain and Society brain were "doing this with one another" — she wanted to explore overlap and differentiation before committing fully. This is a thoughtful, not resistant, response.
