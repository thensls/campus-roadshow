Section 3 — Discovery Findings

What They Want to Preserve

  • Success Networking Teams (SNTs): Peer-to-peer connection was cited across advisors as a core differentiator. The vulnerability and mutual support dynamic of SNTs was specifically valued — students engage more openly with peers than with advisors alone.▶ 13:24
  • The sense of accomplishment and belonging NSLS creates — advisors want students to see membership as a lifelong opportunity and a meaningful stepping stone, not just a one-time credential.
  • The scholarship and financial incentive attached to NSLS membership, cited as an effective recruitment tool.
  • The invitation/GPA threshold model (at least by some advisors): The minimum GPA requirement was defended as a motivator for academic performance and a signal of earned access, though there is tension around this across the group.

What Isn't Working

  • SNT drop-off: Approximately 50% of NSLS fall-off was acknowledged as occurring around the SNT formation and attendance stage▶ 41:16 — getting students matched and then keeping them meeting consistently is a persistent failure point.
  • Student data is siloed: Morgan highlighted that NSLS engagement activity (e.g., LinkedIn creation events, program participation) does not automatically populate the Alamo Experience platform▶ 46:25, meaning student leaders lose credit for involvement on their institutional experience transcripts.
  • Advisor bandwidth: Multiple advisors noted that NSLS requires significant time they don't fully have. Having an AI coach as an "assistant" was welcomed specifically because advisors feel stretched.
  • Program accessibility and student-centeredness: At NVC specifically (not Palo Alto), new leadership is questioning whether NSLS is sufficiently student-led and open to all students, raising structural tension around induction format and GPA requirements.
  • Long-form video content: Implicit in the reaction to Society's modularity — the existing hour-and-a-half videos are not well-suited to the way advisors run in-person events or how students engage today.

School Priorities

  • Student engagement and persistence: Across Alamo campuses, institutional leadership is focused on getting students to connect outside the classroom as a driver of retention and academic success.
  • Experience transcript documentation: Palo Alto (via Morgan) is actively working to ensure co-curricular participation is captured in Alamo Experience, tying into district-wide workforce readiness goals.
  • Career readiness infrastructure: Morgan identified the career team as a natural partner for Society and suggested they should be brought into the pilot — signaling that career outcomes are a priority framing at Palo Alto.
  • Cross-campus connectivity: Students in the Alamo system take classes at multiple campuses, making cross-campus SNT matching a relevant and valued capability for the future.
  • Workforce readiness demonstration: Consistent with NSLS's broader framing, there is institutional pressure to show that student programs produce demonstrable career and workforce outcomes, not just leadership development in the abstract.

Section 4 — Key Themes & Signals

1. Data Portability Is a Real Barrier to Institutional Value

Morgan's most substantive contribution was identifying that NSLS engagement data does not flow into Alamo Experience (the district's co-curricular tracking platform, formerly Campus Labs). This means students don't get institutional credit for their NSLS activity on their experience transcripts, and advisors can't use NSLS participation data to tell a story to their own administration. Morgan explicitly connected this gap to Society's new career readiness features, asking whether the career team should be tracking this data too. Integration with Alamo Experience / Engkura Engage is a concrete, named ask from this campus.▶ 46:25

2. Career Services Is an Untapped Institutional Stakeholder

Morgan proactively raised the idea of pulling in Palo Alto's career team▶ 47:00, noting that Society's career-readiness features (LinkedIn creation events, career clarity track, career readiness scores) overlap directly with what career navigators are responsible for. This is not just a data-sharing suggestion — it reflects an institutional readiness to position Society as a shared tool across student affairs and career services, not just an NSLS chapter add-on. This is a strong signal for a broader departmental footprint at Palo Alto.

3. SNT Drop-Off Is the Acknowledged Failure Point — and the Most Resonant Fix

The automated SNT matching feature generated the most immediate and enthusiastic reaction in the meeting.▶ 41:16 The 50% drop-off rate around SNT formation was validated by multiple advisors, and the idea of students matching availability, chatting, meeting, and having transcripts saved in one platform was described as "exactly right" by Miranda (NVC). Morgan seconded the enthusiasm, rating Society a 10/10 immediately after this feature was shown.▶ 56:25 Reducing SNT friction is the clearest near-term product-market fit signal from this group.

4. AI Coach as Advisor Relief, Not Replacement

Advisors across the group — including Morgan implicitly through her interest in involving career staff — are time-constrained. The framing of the AI coach as an "advisor in the student's pocket" resonated because it addresses a real workload problem. The school-specific AI agent concept (trained on course catalogs, staff directories, campus resources) was received as particularly practical. Advisors want to spend their limited time on uniquely human interactions, and they welcome a system that handles routine student questions and accountability nudges.

5. Cross-Campus SNT Expansion Is a Named Future Priority for Alamo

Morgan asked directly whether SNTs would be scoped to Palo Alto's chapter only or expanded to the broader Alamo Colleges system.▶ 39:46 This question was affirmed by another advisor who noted that Alamo students already take classes across multiple campuses and naturally form cross-campus peer relationships. The roadmap commitment to expand SNT matching across campuses — and eventually nationally — was welcomed. This feature has specific structural relevance for multi-campus district systems like Alamo Colleges.

Section 5 — Society Reception

10/10

What Landed Well

  • Automated SNT matching: Described as "exactly right" in solving the known drop-off problem. Morgan rated Society a 10/10 immediately following the SNT matching demo, with an endorsement that the group collectively agreed.
  • Career Clarity Track: The AI-powered career direction experience — especially the dream job recommendations, career statement, and SMART goal-setting — was positioned as filling a genuine gap. Morgan connected this directly to the career team's work and asked whether career navigators should be involved.
  • Career Readiness Score on the advisor dashboard: The concept of a student-level career readiness roll-up score visible to advisors resonated with the career services framing Morgan raised.
  • Modular video content: The shift away from long-form videos toward shorter, selectable clips was well-received in the context of in-person event facilitation.
  • AI coach as an "advisor in the student's pocket": The concept of asynchronous, personalized support reducing advisor workload was embraced across the group, including the school-specific agent concept.
  • Pilot partner model: The offer of free access, dedicated onboarding, and direct roadmap input was received positively. Morgan expressed clear readiness to participate.

What Raised Questions or Friction

  • SNT scope — Palo Alto chapter only vs. Alamo-wide: Morgan asked this question directly and it was partially answered, with confirmation that cross-campus expansion is on the roadmap but not yet available.
  • Induction gate for Society access: The requirement that students complete induction before unlocking the Clarity Track prompted a clarifying question about the exact sequencing and whether the in-person induction events would use Society components simultaneously.
  • Whether pilot decisions are made individually per campus or collectively as a district: Multiple advisors raised this — the answer (individual chapter decisions) was confirmed, but it introduces coordination complexity for follow-up.
  • Data integration with Alamo Experience: Morgan named this as an open need. No firm commitment or timeline was given beyond "we're looking at systems to integrate with."
  • Whether career services staff can participate in the pilot now or only after career features are further built out: Chris indicated they'd be welcome once more career readiness features are ready, but couldn't confirm a timeline.

Surprises

  • Morgan's immediate identification of Alamo Experience (Engkura Engage) as a named integration target — and her framing of the career team as a co-stakeholder — was notably proactive. Most advisors in this session engaged with Society as an NSLS-internal tool; Morgan was the only one who immediately placed it in the broader institutional data and career services ecosystem.
  • Morgan had already been in conversation with Maryanna the prior day about NSLS event data and experience transcripts, meaning she arrived at the meeting with a pre-formed integration question — signaling high prior engagement and a strong institutional use case already in development.
